# Come Follow Me artwork choices

The existing portrait stays in the app until the owner selects a replacement. All choices below
are existing paintings, never generated images of the Savior. Wikimedia Commons identifies each
as public domain, under its Creative Commons Public Domain Mark. This is a public-domain designation,
not a CC BY or CC BY-SA license.

1. **Figure of Christ — Heinrich Hofmann, 1884 (current).** Direct portrait; existing crop and provenance
   are documented in [browse-art-provenance.md](browse-art-provenance.md).
   [Source and reuse evidence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Jesus_Christ_-_Hofmann.jpg).
2. **Christ in Gethsemane — Heinrich Hofmann, 1886.** A quiet prayer scene, with Christ kneeling.
   [Source and reuse evidence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Christ_in_Gethsemane.jpg).
3. **The Sermon on the Mount — Carl Bloch, 1877.** A teaching scene; a crop around Christ can work well
   on a small study card. [Source and reuse evidence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bloch-SermonOnTheMount.jpg).
4. **Good Shepherd — Bernhard Plockhorst, nineteenth century.** A pastoral scene with a lamb and flock.
   [Source and reuse evidence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bernhard_Plockhorst_-_Good_Shephard.jpg).
5. **Christ in Gethsemane — Carl Bloch, 1880.** Christ supported by an angel; a more intimate composition.
   [Source and reuse evidence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:'Christ_in_Gethsemane'_by_Carl_Heinrich_Bloch,_1880.jpg).

Hofmann died in 1911, Bloch in 1890, and Plockhorst in 1907. The cited pages document expired copyright
and the status of the reproductions. The option thumbnails were downloaded and mechanically resized
for comparison only; no generative editing or retouching was used. Source artwork has not been added
to the production app beyond its already-existing current portrait.
