# There Is No Other God That Can Deliver

This guide covers the *Come, Follow Me* reading for **November 9–15, 2026**: Daniel 1–7 ([official lesson](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-old-testament-2026/46?lang=eng)). It is a personal Latter-day Saint study guide. It is not an official publication of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. Scripture quotations come from the King James Version and the other standard works of the Church. The guide goes through all 224 assigned verses in order, in small groups. Each group gets an explanation, cross-references, labeled Christ connections where they fit, and a question for personal study or discussion.

## Historical and Literary Context

**Young captives in a royal school.** The book opens with a siege: "In the third year of the reign of Jehoiakim king of Judah came Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon unto Jerusalem, and besieged it" ([Daniel 1:1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/1?lang=eng&id=p1#p1)). Kings and Chronicles describe how Jehoiakim became Nebuchadnezzar's servant and how vessels from the temple were carried to Babylon ([2 Kings 24:1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/2-kgs/24?lang=eng&id=p1#p1); [2 Chronicles 36:6–7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/2-chr/36?lang=eng&id=p6-p7#p6)). A century earlier Isaiah had warned King Hezekiah that some of his descendants would become "eunuchs in the palace of the king of Babylon" ([Isaiah 39:6–7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/39?lang=eng&id=p6-p7#p6)). Daniel and his friends were young men "of the king's seed, and of the princes" taken for exactly that kind of service ([Daniel 1:3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/1?lang=eng&id=p3#p3)).

**Dates and their limits.** Daniel 1:1 places the siege in Jehoiakim's third year. Jeremiah calls the fourth year of Jehoiakim "the first year of Nebuchadrezzar" ([Jeremiah 25:1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/25?lang=eng&id=p1#p1)). Ancient scribes did not all count a king's first partial year the same way, which may account for the one-year difference. *Scripture Helps* notes that the biblical date points to about 606 B.C., while scholars generally date Nebuchadnezzar's capture of Jerusalem to 597 B.C. ([Scripture Helps: Daniel 1–7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/scripture-helps-old-testament/44-daniel-1-7?lang=eng)). The 597 deportation is the one that took King Jehoiachin and thousands of Judah's leaders and craftsmen ([2 Kings 24:10–16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/2-kgs/24?lang=eng&id=p10-p16#p10)), and the temple was burned about ten years later ([2 Kings 25:8–9](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/2-kgs/25?lang=eng&id=p8-p9#p8)). Daniel 1 describes an earlier, smaller removal of vessels and young nobles. The safest summary is that the story begins in the first years of Nebuchadnezzar's reign, which the Bible Dictionary dates 604–561 B.C. ([Bible Dictionary, "Nebuchadnezzar"](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bd/nebuchadnezzar?lang=eng)).

**One life across an empire's whole span.** Daniel served from Nebuchadnezzar's early years "even unto the first year of king Cyrus" ([Daniel 1:21](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/1?lang=eng&id=p21#p21)). Cyrus conquered Babylon in 539 B.C. (*Scripture Helps*), so Daniel watched the Babylonian empire rise to its height and then fall. Three rulers in these chapters need a brief note:

- **Nebuchadnezzar** is the king of chapters 1–4. He died about two decades before Babylon fell.
- **Belshazzar** is the king of chapter 5. The Bible Dictionary explains that ancient records show he was the son of Nabonidus, Babylon's last king, and that he ruled as prince-regent while his father lived; he was never sole king ([Bible Dictionary, "Belshazzar"](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bd/belshazzar?lang=eng); compare [Bible Dictionary, "Cyrus"](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bd/cyrus?lang=eng)). Daniel 5 calls Nebuchadnezzar his "father." Biblical language often uses "father" for an ancestor or royal predecessor, though the chapter does not explain the relationship.
- **Darius the Mede** receives the kingdom in [Daniel 5:31](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/5?lang=eng&id=p31#p31) and rules in chapter 6. The Bible Dictionary says plainly that he cannot be identified with any king of Babylon known from other historical sources ([Bible Dictionary, "Darius"](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bd/darius?lang=eng)). Several identifications have been proposed; none is settled.

**The chapters are not in time order.** Chapter 7 is dated "in the first year of Belshazzar" ([Daniel 7:1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/7?lang=eng&id=p1#p1)). That is earlier than Belshazzar's last feast in chapter 5 and earlier than Darius in chapter 6. The book is arranged by kind rather than by date: chapters 1–6 tell stories about Daniel and his companions, and chapters 7–12 report Daniel's visions in his own voice ([Bible Dictionary, "Daniel, book of"](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bd/daniel-book-of?lang=eng)). So the vision of beasts and of "one like the Son of man" had already been given to Daniel years before he read the writing on Belshazzar's wall.

**Two languages.** Daniel 1:1–2:4 and chapters 8–12 are in Hebrew. From the middle of 2:4 through the end of chapter 7 the book is in Aramaic, the international language of the age ([Bible Dictionary, "Aramaic"](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bd/aramaic?lang=eng)). The King James translators marked the switch with the word "Syriack" ([Daniel 2:4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/2?lang=eng&id=p4#p4)). This year's assignment ends exactly where the Aramaic section ends. Read as a block, the Aramaic chapters fall into matching pairs:

- **Chapters 2 and 7:** four kingdoms, then a kingdom from God that never ends.
- **Chapters 3 and 6:** faithful servants sentenced to death for their worship, and delivered.
- **Chapters 4 and 5:** a proud king, a message from heaven that only Daniel can read, and a humbling. One king learns from it; the other does not.

The pairing helps the chapters interpret one another. It also shows that the whole section is about one question: whose kingdom lasts?

**What kind of writing this is.** The Bible Dictionary calls Daniel the earliest example of apocalyptic literature in the Bible and compares it with John's Revelation. It also notes that Moses 7 and Nephi's visions in 1 Nephi 8 and 11 are earlier examples of the same kind of revelation. Apocalyptic writing uses symbols such as beasts, horns, metals, and numbers to show the heavenly reality behind earthly events. Daniel interprets some symbols for us ([Daniel 2:36–45](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/2?lang=eng&id=p36-p45#p36); [7:17–27](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/7?lang=eng&id=p17-p27#p17)) and leaves others open. A careful reader follows the book's own interpretations first and holds the rest loosely.

**Questions of authorship and date.** Latter-day Saint reference works accept the book as Daniel's writing and as scripture. Many academic scholars instead conclude that the book reached its final form in the second century B.C., during the persecution of faithful Jews by the Seleucid king Antiochus IV. That king does matter for the later visions: John Hilton III's class slides for this week connect the "little horn" of Daniel 8 with Antiochus IV and his defiling of the temple ([Hilton, Class 45](https://johnhiltoniii.com/findingchrist/class-45-the-abomination-of-desolation-daniel-1-7/)). This guide reads the text as scripture, notes where interpretations differ, and does not depend on settling those debates.

**Neighbors in the story of exile.** Jeremiah wrote to the captives in Babylon, "seek the peace of the city whither I have caused you to be carried away captives, and pray unto the Lord for it" ([Jeremiah 29:7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/29?lang=eng&id=p7#p7)). Ezekiel, prophesying among the exiles, twice names a righteous and wise Daniel ([Ezekiel 14:14](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/14?lang=eng&id=p14#p14); [28:3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/28?lang=eng&id=p3#p3)). Lehi's family left Jerusalem in these same years ([1 Nephi 1:4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/1-ne/1?lang=eng&id=p4#p4)). Later in his own book Daniel is seen studying Jeremiah's prophecy of seventy years ([Daniel 9:2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/9?lang=eng&id=p2#p2)). The Bible Dictionary also notes the likeness between Daniel and Joseph in Egypt ([Bible Dictionary, "Daniel"](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bd/daniel?lang=eng)): both were young captives who served foreign kings, interpreted dreams, credited God, and rose to high office.

**Threads worth tracking through the week:**

- **"Deliver."** "Who is that God that shall deliver you out of my hands?" asks Nebuchadnezzar ([3:15](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/3?lang=eng&id=p15#p15)). The book answers with "There is no other God that can deliver after this sort" ([3:29](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/3?lang=eng&id=p29#p29), the source of this week's lesson title) and "He delivereth and rescueth" ([6:27](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/6?lang=eng&id=p27#p27)).
- **A kingdom that "shall not be destroyed."** The phrase or its equivalent returns in 2:44; 4:3, 34; 6:26; and 7:14, 27, spoken by a prophet, a Babylonian king, a Median king, and a heavenly interpreter.
- **"People, nations, and languages."** Kings use this formula when they command the whole world (3:4; 4:1; 6:25). In 7:14 the same phrase describes those who will serve the Son of Man.
- **"The most High ruleth in the kingdom of men."** This is the lesson Nebuchadnezzar must learn (4:17, 25, 32) and Belshazzar refuses to learn (5:21–22).
- **Power that "was given."** Again and again kingdoms, dominion, and judgment are *given* (2:37–38; 7:6, 12, 14, 22, 27). Behind every throne is the Giver.
- **Things "changed."** Faces change with rage or fear (3:19; 5:6; 7:28). Royal decrees claim they cannot be changed (6:8, 15). But only God "changeth the times and the seasons" (2:21), and the arrogant horn of chapter 7 merely "think[s] to change times and laws" (7:25).

**Reading in layers.** This guide tries to keep four kinds of statement apart: what the ancient text says to its first audience; what official Latter-day Saint sources teach about it; what individual scholars or teachers propose; and devotional applications, including readings that see Jesus Christ in the passage. These layers can support each other, but they are not the same thing, and the guide labels them as it goes.

## Significant Insights

1. **Faithfulness is usually decided before the crisis.** "Daniel purposed in his heart" ([1:8](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/1?lang=eng&id=p8#p8)) long before anyone threatened him with lions. The friends who refused the golden image had already refused the king's table. Small settled choices made in ordinary days become the strength people draw on in extraordinary ones.

2. **Living in Babylon without belonging to Babylon takes discernment.** Daniel and his friends accepted foreign names, a foreign education, and service to a foreign king. They worked hard for the peace of the city. They drew firm lines at worship, at covenant faithfulness, and at prayer. The book does not teach withdrawal from the world or surrender to it. It teaches knowing which things can bend and which cannot.

3. **Faithful refusal can be courteous and creative.** Daniel asked permission, proposed a ten-day test, and protected the official who feared for his head ([1:8–14](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/1?lang=eng&id=p8-p14#p8)). He greeted kings with "O king, live for ever" even after a night among lions ([6:21](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/6?lang=eng&id=p21#p21)). Conviction in Daniel is never contempt.

4. **Revelation comes to prepared people, is sought together, and is credited to God.** Facing death, Daniel asked for time, gathered his companions to pray, received the answer, and *praised God before he did anything else* ([2:16–23](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/2?lang=eng&id=p16-p23#p16)). Then he told the king that the secret was not revealed "for any wisdom that I have" ([2:30](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/2?lang=eng&id=p30#p30)).

5. **"But if not" is mature faith.** Shadrach, Meshach, and Abed-nego trusted that God was able to deliver them and committed themselves before they knew whether he would ([3:17–18](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/3?lang=eng&id=p17-p18#p17)). The book reports miraculous deliverances, but this confession keeps faith from becoming a bargain. Other scriptures, such as Alma 14, show that faithful people are not always rescued from death.

6. **Pride is the refusal to remember who rules.** Chapters 4 and 5 are paired portraits. Nebuchadnezzar boasted, was humbled, "lifted up [his] eyes unto heaven," and was restored ([4:34](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/4?lang=eng&id=p34#p34)). Belshazzar knew the whole story and still did not humble his heart ([5:22](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/5?lang=eng&id=p22#p22)). The difference between them was not information but response.

7. **Human empires look like beasts; God's kingdom comes through a Son of Man.** The kingdoms of chapter 7 rise out of a churning sea as predators. The everlasting dominion is given to "one like the Son of man" ([7:13–14](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/7?lang=eng&id=p13-p14#p13)), a humane figure who comes with the clouds of heaven. Jesus took this title for himself and drew on this vision at his trial ([Mark 14:61–62](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/mark/14?lang=eng&id=p61-p62#p61)).

8. **God's kingdom grows without human hands and cannot be destroyed.** The stone "cut out without hands" becomes a mountain that fills the earth ([2:34–35](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/2?lang=eng&id=p34-p35#p34)). Latter-day Saints read this as a prophecy of the restored Church as God's latter-day kingdom ([Doctrine and Covenants 65:2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/65?lang=eng&id=p2#p2)). Its final fulfillment is the everlasting reign of Jesus Christ.

9. **Faithful lives teach outsiders, but God's kingdom is not built by force.** Three kings in these chapters confess the God of Israel (2:47; 3:28–29; 4:34–37; 6:25–27). Yet their decrees still threaten death, and one ends with innocent families thrown to lions (6:24). Daniel's integrity changed hearts; royal compulsion did not. The Lord's own dominion, as Restoration scripture teaches, flows "without compulsory means" ([Doctrine and Covenants 121:46](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/121?lang=eng&id=p46#p46)).

## Daniel 1: Purposed in His Heart

Chapter 1 sets up the whole book. It introduces the four young men, the king who controls their food, names, and education, and the quiet decision that shapes everything after. It ends by noting that Daniel outlasted the empire that captured him.

### Daniel 1:1–2 — The Lord Gave Jehoiakim into His Hand

Nebuchadnezzar "came ... unto Jerusalem, and besieged it." Verse 2 then makes a startling theological claim: "And the Lord gave Jehoiakim king of Judah into his hand." From a Babylonian point of view, this was a victory of Babylon's king and gods. Nebuchadnezzar carried "part of the vessels of the house of God" into "the land of Shinar to the house of his god," and put them "into the treasure house of his god." Displaying a conquered people's sacred objects in your own temple was a way of saying your god had won.

The narrator flatly disagrees. Jehoiakim fell because the Lord gave him over, as the prophets had warned (compare [Jeremiah 25:8–11](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/25?lang=eng&id=p8-p11#p8)). "Shinar" is an old name for the region of Babylon, the land where people once built a tower to "make us a name" ([Genesis 11:2–4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/gen/11?lang=eng&id=p2-p4#p2)). The opening hints that Babylon is a place of human self-exaltation, and that God remains in charge even there. The vessels matter later. They will reappear on Belshazzar's banquet tables in [Daniel 5:2–3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/5?lang=eng&id=p2-p3#p2), and their abuse will be the last straw for that king.

**Question:** When events seem to show that God has lost, what helps you look for the deeper claim of verse 2, that the Lord is still the one who gives and allows?

### Daniel 1:3–5 — The King's School

The king told "Ashpenaz the master of his eunuchs" to select young men "of the king's seed, and of the princes." The Church's footnote gives "chief of his officers" as an alternative for the title. Hebrew can use the word translated "eunuch" for a court official, so the text does not settle whether these young men were castrated, though Isaiah's earlier warning about Hezekiah's descendants uses the same word ([Isaiah 39:7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/39?lang=eng&id=p7#p7)).

The selection list in verse 4 is a portrait of an elite recruit: "no blemish, but well favoured, and skilful in all wisdom, and cunning in knowledge, and understanding science." They were to learn "the learning and the tongue of the Chaldeans." In Daniel the "Chaldeans" are sometimes a nation and sometimes a learned class of court scholars who studied omens and the stars ([Bible Dictionary, "Chaldea"](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bd/chaldea?lang=eng)). The program lasted three years and included "a daily provision of the king's meat, and of the wine which he drank" (verse 5). "Meat" in the King James Version means food in general; the footnote gives "delicacies."

Notice what the empire was doing. It took the best youth of a conquered people and gave them a new language, a new curriculum, and a seat at the king's table. The goal was not cruelty but absorption. Babylon wanted their gifts, not their destruction, and it wanted those gifts to serve Babylon.

**Question:** What "daily provision" does the world around you offer that can slowly train your loyalties without any open threat at all?

### Daniel 1:6–7 — New Names

Among the recruits were four "of the children of Judah, Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah." Each of their Hebrew names honors Israel's God. Daniel means "God is judge" ([Bible Dictionary, "Daniel"](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bd/daniel?lang=eng)). Hananiah and Azariah contain a short form of the divine name and are usually explained as "the Lord has been gracious" and "the Lord has helped." Mishael is commonly explained as a question, "Who is what God is?"

"The prince of the eunuchs gave names": Belteshazzar, Shadrach, Meshach, and Abed-nego. Nebuchadnezzar later says that Daniel's new name was given "according to the name of my god" ([Daniel 4:8](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/4?lang=eng&id=p8#p8)), and the Bible Dictionary explains Abed-nego as "servant of Nego (or Nebo)," a Babylonian god ([Bible Dictionary, "Abed-nego"](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bd/abed-nego?lang=eng)). The meanings of Shadrach and Meshach are less certain. Renaming someone is an act of ownership. Pharaoh likewise renamed Joseph ([Genesis 41:45](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/gen/41?lang=eng&id=p45#p45)).

The young men did not fight over their names. The text uses both sets of names from here on, and later Daniel himself is introduced to kings as "Daniel, whose name was Belteshazzar" (2:26; 4:19). Their faithfulness would be measured elsewhere.

**Question:** What part of your identity does the world try to rename? How do you keep your true name, your covenant identity, when others call you something else?

### Daniel 1:8 — "Daniel Purposed in His Heart"

"But Daniel purposed in his heart that he would not defile himself with the portion of the king's meat, nor with the wine which he drank." The decision was inward first ("in his heart") and only then spoken: "therefore he requested of the prince of the eunuchs that he might not defile himself."

Why the food? The text does not say. *Scripture Helps* gives two possibilities: the king's food and wine may have been offered first to Babylonian gods, so that eating it would mean taking part in idol worship; or the food may have broken the dietary laws of Moses. Both concerns have support elsewhere in scripture. The law warned Israel not to "eat of his sacrifice" when neighbors sacrificed to their gods ([Exodus 34:15](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ex/34?lang=eng&id=p15#p15)), and the early Christian church asked Gentile converts to "abstain from meats offered to idols" ([Acts 15:29](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/acts/15?lang=eng&id=p29#p29)). Hosea had foreseen exiles who would "eat unclean things in Assyria" ([Hosea 9:3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/hosea/9?lang=eng&id=p3#p3)). Both reasons are possible; neither is certain.

Daniel's choice was not a lifelong rule against meat and wine. Much later, he mentions that during a season of mourning he ate "no pleasant bread, neither came flesh nor wine in my mouth" for three weeks ([Daniel 10:2–3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/10?lang=eng&id=p2-p3#p2)), which implies that by then such food was ordinary for him. In chapter 1 the issue was defilement in that setting, not diet in general. John Hilton III has suggested studying Daniel by looking for short, powerful phrases like this one, which a student can remember and explain ([Hilton, "Teaching the Book of Daniel"](https://johnhiltoniii.com/teaching-the-book-of-daniel/)). "Daniel purposed in his heart" is perhaps the best example in the book.

**Question:** What have you already purposed in your heart so firmly that you no longer need to decide it again each time it comes up? What would you like to add to that list?

### Daniel 1:9–10 — Favour, and an Official's Fear

"Now God had brought Daniel into favour and tender love with the prince of the eunuchs." The footnote gives "compassion" for "tender love." God was at work in a relationship before he worked in the outcome. Compare [Proverbs 16:7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/16?lang=eng&id=p7#p7): "When a man's ways please the Lord, he maketh even his enemies to be at peace with him." The same pattern appears with Joseph, to whom the Lord gave "favour in the sight of the keeper of the prison" ([Genesis 39:21](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/gen/39?lang=eng&id=p21#p21)).

Yet favor did not remove the official's fear. "I fear my lord the king," he said. If the young men looked "worse liking" (the footnote explains this as "less healthy") than the others, "then shall ye make me endanger my head to the king." His concern was real. A refusal that costs only yourself is one thing; a refusal that puts someone else at risk is another, and Daniel took that seriously.

**Question:** When your convictions might cost someone else, as here they might have cost the official, how can you honor both your commitment and your concern for that person?

### Daniel 1:11–14 — "Prove Thy Servants, I Beseech Thee, Ten Days"

Daniel went next to "Melzar, whom the prince of the eunuchs had set over" the four. The footnote suggests that "Melzar" may be a title, "the steward," rather than a personal name. Daniel proposed a test: "Prove thy servants, I beseech thee, ten days; and let them give us pulse to eat, and water to drink." "Pulse" means food grown from seeds, such as legumes and grains ([Bible Dictionary, "Pulse"](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bd/pulse?lang=eng)).

Look at the shape of the request. Daniel did not threaten, demand, or accuse. He used "I beseech thee." He proposed a short, measurable trial and put the decision back in the steward's hands: "as thou seest, deal with thy servants" (verse 13). He found a way for the steward to say yes without being disloyal to the king. "So he consented to them in this matter, and proved them ten days" (verse 14).

Daniel's request combines firmness with humility. Faith here works through ordinary channels: courtesy, wisdom, and a reasonable proposal.

**Question:** Think of a standard you hold that others may not share. How could you explain or ask for it in a way that respects the people around you and gives them a way to help?

### Daniel 1:15–16 — "Fairer and Fatter in Flesh"

"At the end of ten days their countenances appeared fairer and fatter in flesh than all the children which did eat the portion of the king's meat." In an age of scarcity, being "fatter in flesh" meant being healthy and well fed. "Thus Melzar took away the portion of their meat, and the wine that they should drink; and gave them pulse" (verse 16). What began as a ten-day test became the arrangement for the whole three years.

The point of the story is not a modern nutrition claim. The text presents the result as God's blessing on faithful young men who honored him (compare [1 Samuel 2:30](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/1-sam/2?lang=eng&id=p30#p30): "them that honour me I will honour"). The official lesson's ideas for children pair Daniel 1:15–17 with the Word of Wisdom's promises in [Doctrine and Covenants 89:18–21](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/89?lang=eng&id=p18-p21#p18) ([official lesson](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-old-testament-2026/46?lang=eng)). That is a fitting application: the Lord blesses those who keep the health law he has given them. It is an application, not a claim that Daniel kept the Word of Wisdom as it was later revealed.

**Question:** Where have you seen a small act of obedience lead to a blessing larger than its natural results would explain?

### Daniel 1:17 — "God Gave Them Knowledge"

"As for these four children, God gave them knowledge and skill in all learning and wisdom: and Daniel had understanding in all visions and dreams." Both kinds of gift came from God. The four studied Babylon's curriculum and mastered it, and the text credits God for that learning too. Daniel received an additional gift for revelation, which the rest of the book will put to the test.

The verse joins study and spiritual gifts without opposing them. The Church's footnotes point to [Jacob 4:8](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/jacob/4?lang=eng&id=p8#p8) and [Alma 12:9](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/12?lang=eng&id=p9#p9), which teach that the mysteries of God are known by revelation, and to the promise of "wisdom and great treasures of knowledge" in [Doctrine and Covenants 89:19](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/89?lang=eng&id=p19#p19). The gift named here, "understanding in all visions and dreams," is exactly what chapter 2 will require. The preparation was quiet, and it came before the need.

**Question:** What learning, in school, work, or scripture study, might God be preparing you with now for something you cannot yet see?

### Daniel 1:18–20 — "Ten Times Better"

At the end of the three years the young men stood before Nebuchadnezzar. "The king communed with them; and among them all was found none like Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah: therefore stood they before the king." Notice that in the narrator's voice they are still called by their Hebrew names. "In all matters of wisdom and understanding ... he found them ten times better than all the magicians and astrologers that were in all his realm" (verse 20).

"Ten times better" answers the "ten days" test. A small act of trust in verse 12 grows into a tenfold result. It also sets up the contest that drives chapters 2, 4, and 5: Babylon's professional wise men against the servants of the God of heaven. Like Joseph, who told Pharaoh "It is not in me: God shall give Pharaoh an answer" ([Genesis 41:16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/gen/41?lang=eng&id=p16#p16)), these young men excel because of what they have received, not merely what they have learned.

**Question:** The four served the king with excellence while refusing his table. How can excellent work in a secular setting be part of your discipleship?

### Daniel 1:21 — "Unto the First Year of King Cyrus"

"And Daniel continued even unto the first year of king Cyrus." This short verse leaps across more than sixty years. Daniel outlasted Nebuchadnezzar, the whole Babylonian dynasty, and the empire itself. The verse marks his service through the change of empires; it does not mark his death, because [Daniel 10:1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/10?lang=eng&id=p1#p1) records a vision in Cyrus's third year.

The first year of Cyrus also had special meaning for the exiles. In that year Cyrus issued the decree that let Jews return and rebuild the temple ([Ezra 1:1–2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezra/1?lang=eng&id=p1-p2#p1)), fulfilling Jeremiah's word about seventy years ([Jeremiah 29:10](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/29?lang=eng&id=p10#p10)). The young captive taken when the first temple vessels went to Babylon lived to see the decree that would send them home.

*Christ connection (devotional reading):* Jesus prayed for his disciples, "I pray not that thou shouldest take them out of the world, but that thou shouldest keep them from the evil" ([John 17:15](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/john/17?lang=eng&id=p15#p15)). Daniel 1 is a portrait of that prayer answered: four young people kept in a foreign court, not removed from it.

**Question:** Daniel stayed faithful for more than sixty years, not just for one dramatic week. What helps you sustain faithfulness over the long middle of life?

## Daniel 2: A Stone Cut Out Without Hands

Chapter 2 moves from a private test of faith to a public crisis. A troubled king demands the impossible, the whole guild of wise men faces execution, and Daniel and his friends pray together for mercy. The answer reveals a statue of four metals, a stone made without hands, and a kingdom that will never be destroyed.

### Daniel 2:1–3 — A King Who Could Not Sleep

"In the second year of the reign of Nebuchadnezzar Nebuchadnezzar dreamed dreams, wherewith his spirit was troubled, and his sleep brake from him." The most powerful man in the world could not sleep. He called "the magicians, and the astrologers, and the sorcerers, and the Chaldeans," the full range of Babylon's experts in omens and dreams, and told them, "I have dreamed a dream, and my spirit was troubled to know the dream."

The date raises a small puzzle. Chapter 1 described a three-year training program, yet this happens in Nebuchadnezzar's second year. Different ways of counting a king's first year, or a crisis that came before the training formally ended, could explain it. The text does not resolve the question, and its message does not depend on it.

Pharaoh's troubled dreams in Joseph's day began the same way ([Genesis 41:8](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/gen/41?lang=eng&id=p8#p8)). In both stories, God speaks to a pagan king through a dream that only a servant of the Lord can explain.

**Question:** Nebuchadnezzar's power could not quiet his mind. What does it suggest that God chose to speak to this king through a troubled night?

### Daniel 2:4–6 — "Tell Thy Servants the Dream"

"Then spake the Chaldeans to the king in Syriack, O king, live for ever: tell thy servants the dream, and we will shew the interpretation." Here the book switches from Hebrew to Aramaic (the KJV's "Syriack"), and it stays in Aramaic through chapter 7. "O king, live for ever" was the standard court greeting (compare [Nehemiah 2:3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/neh/2?lang=eng&id=p3#p3)).

The king's reply in verse 5 is difficult. The KJV reads, "The thing is gone from me," which sounds as if he forgot the dream. The Church's footnote explains the underlying word as Persian, meaning "sure with me," and suggests that the king knew his dream and wanted to test the wise men (see verses 8–9). Either way, he demanded both the dream and its meaning, under threat that they would "be cut in pieces, and your houses shall be made a dunghill." He also promised "gifts and rewards and great honour" (verse 6).

The threat is the language of a tyrant. The book reports it, as it reports other royal decrees, without approving it. The same brutal formula will come back in a decree that sounds pious ([Daniel 3:29](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/3?lang=eng&id=p29#p29)).

**Question:** Why might a king who held absolute power still be so anxious about whether his advisers were telling him the truth?

### Daniel 2:7–9 — "Ye Would Gain the Time"

The wise men repeated their request: "Let the king tell his servants the dream." The king accused them of stalling: "I know of certainty that ye would gain the time." He believed they had "prepared lying and corrupt words to speak before me, till the time be changed." The footnote explains the last phrase as waiting until circumstances change.

His logic was shrewd. Anyone can invent an interpretation for a dream someone else describes. "Tell me the dream, and I shall know that ye can shew me the interpretation thereof" (verse 9). Only a person with real access to the unseen could pass that test. The king had set a test that only genuine revelation could meet.

**Question:** What makes it easy to recognize a counterfeit when someone claims to speak for God? What makes it hard?

### Daniel 2:10–11 — "Whose Dwelling Is Not with Flesh"

The wise men answered honestly: "There is not a man upon the earth that can shew the king's matter." No king had ever asked such a thing. "There is none other that can shew it before the king, except the gods, whose dwelling is not with flesh."

In one sense they were right. No human technique could reveal another person's dream. But their theology closed the door: the gods, they assumed, live far from humans and do not communicate this way. Daniel's whole ministry will answer that assumption.

*Christ connection (Christian reading):* The wise men's phrase invites a contrast with the Gospel of John: "the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us" ([John 1:14](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/john/1?lang=eng&id=p14#p14)). The God of Israel does not keep his distance from human flesh. He reveals secrets to his servants, and in Jesus Christ he came to live among them.

**Question:** Where do you see people assuming that God is too distant to speak to ordinary human life? How would you answer them from your own experience?

### Daniel 2:12–13 — The Decree Goes Out

"For this cause the king was angry and very furious, and commanded to destroy all the wise men of Babylon." The decree went out, "and they sought Daniel and his fellows to be slain."

Daniel and his friends had not been called to the audience. They were swept up in a collective sentence simply because they belonged to the class of wise men. Their lives were at risk because of a crisis they had no part in creating. This happens often in real life: faithful people suffer from decisions made by others.

**Question:** How do you keep faith when your life is shaken by decisions you had no voice in?

### Daniel 2:14–16 — Counsel, Wisdom, and a Request for Time

"Then Daniel answered with counsel and wisdom to Arioch the captain of the king's guard." Facing an executioner, Daniel did not panic or rage. He asked a calm question: "Why is the decree so hasty from the king?" Arioch explained. "Then Daniel went in, and desired of the king that he would give him time, and that he would shew the king the interpretation" (verse 16).

Notice the contrast with verse 8. The wise men were accused of trying to "gain the time" with nothing to offer. Daniel asked for time openly and promised an answer before he had one. His request was an act of faith that God would reveal what no human could know.

His first step was a reasonable request for time.

**Question:** When you face an urgent problem, what is the difference between stalling and asking for time in faith?

### Daniel 2:17–18 — "Desire Mercies of the God of Heaven"

"Then Daniel went to his house, and made the thing known to Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah, his companions," so "that they would desire mercies of the God of heaven concerning this secret" (verses 17–18). Daniel did not try to carry the crisis alone. He went home, told his friends, and asked them to pray.

The prayer asked for "mercies," not for a reward. The request was simple: that "Daniel and his fellows should not perish with the rest of the wise men of Babylon." These four had studied together, refused the king's table together, and now prayed together. The friendships formed in chapter 1 carried them through chapter 2.

**Question:** Who are the companions you would call on to pray with you in a crisis? Are you that kind of companion for anyone?

### Daniel 2:19–20 — "Then Daniel Blessed the God of Heaven"

"Then was the secret revealed unto Daniel in a night vision. Then Daniel blessed the God of heaven." The answer came at night, the same setting as the king's troubling dream. Before Daniel ran to Arioch, before he saved his own life, he stopped to praise God.

"Blessed be the name of God for ever and ever: for wisdom and might are his" (verse 20). The two qualities the king trusted in, wisdom (his experts) and might (his armies), belong to God. The Church's footnote points to [Amos 3:7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/amos/3?lang=eng&id=p7#p7): "Surely the Lord God will do nothing, but he revealeth his secret unto his servants the prophets."

**Question:** When an answer to prayer comes, what is your first response? What would change if gratitude came first?

### Daniel 2:21–23 — "He Removeth Kings, and Setteth Up Kings"

Daniel's hymn of praise continues: "And he changeth the times and the seasons: he removeth kings, and setteth up kings: he giveth wisdom unto the wise, and knowledge to them that know understanding." The whole message of the coming dream is here before the dream is told. Empires rise and fall at God's word. Compare [Psalm 75:6–7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ps/75?lang=eng&id=p6-p7#p6): "God is the judge: he putteth down one, and setteth up another." Later in the book the arrogant horn of Daniel 7 will "think to change times and laws" ([Daniel 7:25](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/7?lang=eng&id=p25#p25)); only God actually changes the times.

"He revealeth the deep and secret things: he knoweth what is in the darkness, and the light dwelleth with him" (verse 22). Revelation came in the night, but the light lives with God. James calls him "the Father of lights" ([James 1:17](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/james/1?lang=eng&id=p17#p17)).

Verse 23 shifts to personal thanks: "I thank thee, and praise thee, O thou God of my fathers, who hast given me wisdom and might, and hast made known unto me now what we desired of thee: for thou hast now made known unto us the king's matter." Notice "me" and "we" and "us." Daniel received the vision, but he credits his friends' prayers as part of the answer.

**Question:** Daniel praised God's control over history in a moment when his own life was in danger. How does remembering God's larger rule help you face a personal crisis?

### Daniel 2:24–25 — "Destroy Not the Wise Men of Babylon"

Daniel went to Arioch with a striking first sentence: "Destroy not the wise men of Babylon: bring me in before the king, and I will shew unto the king the interpretation." His prayer in verse 18 had asked that he and his friends not perish "with the rest." Now that he had the answer, he pleaded for the rest too. The magicians and astrologers were his professional rivals and part of a pagan system he would not join, but he would not let them die.

Arioch, eager to get credit, "brought in Daniel before the king in haste, and said thus unto him, I have found a man of the captives of Judah, that will make known unto the king the interpretation." Arioch had found no one; Daniel had come to him. The phrase "of the captives of Judah" reminds us of Daniel's low status in Babylon's eyes.

**Question:** Daniel used his opportunity to save even those who did not share his faith. Whose good can you seek this week, beyond those who share your beliefs?

### Daniel 2:26–28 — "There Is a God in Heaven That Revealeth Secrets"

The king asked "Daniel, whose name was Belteshazzar," whether he could do it. Daniel began by agreeing with the wise men: "The secret which the king hath demanded cannot the wise men, the astrologers, the magicians, the soothsayers, shew unto the king." Then came the turn: "But there is a God in heaven that revealeth secrets, and maketh known to the king Nebuchadnezzar what shall be in the latter days" (verse 28).

Daniel put God at the center before saying a word about himself or the dream. The phrase "the latter days" here means the days to come. It looks beyond Nebuchadnezzar's reign to the end of the sequence of kingdoms. Latter-day Saints have long read this chapter as reaching all the way to the latter-day restoration. The Church's footnote compares Joseph's answer to Pharaoh's servants: "Do not interpretations belong to God?" ([Genesis 40:8](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/gen/40?lang=eng&id=p8#p8)).

**Question:** How can you speak of spiritual gifts or answers you have received in a way that points to God rather than to yourself?

### Daniel 2:29–30 — "Not ... for Any Wisdom That I Have"

Daniel told the king that his thoughts "came into thy mind upon thy bed, what should come to pass hereafter: and he that revealeth secrets maketh known to thee what shall come to pass." The king had been lying awake wondering about the future, and God answered that wondering.

Then Daniel disclaimed any credit: "But as for me, this secret is not revealed to me for any wisdom that I have more than any living." The footnote clarifies the purpose clause: it was revealed so that the interpretation might be made known to the king. And it was so "that thou mightest know the thoughts of thy heart." The revelation was aimed at Nebuchadnezzar's own heart. Peter later made the same disclaimer after a miracle: "why look ye so earnestly on us, as though by our own power or holiness we had made this man to walk?" ([Acts 3:12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/acts/3?lang=eng&id=p12#p12)).

**Question:** Daniel says the revelation was given partly so the king might "know the thoughts of [his] heart." How can God's word help you understand your own heart?

### Daniel 2:31–33 — The Great Image

"Thou, O king, sawest, and behold a great image. This great image, whose brightness was excellent, stood before thee; and the form thereof was terrible." The footnote explains "terrible" as "its appearance was frightening." The statue's parts descend in a clear order: "This image's head was of fine gold, his breast and his arms of silver, his belly and his thighs of brass," and then, "His legs of iron, his feet part of iron and part of clay" (verses 32–33).

Two patterns run down the statue. The metals lose value from top to bottom: gold, silver, brass (bronze), iron. They also grow harder until the very bottom, where hard iron is mixed with brittle clay. The whole massive figure stands on a weak base. The English expression "feet of clay," meaning a hidden weakness in someone admired, comes from this verse.

**Question:** What impressive structures in the world, or in your own life, rest on "feet of clay"?

### Daniel 2:34–35 — A Stone That Became a Mountain

"Thou sawest till that a stone was cut out without hands, which smote the image upon his feet that were of iron and clay, and brake them to pieces." The stone struck the weakest point. Then the whole statue, "the iron, the clay, the brass, the silver, and the gold," broke "to pieces together, and became like the chaff of the summer threshingfloors; and the wind carried them away, that no place was found for them."

Chaff is the light husk that blows away when grain is tossed in the air. The psalmist uses the same image: the ungodly "are like the chaff which the wind driveth away" ([Psalm 1:4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ps/1?lang=eng&id=p4#p4)). Empires that seemed permanent vanish without a trace. Then the reversal: "the stone that smote the image became a great mountain, and filled the whole earth." A great statue made with human craft is replaced by a small stone made "without hands," and the stone grows into a mountain. Isaiah's prophecy of "the mountain of the Lord's house" established in the last days ([Isaiah 2:2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/2?lang=eng&id=p2#p2)) uses the same picture of a holy mountain that draws the world.

**Question:** Why might God choose to establish his kingdom through something small rather than something already great?

### Daniel 2:36–38 — "Thou Art This Head of Gold"

"This is the dream; and we will tell the interpretation thereof before the king." Daniel said "we," perhaps including his companions whose prayers shared in the answer, or speaking with modesty. He then addressed the king with surprising honor: "Thou, O king, art a king of kings: for the God of heaven hath given thee a kingdom, power, and strength, and glory."

The key word is "given." Nebuchadnezzar's empire was a gift with a source. Jeremiah said the same thing, even calling him "Nebuchadnezzar the king of Babylon, my servant" ([Jeremiah 27:5–6](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/27?lang=eng&id=p5-p6#p5)). Verse 38 echoes the creation mandate given to humanity: the beasts of the field and the fowls of heaven were placed in his hand. "Thou art this head of gold." This is the only part of the statue that the book identifies by name in the chapter.

**Question:** If everything you have of "power, and strength, and glory" was given, how should that change the way you use it?

### Daniel 2:39–40 — Silver, Brass, and Iron

"And after thee shall arise another kingdom inferior to thee, and another third kingdom of brass, which shall bear rule over all the earth. And the fourth kingdom shall be strong as iron: forasmuch as iron breaketh in pieces and subdueth all things." Daniel names none of these later kingdoms. Elsewhere the book speaks of "the Medes and Persians" ([5:28](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/5?lang=eng&id=p28#p28)) and later names Media, Persia, and Greece in the visions of chapter 8.

Readers have mapped the four kingdoms in more than one way. A Latter-day Saint reading found in Jeffrey R. Chadwick's 1992 *Encyclopedia of Mormonism* article reads the silver as Persia, the brass as the Hellenistic (Greek) kingdoms, the iron legs as Rome (two legs suggesting its later division), and the feet as the European kingdoms that grew out of Rome ([Chadwick, "Daniel, Prophecies of"](https://scripturecentral.org/archive/articles/encyclopedia-entry/daniel-prophecies)). That article is one interpreter's contribution, not an official Church statement. Many other scholars count the kingdoms differently, as discussed under chapter 7. Whatever the mapping, the iron kingdom's character is clear: it "breaketh in pieces and subdueth all things," a description of raw military force.

**Question:** The fourth kingdom is the strongest and the most destructive. Why might great strength without goodness be more frightening than weakness?

### Daniel 2:41–43 — Iron Mixed with Miry Clay

"The kingdom shall be divided; but there shall be in it of the strength of the iron." The feet and toes were "part of potters' clay, and part of iron," so "the kingdom shall be partly strong, and partly broken." Verse 43 adds a cryptic detail: "they shall mingle themselves with the seed of men: but they shall not cleave one to another, even as iron is not mixed with clay."

Interpreters have suggested royal marriages between rival dynasties, or the blending of different peoples and cultures within one realm. The text itself does not specify. Its point is clear enough: this last stage of human power tries to hold together things that do not bond. Iron and clay can be pressed together, but they do not become one material. Unity imposed from outside cannot do what a shared heart does.

**Question:** What does the image of iron and clay teach about trying to build unity without real oneness of heart?

### Daniel 2:44–45 — A Kingdom That Shall Never Be Destroyed

"And in the days of these kings shall the God of heaven set up a kingdom, which shall never be destroyed: and the kingdom shall not be left to other people, but it shall break in pieces and consume all these kingdoms, and it shall stand for ever." Each earlier kingdom passed to another people. This one will not. Its builder is "the God of heaven," which is why the stone was "cut out of the mountain without hands" (verse 45). "The dream is certain, and the interpretation thereof sure."

Latter-day Saints understand this prophecy in a specific way. The official lesson quotes President D. Todd Christofferson's teaching that the Church is this prophesied latter-day kingdom, set up by God and not by man, rolling forth to fill the earth ([official lesson](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-old-testament-2026/46?lang=eng)). The Lord taught Joseph Smith that "the keys of the kingdom of God are committed unto man on the earth," and that the gospel will roll forth "as the stone which is cut out of the mountain without hands shall roll forth, until it has filled the whole earth" ([Doctrine and Covenants 65:2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/65?lang=eng&id=p2#p2)). At the dedication of the Kirtland Temple the Prophet prayed that "the kingdom, which thou hast set up without hands, may become a great mountain and fill the whole earth" ([Doctrine and Covenants 109:72](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/109?lang=eng&id=p72#p72)). Chadwick's article adds that the restored Church was organized in 1830, while Europe's monarchies still ruled.

The image should be read in its own spirit. The stone works "without hands." The kingdom grows by God's power, through covenant and testimony, not by human force or hostility toward nations.

*Christ connection (New Testament reading):* Jesus called himself "the stone which the builders rejected" and warned, "on whomsoever it shall fall, it will grind him to powder" ([Luke 20:17–18](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/luke/20?lang=eng&id=p17-p18#p17)), language that echoes this chapter. The kingdom belongs to him: "of his kingdom there shall be no end" ([Luke 1:33](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/luke/1?lang=eng&id=p33#p33)). The restored Church is the kingdom prepared on earth; its king is Christ.

**Question:** This kingdom "shall not be left to other people." What is one way the "stone" can grow through you this week, without hands and without force?

### Daniel 2:46–47 — "A God of Gods, and a Lord of Kings"

"Then the king Nebuchadnezzar fell upon his face, and worshipped Daniel, and commanded that they should offer an oblation and sweet odours unto him." This is an extraordinary scene: the conqueror prostrate before a captive. The text does not record Daniel's response. The Church's footnote points to [Acts 14:11–15](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/acts/14?lang=eng&id=p11-p15#p11), where Paul and Barnabas tore their clothes and cried, "We also are men of like passions with you," when a crowd tried to offer them sacrifice.

The king's words immediately point beyond Daniel: "Of a truth it is, that your God is a God of gods, and a Lord of kings, and a revealer of secrets, seeing thou couldest reveal this secret" (verse 47). This is real progress, but it is not yet conversion. Nebuchadnezzar still speaks of "your God," and chapter 3 will show how shallow his understanding remains.

**Question:** The king's confession came "seeing thou couldest reveal this secret." What have you seen in another person's life that strengthened your own faith in God?

### Daniel 2:48–49 — Rewards, Office, and Friends Remembered

"Then the king made Daniel a great man, and gave him many great gifts, and made him ruler over the whole province of Babylon, and chief of the governors over all the wise men of Babylon." Like Joseph in Egypt ([Genesis 41:40–41](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/gen/41?lang=eng&id=p40-p41#p40)), the captive became a high official overnight.

Daniel did not forget his companions: "Daniel requested of the king, and he set Shadrach, Meshach, and Abed-nego, over the affairs of the province of Babylon: but Daniel sat in the gate of the king." The footnote explains the last phrase as remaining at the king's court. The friends who prayed with him now shared his responsibilities. Their new prominence will also make them targets in chapter 3, when jealous rivals notice "certain Jews whom thou hast set over the affairs of the province of Babylon" ([3:12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/3?lang=eng&id=p12#p12)).

**Question:** When you receive an opportunity or blessing, whom do you remember and bring along with you?

## Daniel 3: The Golden Image and the Fiery Furnace

Chapter 3 is a story about public pressure. The whole machinery of the empire, its officials, its music, its herald, and its furnace, is set up to make everyone bow at once. Three men stand up. Their answer to the king, "But if not," is one of the clearest statements of faith in scripture.

### Daniel 3:1 — An Image of Gold on the Plain of Dura

"Nebuchadnezzar the king made an image of gold, whose height was threescore cubits, and the breadth thereof six cubits: he set it up in the plain of Dura, in the province of Babylon." Sixty cubits is roughly ninety feet, and six cubits roughly nine feet. Those proportions are very tall and thin, which may mean the figure stood on a pillar or base, though the text does not say. The exact site of "the plain of Dura" is not known.

The chapter does not say what the image represented: the king, one of his gods, or the empire itself. Readers often notice a link with chapter 2. Nebuchadnezzar had been told, "Thou art this head of gold" ([2:38](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/2?lang=eng&id=p38#p38)), and here he builds an image that is gold from top to bottom. If that link is intended, the king answered a vision of passing kingdoms by building a monument that denied any kingdom would pass. This is a reasonable reading, not a statement of the text.

**Question:** What monuments, visible or invisible, do people build to insist that their success will last forever?

### Daniel 3:2–3 — Every Official in the Empire

Nebuchadnezzar summoned "the princes, the governors, and the captains, the judges, the treasurers, the counsellors, the sheriffs, and all the rulers of the provinces" to "the dedication of the image." Verse 3 repeats the whole list, word for word, as they arrive "and they stood before the image that Nebuchadnezzar had set up."

The repetition is deliberate and almost comic. The narrator piles up the titles of the empire's officials the way the empire piled up its authority. Some form of the phrase "that Nebuchadnezzar the king had set up" appears again and again through the chapter (verses 2, 3, 5, 7, 12, 14, 18). The image is the king's project, and the repetition keeps reminding us of that. The God of heaven "setteth up kings" ([2:21](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/2?lang=eng&id=p21#p21)); here a king sets up a god.

**Question:** Why might it matter that the whole government was present at the dedication? How does a crowd make conformity feel normal?

### Daniel 3:4–7 — "At What Time Ye Hear the Sound"

"Then an herald cried aloud, To you it is commanded, O people, nations, and languages." Whenever the music played, everyone was to "fall down and worship the golden image." The instrument list is long: "cornet, flute, harp, sackbut, psaltery, dulcimer, and all kinds of musick." The Church's footnotes explain that "sackbut" translates a word for a triangular stringed instrument and "dulcimer" a word for a bagpipe. The penalty was immediate: "whoso falleth not down and worshippeth shall the same hour be cast into the midst of a burning fiery furnace" (verse 6).

Music was the signal that coordinated the whole crowd. It made obedience automatic: hear the sound, fall down. "Therefore at that time, when all the people heard the sound ... all the people, the nations, and the languages, fell down and worshipped" (verse 7). The narrator says "all." For a moment it looks as if the empire has succeeded completely.

The second of the Ten Commandments addressed this very act: "Thou shalt not bow down thyself to them, nor serve them" ([Exodus 20:5](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ex/20?lang=eng&id=p5#p5)). The test was not about belief in private but about one public gesture.

**Question:** What are the "sounds" in your world that signal everyone to bow at once? How do you notice them before your knees bend?

### Daniel 3:8–12 — "Certain Jews ... Have Not Regarded Thee"

"Wherefore at that time certain Chaldeans came near, and accused the Jews." The Aramaic expression behind "accused" is usually understood as a vivid idiom meaning something like "ate the pieces of," that is, slandered. The same expression returns in [Daniel 6:24](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/6?lang=eng&id=p24#p24).

The accusers began with flattery, "O king, live for ever," and then repeated the decree back to the king. Their charge in verse 12 reveals their motive: "There are certain Jews whom thou hast set over the affairs of the province of Babylon, Shadrach, Meshach, and Abed-nego; these men, O king, have not regarded thee: they serve not thy gods, nor worship the golden image which thou hast set up." They named the friends' promotion first, then framed their worship as personal disrespect to the king. Envy can disguise itself as patriotism. Centuries later Haman would make a similar accusation in Persia: "their laws are diverse from all people; neither keep they the king's laws" ([Esther 3:8](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/esth/3?lang=eng&id=p8#p8)).

The accusation was partly true. The three did not serve the king's gods. But "have not regarded thee" was false. These men had served the king faithfully in every other matter.

**Question:** How can you tell the difference between real disloyalty and faithfulness that others find inconvenient?

### Daniel 3:13–15 — "Who Is That God That Shall Deliver You?"

"Then Nebuchadnezzar in his rage and fury commanded to bring Shadrach, Meshach, and Abed-nego." Even in anger, the king gave them a second chance: "Is it true ...?" If they were ready to bow when the music played, "well." If not, "ye shall be cast the same hour into the midst of a burning fiery furnace."

Then he asked the question at the center of the book: "and who is that God that shall deliver you out of my hands?" (verse 15). He had praised their God as "a God of gods" only a chapter earlier ([2:47](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/2?lang=eng&id=p47#p47)). Now he assumed no god could save anyone from him. The rest of the chapter, and chapter 6, answer his question.

**Question:** What "furnaces" in modern life pressure people to bow? What does it look like to stand when everyone else kneels?

### Daniel 3:16–18 — "But If Not"

"Shadrach, Meshach, and Abed-nego, answered and said to the king, O Nebuchadnezzar, we are not careful to answer thee in this matter." In the King James Version, "careful" means "full of care," that is, anxious. Paul uses it that way: "Be careful for nothing" ([Philippians 4:6](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/philip/4?lang=eng&id=p6#p6)). The three were not being rude. They had no need to defend themselves or plead. The decision was already made.

"If it be so, our God whom we serve is able to deliver us from the burning fiery furnace, and he will deliver us out of thine hand, O king. But if not, be it known unto thee, O king, that we will not serve thy gods, nor worship the golden image which thou hast set up" (verses 17–18).

The confession has two parts. First, God is *able*, and they expect him to deliver them. Second, their obedience does not depend on that deliverance. They committed themselves before they knew the outcome. This is faith that trusts God's power without dictating God's choices. Esther would later say, "if I perish, I perish" ([Esther 4:16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/esth/4?lang=eng&id=p16#p16)). The Church's footnote compares the Hebrew midwives who "feared God, and did not as the king of Egypt commanded them" ([Exodus 1:17](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ex/1?lang=eng&id=p17#p17)).

The official lesson pairs these verses with [Alma 14:8–13](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/14?lang=eng&id=p8-p13#p8) ([official lesson](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-old-testament-2026/46?lang=eng)). There, faithful women and children were burned, and Alma was constrained not to stop it. Alma explained that "the Lord receiveth them up unto himself, in glory" ([Alma 14:11](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/14?lang=eng&id=p11#p11)). The friends in Daniel 3 were delivered; the believers in Ammonihah were not. "But if not" covers both stories. The Book of Mormon prophet Abinadi also died by fire "because he would not deny the commandments of God" ([Mosiah 17:20](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/mosiah/17?lang=eng&id=p20#p20)).

*Christ connection:* In Gethsemane Jesus prayed, "O my Father, if it be possible, let this cup pass from me: nevertheless not as I will, but as thou wilt" ([Matthew 26:39](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/26?lang=eng&id=p39#p39)). Jesus asked for deliverance and also yielded to his Father's will. "But if not" is the disciple's echo of his "nevertheless."

**Question:** Finish the sentence for something you are praying about now: "My God is able to ... but if not, I will still ..."

### Daniel 3:19–21 — Seven Times Hotter

"Then was Nebuchadnezzar full of fury, and the form of his visage was changed against Shadrach, Meshach, and Abed-nego." The footnote explains this as a changed expression of his face. The man who ruled the world could not rule his own face. He ordered the furnace heated "one seven times more than it was wont to be heated," a way of saying as hot as possible. He chose "the most mighty men that were in his army" to bind the three (verse 20).

The details of verse 21 are specific: they were bound "in their coats, their hosen, and their hats, and their other garments." The exact meaning of these Aramaic clothing words is uncertain, but the point is that they went into the fire fully dressed. That detail will matter in verse 27.

**Question:** Rage made the king's response more extreme but no more effective. What does this chapter suggest about decisions made in fury?

### Daniel 3:22–23 — The Flame That Killed the Executioners

"Therefore because the king's commandment was urgent, and the furnace exceeding hot, the flame of the fire slew those men that took up Shadrach, Meshach, and Abed-nego." The king's overheating killed his own strongest soldiers. His fury harmed the people who obeyed him.

"And these three men, Shadrach, Meshach, and Abed-nego, fell down bound into the midst of the burning fiery furnace." The verb "fell down" is the same word used for the crowds who fell down before the image. These three would not fall down in worship, so they fell down into the fire. Verse 23 is the lowest point of the chapter. Faithfulness has not prevented the furnace.

**Question:** Have you ever done the right thing and still found yourself "in the furnace"? What helped you keep faith there?

### Daniel 3:24–25 — Four Men Loose in the Fire

"Then Nebuchadnezzar the king was astonied, and rose up in haste." He questioned his counsellors: "Did not we cast three men bound into the midst of the fire?" "True, O king." Then he said, "Lo, I see four men loose, walking in the midst of the fire, and they have no hurt; and the form of the fourth is like the Son of God."

Several details deserve attention. The men are "loose." The fire burned their bonds but nothing else. They are "walking," not struggling. And there are four.

Who was the fourth? The King James Version capitalizes "Son of God," but the Aramaic phrase is usually translated "a son of the gods," that is, a divine being. A pagan king would naturally describe what he saw in those terms. *Scripture Helps* says the text leaves unclear whether this was Jehovah or an angel. Nebuchadnezzar himself later says God "sent his angel" ([verse 28](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/3?lang=eng&id=p28#p28)). Many Christian readers have seen in the fourth figure a pre-mortal appearance of the Lord; others read it as an angel. The text leaves the identity open. What it makes certain is that God's presence was with them in the fire.

The Church's footnotes point to Psalm 91 and to the three Nephite disciples, who "thrice ... were cast into a furnace and received no harm" ([3 Nephi 28:21](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/3-ne/28?lang=eng&id=p21#p21)). Isaiah's promise fits the scene: "when thou walkest through the fire, thou shalt not be burned; neither shall the flame kindle upon thee" ([Isaiah 43:2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/43?lang=eng&id=p2#p2)).

**Question:** The three were not kept out of the fire, but they were not alone in it. When have you felt the Lord's presence in a trial he did not remove?

### Daniel 3:26–27 — Not Even the Smell of Fire

Nebuchadnezzar came to "the mouth of the burning fiery furnace" and called, "Shadrach, Meshach, and Abed-nego, ye servants of the most high God, come forth, and come hither." The king who had asked "who is that God?" now named him "the most high God." The three "came forth of the midst of the fire."

The same officials who had gathered for the image's dedication now gathered as witnesses. They "saw these men, upon whose bodies the fire had no power, nor was an hair of their head singed, neither were their coats changed, nor the smell of fire had passed on them" (verse 27). Bodies, hair, clothing, and even smell were untouched. The only things the fire had destroyed were their bonds.

The Church's footnotes connect this verse with Hebrews, which describes the faithful who "quenched the violence of fire" ([Hebrews 11:34](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/heb/11?lang=eng&id=p34#p34)). Peter writes of faith "tried with fire" and found "unto praise and honour and glory at the appearing of Jesus Christ" ([1 Peter 1:7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/1-pet/1?lang=eng&id=p7#p7)).

**Question:** What "bonds" might a trial burn away from you while leaving you otherwise unharmed?

### Daniel 3:28 — "Yielded Their Bodies"

Nebuchadnezzar spoke again: "Blessed be the God of Shadrach, Meshach, and Abed-nego, who hath sent his angel, and delivered his servants that trusted in him." He described their faith accurately. They "have changed the king's word" (the footnote explains this as successfully defying the king's decree) "and yielded their bodies, that they might not serve nor worship any god, except their own God."

"Yielded their bodies" is a remarkable phrase from a king. He recognized that they had handed over their lives rather than their worship. Paul asked the Saints to "present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God" ([Romans 12:1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/rom/12?lang=eng&id=p1#p1)). The three offered their bodies to the fire, and God gave them back.

*Christ connection:* The Savior did not only risk his body; he gave it: "This is my body which is given for you" ([Luke 22:19](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/luke/22?lang=eng&id=p19#p19)). The friends were delivered from death. Jesus went through death itself and rose. Their story points toward his, but his goes further.

**Question:** What does it mean for you to yield your body to God in daily life, in what you do, where you go, and what you refuse?

### Daniel 3:29 — "No Other God That Can Deliver After This Sort"

"Therefore I make a decree, That every people, nation, and language, which speak any thing amiss against the God of Shadrach, Meshach, and Abed-nego, shall be cut in pieces, and their houses shall be made a dunghill: because there is no other God that can deliver after this sort."

The last clause gives this week's lesson its title, and it is true. But notice what surrounds it. The king's decree uses the same violent threat he made against his wise men in [Daniel 2:5](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/2?lang=eng&id=p5#p5). He has changed which god he protects, but not his methods. He still rules by terror. The man who threw people into a furnace for refusing to worship his image now threatens to cut people in pieces for speaking against Israel's God.

The book does not ask us to approve of this decree, any more than it approved of the furnace. Its own heroes never forced worship on anyone. Latter-day Saints believe in allowing "all men the same privilege, let them worship how, where, or what they may" ([Articles of Faith 1:11](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/pgp/a-of-f/1?lang=eng&id=p11#p11)). The Lord's own dominion, Restoration scripture teaches, flows "without compulsory means" ([Doctrine and Covenants 121:46](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/121?lang=eng&id=p46#p46)), and righteous influence works "only by persuasion, by long-suffering, by gentleness and meekness, and by love unfeigned" ([Doctrine and Covenants 121:41](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/121?lang=eng&id=p41#p41)). Nebuchadnezzar recognized God's power but had not yet learned God's ways. Chapter 4 will continue his education.

**Question:** Nebuchadnezzar spoke a true sentence inside a cruel decree. How can believers defend the truth about God without using the world's methods of fear and force?

### Daniel 3:30 — Promoted in the Province

"Then the king promoted Shadrach, Meshach, and Abed-nego, in the province of Babylon." The chapter ends quietly. The three return to their work, now with higher positions. This is the last time they are named in the book.

They did not leave Babylon or stop serving its government. They went back to the same offices, among the same colleagues who had accused them, and kept serving. Faithfulness under fire did not turn them into permanent heroes on display. It returned them to ordinary duty.

**Question:** After a spiritual victory, how do you return to ordinary responsibilities with the same faithfulness?

## Daniel 4: The Most High Ruleth in the Kingdom of Men

Chapter 4 is unusual: most of it is told by Nebuchadnezzar himself, as an open letter to the world. It describes a dream of a great tree cut down, a warning Daniel delivers with obvious reluctance, a year of grace, a sudden fall, and a restoration that begins when the king looks up. It is a story of humbling that ends in praise.

### Daniel 4:1–3 — A King's Letter to the World

"Nebuchadnezzar the king, unto all people, nations, and languages, that dwell in all the earth; Peace be multiplied unto you." The opening uses the formula of a royal proclamation. Its greeting, "Peace be multiplied," is the same one Darius will use ([6:25](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/6?lang=eng&id=p25#p25)) and that Peter later uses for the Saints ([1 Peter 1:2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/1-pet/1?lang=eng&id=p2#p2)).

"I thought it good to shew the signs and wonders that the high God hath wrought toward me" (verse 2). The king tells the story because something was done "toward me." Then, before any details, he gives the conclusion: "How great are his signs! and how mighty are his wonders! his kingdom is an everlasting kingdom, and his dominion is from generation to generation" (verse 3). This sounds like a psalm: "Thy kingdom is an everlasting kingdom, and thy dominion endureth throughout all generations" ([Psalm 145:13](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ps/145?lang=eng&id=p13#p13)). The conqueror of Jerusalem is now speaking the language of Israel's worship.

**Question:** Nebuchadnezzar shares the most humbling experience of his life with the whole world. What gives a person the freedom to tell openly how God has corrected them?

### Daniel 4:4–7 — Flourishing, and Afraid

"I Nebuchadnezzar was at rest in mine house, and flourishing in my palace." Everything was going well. "I saw a dream which made me afraid, and the thoughts upon my bed and the visions of my head troubled me" (verse 5). The trouble came not in disaster but in prosperity.

As in chapter 2, he summoned "all the wise men of Babylon." This time he told them the dream: "but they did not make known unto me the interpretation thereof" (verse 7). Even with the dream in hand, Babylon's experts failed. Perhaps they could tell that the dream was bad news for the king and were afraid to say so.

**Question:** Why do you think God often speaks warnings to people when they feel most secure?

### Daniel 4:8–9 — "In Whom Is the Spirit of the Holy Gods"

"But at the last Daniel came in before me, whose name was Belteshazzar, according to the name of my god, and in whom is the spirit of the holy gods." The king's words mix truth and paganism. He still thinks of Daniel by a name honoring his own god, and he describes Daniel's gift in polytheistic terms, "the spirit of the holy gods." Yet he sees something real. Pharaoh had said of Joseph, "Can we find such a one as this is, a man in whom the Spirit of God is?" ([Genesis 41:38](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/gen/41?lang=eng&id=p38#p38)).

"O Belteshazzar, master of the magicians, because I know that the spirit of the holy gods is in thee, and no secret troubleth thee" (verse 9). "Master of the magicians" was Daniel's office ([2:48](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/2?lang=eng&id=p48#p48)); it does not mean Daniel practiced magic. Chapter 1 had already set Daniel and his friends apart from "all the magicians and astrologers."

**Question:** Nebuchadnezzar recognized Daniel's gift before he understood its source. How can others recognize the Spirit in you even if they do not yet share your faith?

### Daniel 4:10–12 — A Tree at the Center of the Earth

"I saw, and behold a tree in the midst of the earth, and the height thereof was great." It grew until "the height thereof reached unto heaven, and the sight thereof to the end of all the earth." Its leaves were fair and its fruit abundant, "and in it was meat for all: the beasts of the field had shadow under it, and the fowls of the heaven dwelt in the boughs thereof, and all flesh was fed of it" (verse 12).

This is an image of generous greatness. The tree is not only tall; it shelters and feeds everyone. Ezekiel used similar language for Assyria, a great cedar in whose boughs "all the fowls of heaven made their nests" ([Ezekiel 31:6](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/31?lang=eng&id=p6#p6)). An empire at its best can provide order, protection, and food for many people. The dream does not deny that.

*Christ connection (New Testament comparison):* Jesus described the kingdom of heaven as a mustard seed, "the least of all seeds," that becomes a tree "so that the birds of the air come and lodge in the branches thereof" ([Matthew 13:31–32](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/13?lang=eng&id=p31-p32#p31)). Nebuchadnezzar's tree began great and was cut down. Christ's kingdom begins small and keeps growing, like the stone of chapter 2.

**Question:** What good does your influence provide to others? What would change if you saw that influence as a stewardship from God rather than your own achievement?

### Daniel 4:13–16 — The Watcher's Decree

"I saw in the visions of my head upon my bed, and, behold, a watcher and an holy one came down from heaven." A "watcher" appears to be an angelic messenger. He "cried aloud": "Hew down the tree, and cut off his branches, shake off his leaves, and scatter his fruit: let the beasts get away from under it, and the fowls from his branches" (verse 14).

But verse 15 offers mercy within judgment: "Nevertheless leave the stump of his roots in the earth, even with a band of iron and brass." The tree would be cut down but not uprooted, and the stump would be bound and protected. Then the imagery shifts from tree to person: "let it be wet with the dew of heaven, and let his portion be with the beasts in the grass of the earth." And verse 16 continues, "Let his heart be changed from man's, and let a beast's heart be given unto him; and let seven times pass over him." The footnote offers "years, or seasons" for "times."

The pronouns change from "it" to "his." The tree is a person, and the person's heart will be changed from human to animal.

**Question:** The decree includes a protected stump. Where in your life has a hard correction from God come with a hidden promise of renewal?

### Daniel 4:17–18 — "The Basest of Men"

"This matter is by the decree of the watchers, and the demand by the word of the holy ones: to the intent that the living may know that the most High ruleth in the kingdom of men, and giveth it to whomsoever he will, and setteth up over it the basest of men." Here is the chapter's thesis, which will be repeated in verses 25 and 32: "the most High ruleth in the kingdom of men."

"The basest of men" sounds like an insult, but the footnote explains it as "humblest." God can raise up the lowly. Hannah sang that he "raiseth up the poor out of the dust ... to set them among princes" ([1 Samuel 2:8](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/1-sam/2?lang=eng&id=p8#p8)), and Mary that "He hath put down the mighty from their seats, and exalted them of low degree" ([Luke 1:52](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/luke/1?lang=eng&id=p52#p52)). Daniel himself was a captive raised to high office.

In verse 18 the king turned to Daniel: "thou art able; for the spirit of the holy gods is in thee." His confidence in Daniel had grown since chapter 2.

**Question:** If God "setteth up over it the basest of men," meaning the humblest, what does that suggest about the qualities he values in leaders, and in you?

### Daniel 4:19 — "Astonied for One Hour"

"Then Daniel, whose name was Belteshazzar, was astonied for one hour, and his thoughts troubled him." Daniel understood immediately, and the meaning shook him. The king noticed and gently urged him: "let not the dream, or the interpretation thereof, trouble thee."

Daniel's reply is one of the most humane moments in the book: "My lord, the dream be to them that hate thee, and the interpretation thereof to thine enemies." This was the king who had destroyed Jerusalem and carried Daniel away as a youth. Daniel did not take pleasure in his coming humiliation. He wished it on someone else. Jesus would teach, "Love your enemies, bless them that curse you, do good to them that hate you" ([Matthew 5:44](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/5?lang=eng&id=p44#p44)).

**Question:** Daniel grieved over a hard message for a man who had harmed his people. How can you deliver hard truths without taking satisfaction in someone else's downfall?

### Daniel 4:20–23 — "It Is Thou, O King"

Daniel repeated the dream back to the king, line by line: the tree that "grew, and was strong," its height, its fair leaves, abundant fruit, and shelter for beasts and birds. Then came the interpretation: "It is thou, O king, that art grown and become strong: for thy greatness is grown, and reacheth unto heaven, and thy dominion to the end of the earth" (verse 22).

Daniel spoke directly, much like the prophet Nathan, who told King David, "Thou art the man" ([2 Samuel 12:7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/2-sam/12?lang=eng&id=p7#p7)). He did not soften the identification. Verse 23 restates the watcher's decree: hew down the tree and destroy it, "yet leave the stump of the roots thereof in the earth ... till seven times pass over him." Both judgment and mercy remain in the interpretation.

**Question:** What makes it possible to receive a direct, personal correction like "It is thou" without becoming defensive?

### Daniel 4:24–26 — "Till Thou Know That the Most High Ruleth"

"This is the interpretation, O king, and this is the decree of the most High, which is come upon my lord the king." Daniel spelled it out: "they shall drive thee from men, and thy dwelling shall be with the beasts of the field, and they shall make thee to eat grass as oxen, and they shall wet thee with the dew of heaven, and seven times shall pass over thee, till thou know that the most High ruleth in the kingdom of men, and giveth it to whomsoever he will" (verse 25).

The goal is knowledge, not destruction. The judgment would last "till thou know." And the stump meant hope: "thy kingdom shall be sure unto thee, after that thou shalt have known that the heavens do rule" (verse 26). "The heavens" here is a reverent way of referring to God. The prodigal son speaks the same way when he says, "Father, I have sinned against heaven" ([Luke 15:18](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/luke/15?lang=eng&id=p18#p18)).

**Question:** God's judgment had a purpose and an end point: "till thou know." How might seeing correction this way change how you experience it?

### Daniel 4:27 — "Break Off Thy Sins by Righteousness"

Daniel added counsel no one had asked for: "Wherefore, O king, let my counsel be acceptable unto thee, and break off thy sins by righteousness, and thine iniquities by shewing mercy to the poor; if it may be a lengthening of thy tranquillity." The footnote explains the last phrase as "so that your prosperity may be long."

This verse is remarkable for what it shows about repentance. Daniel did not ask the king to perform a ritual or to honor Israel's God with an offering. He named concrete change: righteousness, and mercy to the poor. An empire that fed "all flesh" in the dream was to show mercy in fact. The Church's footnotes point to King Benjamin's call to impart to the needy ([Mosiah 4:21](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/mosiah/4?lang=eng&id=p21#p21)) and to the Lord's law of consecration: "inasmuch as ye impart of your substance unto the poor, ye will do it unto me" ([Doctrine and Covenants 42:31](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/42?lang=eng&id=p31#p31)). John Hilton III lists this verse among the short, powerful phrases worth finding in Daniel (["Teaching the Book of Daniel"](https://johnhiltoniii.com/teaching-the-book-of-daniel/)).

Notice the humility of Daniel's promise: "*if* it may be." He did not guarantee that repentance would cancel the decree. He offered hope without presuming to control God.

**Question:** If you were to "break off" one sin "by shewing mercy to the poor," what would it look like this week?

### Daniel 4:28–30 — "Is Not This Great Babylon?"

"All this came upon the king Nebuchadnezzar." The narration switches here from the king's first-person voice to third person, and it stays that way through verse 33. Perhaps a man who had lost his reason could not narrate those days himself.

"At the end of twelve months he walked in the palace of the kingdom of Babylon." God gave a full year of grace. Then, looking over his city, the king said: "Is not this great Babylon, that I have built for the house of the kingdom by the might of my power, and for the honour of my majesty?" (verse 30). Every phrase is about himself: "I have built," "my power," "my majesty." Nebuchadnezzar was in fact a great builder, and his city was impressive. His sin was not building but claiming all the credit.

Jesus told of a rich man who said to his soul, "take thine ease, eat, drink, and be merry," to whom God replied, "Thou fool, this night thy soul shall be required of thee" ([Luke 12:19–20](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/luke/12?lang=eng&id=p19-p20#p19)).

**Question:** What accomplishments are you most tempted to describe with "I," "my power," and "my majesty"? How could you describe them more truthfully?

### Daniel 4:31–33 — "While the Word Was in the King's Mouth"

"While the word was in the king's mouth, there fell a voice from heaven, saying, O king Nebuchadnezzar, to thee it is spoken; The kingdom is departed from thee." There was no gap between boast and answer. The voice repeated the decree: he would be driven from men, dwell with beasts, eat grass, "and seven times shall pass over thee, until thou know that the most High ruleth" (verse 32).

"The same hour was the thing fulfilled." He "did eat grass as oxen, and his body was wet with the dew of heaven, till his hairs were grown like eagles' feathers, and his nails like birds' claws" (verse 33). The king who had made himself the center of the world now lived like an animal outside it.

The text presents this as a sign and a judgment from God, not as a medical case. Readers have proposed diagnoses, but the scripture does not give one, and this guide does not. Neither should this chapter be used to suggest that mental illness today is a punishment for pride. Most suffering has no such cause, and Jesus himself rejected the assumption that affliction proves sin ([John 9:2–3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/john/9?lang=eng&id=p2-p3#p2)). This chapter is about one king, a specific warning, and its specific purpose: "till thou know."

**Question:** Nebuchadnezzar became like a beast because he forgot he was a creature under God. What helps you remember your dependence on God in the middle of success?

### Daniel 4:34–35 — "Lifted Up Mine Eyes unto Heaven"

The first-person voice returns: "And at the end of the days I Nebuchadnezzar lifted up mine eyes unto heaven, and mine understanding returned unto me." The footnote explains "the end of the days" as the seven years. The order matters. He looked up first, and then his understanding returned. Beasts graze looking down; a human being lifts his eyes to heaven.

His first act was praise: "I blessed the most High, and I praised and honoured him that liveth for ever, whose dominion is an everlasting dominion, and his kingdom is from generation to generation." Verse 35 continues: "all the inhabitants of the earth are reputed as nothing: and he doeth according to his will in the army of heaven, and among the inhabitants of the earth: and none can stay his hand, or say unto him, What doest thou?" Compare Isaiah's "the nations are as a drop of a bucket" ([Isaiah 40:15](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/40?lang=eng&id=p15#p15)) and Job's "who will say unto him, What doest thou?" ([Job 9:12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/job/9?lang=eng&id=p12#p12)).

"Reputed as nothing" means nothing in comparison with God's power, not worthless in God's eyes. Moses learned both truths: "man is nothing" ([Moses 1:10](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/pgp/moses/1?lang=eng&id=p10#p10)), and also that God's work and glory is "to bring to pass the immortality and eternal life of man" ([Moses 1:39](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/pgp/moses/1?lang=eng&id=p39#p39)).

*Christ connection (devotional reading):* The Savior "humbled himself, and became obedient unto death" ([Philippians 2:8](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/philip/2?lang=eng&id=p8#p8)), the opposite of the king's self-exaltation. Nebuchadnezzar was humbled against his will; Christ humbled himself freely. Both stories end with God exalting the humble.

**Question:** What does it mean for you to lift up your eyes to heaven in a practical way when your understanding has failed you?

### Daniel 4:36–37 — "Those That Walk in Pride He Is Able to Abase"

"At the same time my reason returned unto me." His honor and brightness returned, his counsellors sought him, "and I was established in my kingdom, and excellent majesty was added unto me." The stump grew again. The promise of verse 26 was kept.

The letter ends with praise and a lesson: "Now I Nebuchadnezzar praise and extol and honour the King of heaven, all whose works are truth, and his ways judgment: and those that walk in pride he is able to abase." The footnote offers "justice" for "judgment." The king who once claimed all honor for himself now calls God "the King of heaven." Jesus taught the same principle: "whosoever exalteth himself shall be abased; and he that humbleth himself shall be exalted" ([Luke 14:11](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/luke/14?lang=eng&id=p11#p11)).

The text does not say how far Nebuchadnezzar's change went or whether it lasted. It records a real confession, which he wanted the whole world to hear.

**Question:** Nebuchadnezzar's last words in the book are praise. What would you want your own testimony of God's correction and mercy to say?

## Daniel 5: Weighed in the Balances

Chapter 5 jumps to the empire's last night, about two decades after Nebuchadnezzar's death, when Belshazzar is ruling in Babylon. The chapter pairs naturally with chapter 4. Both feature a proud king and a message from heaven that only Daniel can read. Nebuchadnezzar was humbled and restored. Belshazzar knew that story and ignored it.

### Daniel 5:1–4 — Drinking from the Temple Vessels

"Belshazzar the king made a great feast to a thousand of his lords, and drank wine before the thousand." While tasting the wine, he ordered servants to bring "the golden and silver vessels which his father Nebuchadnezzar had taken out of the temple which was in Jerusalem; that the king, and his princes, his wives, and his concubines, might drink therein" (verse 2). These are the vessels of [Daniel 1:2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/1?lang=eng&id=p2#p2). They had stayed in a Babylonian treasure house for decades. Now they became cups at a drinking party.

"They drank wine, and praised the gods of gold, and of silver, of brass, of iron, of wood, and of stone" (verse 4). The list of materials recalls the metals of the statue in chapter 2, now turned into objects of worship. Sacred things were being used to praise idols.

Under the law of Moses, even the Levites who served at the sanctuary were forbidden to "come nigh the vessels of the sanctuary and the altar" at all, "that neither they, nor ye also, die" ([Numbers 18:3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/num/18?lang=eng&id=p3#p3)), and Isaiah urged, "be ye clean, that bear the vessels of the Lord" ([Isaiah 52:11](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/52?lang=eng&id=p11#p11)). Belshazzar's act was not ignorance. It was deliberate mockery of the God of the people his predecessor had conquered.

**Question:** What sacred things can be treated casually or mockingly in modern life? How do you keep holy things holy?

### Daniel 5:5–6 — The Fingers of a Man's Hand

"In the same hour came forth fingers of a man's hand, and wrote over against the candlestick upon the plaster of the wall of the king's palace: and the king saw the part of the hand that wrote." The footnote gives "opposite the lampstand." The writing appeared where the light was strongest, so no one could miss it.

The king's reaction is described physically: "the king's countenance was changed, and his thoughts troubled him, so that the joints of his loins were loosed, and his knees smote one against another" (verse 6). The footnote explains "joints of his loins" as hip joints. His legs gave way and his knees knocked together.

Isaiah had prophesied about Cyrus, the conqueror who would take Babylon: "I will loose the loins of kings, to open before him the two leaved gates" ([Isaiah 45:1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/45?lang=eng&id=p1#p1)). Belshazzar's loins were loosed that very night. The Church's footnotes also connect the hand to the brother of Jared, who saw "the finger of the Lord" ([Ether 3:6](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/ether/3?lang=eng&id=p6#p6)).

**Question:** Belshazzar's confidence collapsed the moment he saw evidence that God was watching. What does it mean to live as if that hand were always present?

### Daniel 5:7–9 — The Third Ruler in the Kingdom

"The king cried aloud to bring in the astrologers, the Chaldeans, and the soothsayers." He offered a lavish reward: "Whosoever shall read this writing, and shew me the interpretation thereof, shall be clothed with scarlet, and have a chain of gold about his neck, and shall be the third ruler in the kingdom."

Why third and not second? The text does not explain. It fits the historical picture described in the Bible Dictionary: Belshazzar ruled as prince-regent for his father Nabonidus, so the best he could offer was the place after the two of them ([Bible Dictionary, "Belshazzar"](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bd/belshazzar?lang=eng)).

"Then came in all the king's wise men: but they could not read the writing, nor make known to the king the interpretation thereof" (verse 8). For the third time in the book, Babylon's experts failed. "Then was king Belshazzar greatly troubled, and his countenance was changed in him, and his lords were astonied" (verse 9). The whole court was paralyzed.

**Question:** What do you turn to first when you face something you cannot understand? What would it look like to turn to God sooner?

### Daniel 5:10–12 — The Queen Remembers Daniel

"Now the queen, by reason of the words of the king and his lords, came into the banquet house." This queen is probably not one of Belshazzar's wives, who were already at the feast (verse 2). She may have been the queen mother. She spoke with calm authority: "O king, live for ever: let not thy thoughts trouble thee, nor let thy countenance be changed."

She remembered what the king had forgotten: "There is a man in thy kingdom, in whom is the spirit of the holy gods." In Nebuchadnezzar's days, "light and understanding and wisdom, like the wisdom of the gods, was found in him." She listed Daniel's gifts: "an excellent spirit, and knowledge, and understanding, interpreting of dreams, and shewing of hard sentences, and dissolving of doubts" (verse 12). "Hard sentences" means riddles; "dissolving of doubts" means solving knotty problems.

Daniel was by now an old man. Belshazzar's court had apparently set him aside. Yet when crisis came, the one person who could read the wall was the faithful servant who had been forgotten.

**Question:** Daniel had been overlooked by those in power, yet he was ready when he was needed. How do you stay faithful during seasons when your gifts seem unnoticed?

### Daniel 5:13–16 — "Art Thou That Daniel?"

"Then was Daniel brought in before the king." Belshazzar's greeting was condescending: "Art thou that Daniel, which art of the children of the captivity of Judah, whom the king my father brought out of Jewry?" ("Jewry" means Judah.) Before acknowledging Daniel's gifts, he reminded him that he was a captive.

He repeated what he had heard: "the spirit of the gods is in thee, and that light and understanding and excellent wisdom is found in thee" (verse 14). He admitted that his own wise men had failed (verse 15) and repeated the offer of scarlet, a gold chain, and the third place in the kingdom (verse 16).

**Question:** Belshazzar knew about Daniel's gifts by reputation only. What is the difference between hearing about God's servants and listening to them?

### Daniel 5:17 — "Let Thy Gifts Be to Thyself"

"Then Daniel answered and said before the king, Let thy gifts be to thyself, and give thy rewards to another; yet I will read the writing unto the king, and make known to him the interpretation."

Daniel refused to let the message be bought. The Church's footnote points to Elisha, who refused Naaman's gifts after his healing: "As the Lord liveth, before whom I stand, I will receive none" ([2 Kings 5:16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/2-kgs/5?lang=eng&id=p16#p16)). Prophets speak what God gives them, not what earns a reward. Daniel's refusal also gave him freedom to say what came next, which no paid adviser would have dared to say.

Daniel's refusal is the turning point of the scene: he stands before the king as a messenger, not a courtier.

**Question:** Where might rewards, approval, or comfort tempt you to soften what you know is true?

### Daniel 5:18–21 — Remember Nebuchadnezzar

Before reading the wall, Daniel gave a history lesson. "O thou king, the most high God gave Nebuchadnezzar thy father a kingdom, and majesty, and glory, and honour." The word "gave" again. Because of that majesty, all peoples feared Nebuchadnezzar: "whom he would he slew; and whom he would he kept alive; and whom he would he set up; and whom he would he put down" (verse 19). This describes absolute power. It is not an endorsement of it; it describes power that God would soon call to account.

"But when his heart was lifted up, and his mind hardened in pride, he was deposed from his kingly throne, and they took his glory from him" (verse 20). Daniel retold chapter 4: driven from men, his heart made like the beasts, his dwelling "with the wild asses," fed with grass, wet with dew, "till he knew that the most high God ruled in the kingdom of men, and that he appointeth over it whomsoever he will" (verse 21).

**Question:** Daniel used another person's story as a warning. What stories of others' pride and humility have helped you see yourself more clearly?

### Daniel 5:22–23 — "Though Thou Knewest All This"

"And thou his son, O Belshazzar, hast not humbled thine heart, though thou knewest all this." This is the heart of the chapter. Belshazzar's sin was not ignorance. He knew the story of chapter 4 and refused its lesson.

Daniel named the charge: "But hast lifted up thyself against the Lord of heaven; and they have brought the vessels of his house before thee, and thou, and thy lords, thy wives, and thy concubines, have drunk wine in them; and thou hast praised the gods of silver, and gold, of brass, iron, wood, and stone, which see not, nor hear, nor know." Israel's scriptures had long mocked such gods: "They have mouths, but they speak not: eyes have they, but they see not" ([Psalm 115:5](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ps/115?lang=eng&id=p5#p5); compare [Deuteronomy 4:28](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/deut/4?lang=eng&id=p28#p28)).

Then the decisive line: "and the God in whose hand thy breath is, and whose are all thy ways, hast thou not glorified." The king worshipped gods who could not breathe, and neglected the God who gave him every breath. King Benjamin taught that God is "lending you breath, that ye may live and move" ([Mosiah 2:21](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/mosiah/2?lang=eng&id=p21#p21)), and Paul told the Athenians that God "giveth to all life, and breath, and all things" ([Acts 17:25](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/acts/17?lang=eng&id=p25#p25)).

**Question:** "Though thou knewest all this." What do you already know about God that you have not yet let humble your heart?

### Daniel 5:24–25 — "Mene, Mene, Tekel, Upharsin"

"Then was the part of the hand sent from him; and this writing was written. And this is the writing that was written, Mene, Mene, Tekel, Upharsin." Daniel traced the hand to its source: it was "sent from him," the God Belshazzar had refused to glorify.

The words are Aramaic. The Babylonian experts may have been able to see the letters, but no one could make sense of them. Ancient writing often omitted vowels, so the same letters could be read in more than one way. *Scripture Helps* notes two layers: the words name weights in descending order, perhaps hinting at Babylon's dwindling power, and they also sound like Aramaic verbs for numbering, weighing, and dividing. Daniel's interpretation draws on the verbs.

**Question:** Why might God have chosen a message that ordinary eyes could see but only an inspired servant could understand?

### Daniel 5:26–28 — Numbered, Weighed, Divided

Daniel gave the interpretation, one word at a time:

- **"Mene; God hath numbered thy kingdom, and finished it."** The footnote gives "numbered." Belshazzar's days had been counted, and the count was complete. The Church's footnote points to Jeremiah's prophecy that after seventy years the Lord would "punish the king of Babylon" ([Jeremiah 25:12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/25?lang=eng&id=p12#p12)).
- **"Tekel; Thou art weighed in the balances, and art found wanting."** The footnote gives "shekel, or weight." Job asked, "Let me be weighed in an even balance, that God may know mine integrity" ([Job 31:6](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/job/31?lang=eng&id=p6#p6)), and Hannah declared that "by him actions are weighed" ([1 Samuel 2:3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/1-sam/2?lang=eng&id=p3#p3)). Belshazzar's life was weighed and came up short.
- **"Peres; Thy kingdom is divided, and given to the Medes and Persians."** The footnote gives "division." "Peres" also sounds like the name of Persia. The Church's footnote records that the Joseph Smith Translation reads "Upharsin" here, matching the form in verse 25.

*Christ connection:* The Father "hath committed all judgment unto the Son," and has given him authority to judge "because he is the Son of man" ([John 5:22](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/john/5?lang=eng&id=p22#p22), [27](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/john/5?lang=eng&id=p27#p27)). Every life will be weighed. The same Judge also invites, "come unto Christ, and be perfected in him," promising that "then is his grace sufficient for you" ([Moroni 10:32](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/moro/10?lang=eng&id=p32#p32)). Belshazzar's tragedy was not that he was weighed, but that he never turned to the One who could have made up what was lacking.

**Question:** If your life were weighed today, what would you most want the Lord to find? What could you begin now?

### Daniel 5:29 — Scarlet and a Chain of Gold

"Then commanded Belshazzar, and they clothed Daniel with scarlet, and put a chain of gold about his neck, and made a proclamation concerning him, that he should be the third ruler in the kingdom." Daniel had said, "Let thy gifts be to thyself," but the king gave them anyway, and Daniel did not resist.

The honors are like those Pharaoh gave Joseph: fine clothing and "a gold chain about his neck" ([Genesis 41:42](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/gen/41?lang=eng&id=p42#p42)). But Joseph's honors lasted for years. Daniel's lasted only hours, because the kingdom that gave them was about to end. The irony is sharp. Belshazzar honored the messenger and ignored the message.

**Question:** Belshazzar rewarded Daniel but did not repent. How can we honor God's servants in outward ways while missing the point of what they teach?

### Daniel 5:30–31 — "In That Night"

"In that night was Belshazzar the king of the Chaldeans slain. And Darius the Median took the kingdom, being about threescore and two years old." Two short verses end the Babylonian empire. Daniel's interpretation was fulfilled within hours.

Babylon fell to the forces of Cyrus in 539 B.C. Jeremiah's oracle against Babylon speaks of feasts that end in sudden ruin: "In their heat I will make their feasts, and I will make them drunken, that they may rejoice, and sleep a perpetual sleep" ([Jeremiah 51:39](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/51?lang=eng&id=p39#p39)). Isaiah had named Cyrus as the Lord's anointed instrument ([Isaiah 45:1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/45?lang=eng&id=p1#p1)).

As noted in the introduction, "Darius the Median" cannot be confidently matched with any ruler known from other ancient records. The text presents him as the man who received the kingdom on behalf of the new Medo-Persian power. Daniel himself survived the change of empire, just as [Daniel 1:21](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/1?lang=eng&id=p21#p21) said he would.

**Question:** Belshazzar's last night began as a party. What does this chapter teach about the danger of feeling secure while ignoring God's warnings?

## Daniel 6: The Lions' Den

Chapter 6 mirrors chapter 3. Again a faithful servant of God is condemned under a royal decree for his worship, thrown to certain death, and delivered, and again a king ends by praising Daniel's God. This time the hero is Daniel himself, now an old man, and the trap is not a golden image but a ban on prayer.

### Daniel 6:1–3 — "An Excellent Spirit Was in Him"

"It pleased Darius to set over the kingdom an hundred and twenty princes, which should be over the whole kingdom," with "three presidents; of whom Daniel was first" over them (verses 1–2). The footnote gives "one" as an alternative to "first." The purpose of the arrangement was accountability: "that the princes might give accounts unto them, and the king should have no damage" (verse 2). Persian kings governed large territories through provincial officials (compare [Esther 1:1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/esth/1?lang=eng&id=p1#p1)).

"Then this Daniel was preferred above the presidents and princes, because an excellent spirit was in him; and the king thought to set him over the whole realm" (verse 3). Daniel had served Babylon's kings, and now a new regime recognized his ability as well. Like Joseph, who was set "over all the land of Egypt" ([Genesis 41:41](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/gen/41?lang=eng&id=p41#p41)), Daniel was about to be placed over everything. That prospect set the plot in motion.

**Question:** What do you think the phrase "an excellent spirit" describes? How might that spirit show up in your work, school, or family?

### Daniel 6:4–5 — "Neither Was There Any Error or Fault"

"Then the presidents and princes sought to find occasion against Daniel concerning the kingdom; but they could find none occasion nor fault; forasmuch as he was faithful, neither was there any error or fault found in him." They investigated his public work and found nothing: no corruption, no carelessness, no mistakes. "Faithful" here means trustworthy.

So they changed strategy: "We shall not find any occasion against this Daniel, except we find it against him concerning the law of his God" (verse 5). The only thing they could use against Daniel was his devotion. Peter later urged believers to live so honestly "that, whereas they speak against you as evildoers, they may by your good works, which they shall behold, glorify God" ([1 Peter 2:12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/1-pet/2?lang=eng&id=p12#p12)).

*Christ connection:* *Scripture Helps* lists several likenesses between Daniel's story and the Savior's last days. The first is here: at Jesus's trial "the chief priests and all the council sought for witness against Jesus to put him to death; and found none" ([Mark 14:55](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/mark/14?lang=eng&id=p55#p55)).

**Question:** If someone examined your life looking for fault, what would they find? What would they find "concerning the law of [your] God"?

### Daniel 6:6–9 — A Statute Built on Flattery

"These presidents and princes assembled together to the king, and said thus unto him, King Darius, live for ever." Their proposal sounded unanimous: "All the presidents of the kingdom, the governors, and the princes, the counsellors, and the captains, have consulted together to establish a royal statute, and to make a firm decree, that whosoever shall ask a petition of any God or man for thirty days, save of thee, O king, he shall be cast into the den of lions" (verse 7).

"All the presidents" was a lie. Daniel was one of the three presidents, and he had not been consulted. The plan flattered the king by making him the only channel of petition for a month. It looked like a loyalty measure; it was a trap built around one man's known habit of prayer.

"Now, O king, establish the decree, and sign the writing, that it be not changed, according to the law of the Medes and Persians, which altereth not" (verse 8). The footnote explains: it could not be revoked once signed. The book of Esther describes the same principle ([Esther 1:19](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/esth/1?lang=eng&id=p19#p19); [8:8](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/esth/8?lang=eng&id=p8#p8)). "Wherefore king Darius signed the writing and the decree" (verse 9). He did not see that he was signing away his best servant.

**Question:** How can flattery lead good people to sign decisions they have not thought through? What questions might Darius have asked?

### Daniel 6:10 — "His Windows Being Open"

"Now when Daniel knew that the writing was signed, he went into his house; and his windows being open in his chamber toward Jerusalem, he kneeled upon his knees three times a day, and prayed, and gave thanks before his God, as he did aforetime."

Every detail matters:

- **"When Daniel knew."** He acted with full knowledge of the decree. This was not an accident.
- **"He went into his house."** He did not go to the public square to protest. He prayed at home, in his own room.
- **"Windows being open ... toward Jerusalem."** At the temple's dedication Solomon had prayed that exiles "in the land of their enemies" might "pray unto thee toward their land ... the city which thou hast chosen, and the house which I have built for thy name" ([1 Kings 8:48](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/1-kgs/8?lang=eng&id=p48#p48)). Daniel was doing exactly that. When he prayed, Jerusalem and its temple were still in ruins. He faced a destroyed house of God, trusting God's promise to restore it.
- **"Three times a day."** Compare the Psalm: "Evening, and morning, and at noon, will I pray" ([Psalm 55:17](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ps/55?lang=eng&id=p17#p17)), and Amulek's counsel to "cry unto him in your houses ... both morning, mid-day, and evening" ([Alma 34:21](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/34?lang=eng&id=p21#p21)).
- **"And gave thanks."** Under a death sentence, Daniel's prayer included thanksgiving.
- **"As he did aforetime."** This is the key phrase. Daniel did not start a new act of defiance. He simply continued what he had always done. Years of daily prayer made this choice almost automatic.

Daniel neither hid his prayer nor made a show of it. Jesus taught that private prayer should not be performed for others ([Matthew 6:6](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/6?lang=eng&id=p6#p6)). Daniel's prayer was private in purpose, yet he would not close the windows to escape being seen.

John Hilton III suggests a helpful contrast. When Alma's people were forbidden to pray aloud on pain of death, they "did pour out their hearts to him" silently, and the Lord heard them ([Mosiah 24:10–12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/mosiah/24?lang=eng&id=p10-p12#p10)). Sometimes God's people follow him openly despite a royal command, and sometimes they are led to worship quietly ([Hilton, "Teaching the Book of Daniel"](https://johnhiltoniii.com/teaching-the-book-of-daniel/)). Both responses can be faithful. What neither group did was stop praying.

**Question:** What spiritual habits are you building "aforetime" that would hold you steady if they were suddenly forbidden or made costly?

### Daniel 6:11–13 — Found Praying

"Then these men assembled, and found Daniel praying and making supplication before his God." The trap worked exactly as planned. They had only to watch his window.

They went to the king and first secured his commitment, getting him to confirm the decree and its unchangeable status (verse 12). Only then did they name their target: "That Daniel, which is of the children of the captivity of Judah, regardeth not thee, O king, nor the decree that thou hast signed, but maketh his petition three times a day" (verse 13). They did not call him the first of the presidents. They called him a captive from Judah, as if he were still a foreigner after more than sixty years of service. And they twisted his faithfulness to God into disrespect for the king.

**Question:** The accusers reduced Daniel to a label. How can you resist reducing people to labels, especially people whose faith or background differs from yours?

### Daniel 6:14–15 — "He Laboured Till the Going Down of the Sun"

"Then the king, when he heard these words, was sore displeased with himself, and set his heart on Daniel to deliver him: and he laboured till the going down of the sun to deliver him." Darius was angry, but at himself. He realized he had been manipulated. He spent the whole day searching for a legal way out.

The conspirators returned at sunset to close the door: "Know, O king, that the law of the Medes and Persians is, That no decree nor statute which the king establisheth may be changed" (verse 15). A law meant to show the king's absolute authority had made him powerless to save his friend.

*Christ connection:* *Scripture Helps* compares Darius with Pilate, who said of Jesus, "I find no fault in this man" ([Luke 23:4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/luke/23?lang=eng&id=p4#p4)), sought to release him, and still "gave sentence that it should be as they required" ([Luke 23:24](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/luke/23?lang=eng&id=p24#p24)).

**Question:** Darius could not undo his own decree. What decisions have you made too quickly that later limited your ability to do good? What can you learn from that?

### Daniel 6:16–17 — The Stone and the Seal

"Then the king commanded, and they brought Daniel, and cast him into the den of lions." Before the stone was rolled into place, the king spoke words that are partly faith and partly hope: "Thy God whom thou servest continually, he will deliver thee." "Continually" echoes "as he did aforetime." Even the king had noticed Daniel's constancy.

"And a stone was brought, and laid upon the mouth of the den; and the king sealed it with his own signet, and with the signet of his lords; that the purpose might not be changed concerning Daniel" (verse 17). The den was apparently a pit with an opening at the top (compare verses 23–24). The double seal meant that neither the king nor the conspirators could tamper with it.

*Christ connection:* After Jesus was buried, the chief priests "made the sepulchre sure, sealing the stone, and setting a watch" ([Matthew 27:66](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/27?lang=eng&id=p66#p66)). *Scripture Helps* notes this likeness as well. Human authorities sealed both stones, and neither seal could stop God.

**Question:** What sealed stones in your life seem final? How does this story, and the Savior's, invite you to see them differently?

### Daniel 6:18–20 — A King's Sleepless Night

"Then the king went to his palace, and passed the night fasting: neither were instruments of musick brought before him: and his sleep went from him." The king who had signed the decree spent the night as a mourner. Like Nebuchadnezzar in [Daniel 2:1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/2?lang=eng&id=p1#p1), he could not sleep.

"Then the king arose very early in the morning, and went in haste unto the den of lions" (verse 19). The early-morning rush to a sealed stone recalls the women who came to Jesus's tomb "very early in the morning ... at the rising of the sun" ([Mark 16:2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/mark/16?lang=eng&id=p2#p2)).

"And when he came to the den, he cried with a lamentable voice unto Daniel: and the king spake and said to Daniel, O Daniel, servant of the living God, is thy God, whom thou servest continually, able to deliver thee from the lions?" (verse 20). The question returns: is God *able*? It is the same question Nebuchadnezzar asked in chapter 3. Darius asked it with hope rather than scorn, and he used a striking title: "the living God."

**Question:** Darius called out his question before he knew the answer. What questions about God's ability are you holding in hope right now?

### Daniel 6:21–23 — "My God Hath Sent His Angel"

"Then said Daniel unto the king, O king, live for ever." After a night among lions, Daniel's first words were a courteous blessing on the man who had put him there. He showed no bitterness.

"My God hath sent his angel, and hath shut the lions' mouths, that they have not hurt me: forasmuch as before him innocency was found in me; and also before thee, O king, have I done no hurt" (verse 22). Daniel claimed innocence before both God and the king. He had disobeyed one decree, but he had not wronged the king. The Articles of Faith hold both principles: "We claim the privilege of worshiping Almighty God according to the dictates of our own conscience" and "We believe in being subject to kings, presidents, rulers, and magistrates" ([Articles of Faith 1:11–12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/pgp/a-of-f/1?lang=eng&id=p11-p12#p11)). When the two collided, Peter's answer applied: "We ought to obey God rather than men" ([Acts 5:29](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/acts/5?lang=eng&id=p29#p29)). Hebrews remembers those "who through faith ... stopped the mouths of lions" ([Hebrews 11:33](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/heb/11?lang=eng&id=p33#p33)).

"Then was the king exceeding glad for him, and commanded that they should take Daniel up out of the den. So Daniel was taken up out of the den, and no manner of hurt was found upon him, because he believed in his God" (verse 23). The narrator gives the reason directly: "because he believed."

*Christ connection, with a difference:* *Scripture Helps* compares Daniel emerging alive from the sealed den with Jesus rising from the sealed tomb ([1 Corinthians 15:3–4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/1-cor/15?lang=eng&id=p3-p4#p3)). The comparison is meaningful, but the difference is just as important. Daniel was preserved from death; he never died in the den. Jesus actually died, was buried, and rose again, "the firstfruits of them that slept" ([1 Corinthians 15:20](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/1-cor/15?lang=eng&id=p20#p20)). Daniel's deliverance is a foreshadowing. Christ's resurrection is the victory that makes every deliverance, including deliverance from death itself, possible.

**Question:** Daniel spoke kindly to the king who had condemned him. How does trust in God free us from bitterness toward people who have harmed us?

### Daniel 6:24 — The Accusers and Their Families

"And the king commanded, and they brought those men which had accused Daniel, and they cast them into the den of lions, them, their children, and their wives; and the lions had the mastery of them, and brake all their bones in pieces or ever they came at the bottom of the den." The footnote gives "overpowered them" for "had the mastery."

This is one of the hardest verses in the week's reading, and it should be read honestly. The verse reports what a Persian king did. It does not present the king as a model, and it does not say God commanded it. Israel's own law forbade this kind of collective punishment: "the children [shall not] be put to death for the fathers: every man shall be put to death for his own sin" ([Deuteronomy 24:16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/deut/24?lang=eng&id=p16#p16); compare [Ezekiel 18:20](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/18?lang=eng&id=p20#p20)). The same royal cruelty appeared in the threats of [2:5](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/2?lang=eng&id=p5#p5) and [3:29](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/3?lang=eng&id=p29#p29). Daniel's God delivered Daniel; the king's law destroyed the innocent along with the guilty.

The verse also serves a function in the story. The lions were not tame. They overpowered the men before they reached the bottom. Daniel's survival had not depended on sleepy or well-fed animals; it depended on God. The same Aramaic idiom used for "accused" here appears in [3:8](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/3?lang=eng&id=p8#p8). Those who had figuratively devoured Daniel were literally devoured.

Readers should not celebrate the deaths of the women and children in this verse. Alma, watching innocent people suffer in Ammonihah, taught that the Lord "receiveth them up unto himself, in glory," and that the blood of the innocent would stand as a witness against those who did it ([Alma 14:11](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/14?lang=eng&id=p11#p11)). Justice for such victims belongs to God.

**Question:** How can we read accounts of violence in scripture honestly, without excusing them and without losing faith in a just and merciful God?

### Daniel 6:25–27 — "He Delivereth and Rescueth"

"Then king Darius wrote unto all people, nations, and languages, that dwell in all the earth; Peace be multiplied unto you." Like Nebuchadnezzar in chapter 4, Darius sent a message to the whole world.

"I make a decree, That in every dominion of my kingdom men tremble and fear before the God of Daniel: for he is the living God, and steadfast for ever, and his kingdom that which shall not be destroyed, and his dominion shall be even unto the end" (verse 26). A Median king now says what Daniel had said in [Daniel 2:44](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/2?lang=eng&id=p44#p44): God's kingdom "shall never be destroyed." Notice that, unlike Nebuchadnezzar's decree in 3:29, this one contains no threat of death. It still commands reverence by decree, but the tone has changed.

"He delivereth and rescueth, and he worketh signs and wonders in heaven and in earth, who hath delivered Daniel from the power of the lions" (verse 27). Here is the answer to Nebuchadnezzar's mocking question in 3:15 and to Darius's own anxious question in 6:20. The God of Daniel is able. The lesson title, taken from 3:29, finds its fullest echo here.

**Question:** Darius's praise came from what he saw in Daniel's life. What might people around you conclude about God from what they see in yours?

### Daniel 6:28 — "So This Daniel Prospered"

"So this Daniel prospered in the reign of Darius, and in the reign of Cyrus the Persian." The story section of the book ends with Daniel thriving under a second empire. The Church's footnotes point to Cyrus's decree allowing the Jews to return and rebuild the temple ([Ezra 1:1–2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezra/1?lang=eng&id=p1-p2#p1)). The Jerusalem Daniel prayed toward from an open window would soon be rebuilt.

The phrase "this Daniel" has appeared before, in verses 3 and 5. "This Daniel" was faithful, blameless, prayerful, and courteous, and "this Daniel" prospered. The verse does not promise that every faithful person will prosper in worldly terms. It records how this one man's long faithfulness ended.

**Question:** Looking back over chapters 1–6, which of Daniel's qualities would you most like to develop, and what first step could you take?

## Daniel 7: The Ancient of Days and One Like the Son of Man

Chapter 7 begins the second half of the book, Daniel's visions told in his own voice. It is also the last Aramaic chapter, and it answers chapter 2. There, a pagan king dreamed of four metals and a stone. Here, the prophet himself sees four beasts and a heavenly court. Both visions end the same way: human empires pass, and God gives an everlasting kingdom. Chapter 7 adds the figure who receives it: "one like the Son of man."

### Daniel 7:1 — "Then He Wrote the Dream"

"In the first year of Belshazzar king of Babylon Daniel had a dream and visions of his head upon his bed: then he wrote the dream, and told the sum of the matters." The date takes us back in time, before the feast of chapter 5. Daniel received this vision years before the empire fell, while Babylon still looked secure.

Daniel "wrote the dream." Until now Daniel had interpreted other people's dreams; now he records his own. He also "told the sum," meaning the main points. The chapter does not answer every question; it gives the essential message.

**Question:** Daniel wrote down what he received. How does recording spiritual impressions help you understand and remember them?

### Daniel 7:2–3 — Four Winds on the Great Sea

"Daniel spake and said, I saw in my vision by night, and, behold, the four winds of the heaven strove upon the great sea. And four great beasts came up from the sea, diverse one from another." The four winds come from every direction at once. The sea churns. Out of that chaos rise four monsters.

In scripture the sea often stands for chaos and for restless nations. Revelation explains that the waters John saw "are peoples, and multitudes, and nations, and tongues" ([Revelation 17:15](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/rev/17?lang=eng&id=p15#p15)). The Church's footnote to Daniel 7:3 points to Revelation 13, where John also sees a beast rise from the sea. In the Joseph Smith Translation of that verse John says the beast was "in the likeness of the kingdoms of the earth" ([Revelation 13:1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/rev/13?lang=eng&id=p1#p1), JST footnote). That matches Daniel's own interpretation in verse 17: the beasts are kingdoms.

Hilton's class slides set Daniel 7 side by side with Revelation 13 and 17 to show how much John's beasts draw on Daniel's: a beast from the sea, a lion, a bear, a leopard, ten horns, a mouth speaking great things, and war against the saints ([Hilton, Class 45](https://johnhiltoniii.com/findingchrist/class-45-the-abomination-of-desolation-daniel-1-7/)). Reading the two visions together helps each make sense.

**Question:** Why might God show human empires as beasts rising from a stormy sea? What does that suggest about how heaven views raw power?

### Daniel 7:4 — A Lion with Eagle's Wings

"The first was like a lion, and had eagle's wings: I beheld till the wings thereof were plucked, and it was lifted up from the earth, and made stand upon the feet as a man, and a man's heart was given to it."

The Church's footnote connects this beast to the head of gold in [Daniel 2:38](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/2?lang=eng&id=p38#p38), that is, to Babylon. Jeremiah used the lion for Babylon's armies ("The lion is come up from his thicket," [Jeremiah 4:7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/4?lang=eng&id=p7#p7)), and Ezekiel pictured Babylon's king as "a great eagle with great wings" ([Ezekiel 17:3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/17?lang=eng&id=p3#p3), [12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/17?lang=eng&id=p12#p12)).

The rest of the verse reads like a summary of chapter 4. Nebuchadnezzar's "heart [was] changed from man's" to a beast's ([4:16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/4?lang=eng&id=p16#p16)), and his hair grew "like eagles' feathers" ([4:33](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/4?lang=eng&id=p33#p33)). Here the process runs in reverse: the wings are plucked, the beast stands upright "as a man," and it is given "a man's heart." Whether or not Daniel intended an allusion, the parallel is striking. The first beast is the one kingdom in the vision that becomes more human.

**Question:** The lion becomes more human when its wings are plucked. When has losing something you were proud of made you more humane?

### Daniel 7:5 — A Bear with Three Ribs

"And behold another beast, a second, like to a bear, and it raised up itself on one side, and it had three ribs in the mouth of it between the teeth of it: and they said thus unto it, Arise, devour much flesh."

The Church's footnotes connect this beast with the second, "inferior" kingdom of [Daniel 2:39](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/2?lang=eng&id=p39#p39) and with "the Medes and Persians" of [Daniel 5:28](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/5?lang=eng&id=p28#p28). Interpreters have suggested that the bear raised "on one side" could reflect an uneven alliance in which one partner was stronger, and that the three ribs could be earlier conquests. Those suggestions are possible but not certain. The clearer point is the command, "Arise, devour much flesh." Someone gives the order. Even this hungry power acts within limits it did not set.

**Question:** The bear is told what to do. How does knowing that even destructive powers are under God's limits help you face fearful events?

### Daniel 7:6 — A Leopard with Four Wings and Four Heads

"After this I beheld, and lo another, like a leopard, which had upon the back of it four wings of a fowl; the beast had also four heads; and dominion was given to it."

A leopard is fast; a leopard with four wings is faster still. The four heads suggest a kingdom that becomes divided four ways. The Church's footnotes point to [Daniel 8:8](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/8?lang=eng&id=p8#p8) and [11:4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/11?lang=eng&id=p4#p4), where a great kingdom breaks into four after its first king falls. Chapter 8 names that kingdom "Grecia" ([Daniel 8:21–22](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/8?lang=eng&id=p21-p22#p21)).

Notice again the passive verb: "dominion was given to it." Throughout chapter 7, power is given and taken away by someone above the beasts. The beasts never grant themselves authority.

**Question:** Each beast is "given" its dominion. How does this phrase change the way you think about the powerful people and institutions of your own day?

### Daniel 7:7–8 — The Fourth Beast and the Little Horn

"After this I saw in the night visions, and behold a fourth beast, dreadful and terrible, and strong exceedingly; and it had great iron teeth: it devoured and brake in pieces, and stamped the residue with the feet of it: and it was diverse from all the beasts that were before it; and it had ten horns."

This beast is not compared with any known animal. It is simply "diverse," more terrible than the rest. Its "iron teeth" link it to the iron kingdom of [Daniel 2:40](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/2?lang=eng&id=p40#p40), which "breaketh in pieces and subdueth all things." It consumes, crushes, and tramples what it does not consume. In scripture a horn often stands for strength or a ruler, and the vision itself will interpret the ten horns as ten kings (verse 24).

"I considered the horns, and, behold, there came up among them another little horn, before whom there were three of the first horns plucked up by the roots: and, behold, in this horn were eyes like the eyes of man, and a mouth speaking great things" (verse 8). This small horn becomes the focus of the vision. It has human eyes and a boastful mouth. Revelation's beast likewise has "a mouth speaking great things and blasphemies" ([Revelation 13:5](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/rev/13?lang=eng&id=p5#p5)). Human intelligence joined to beastly power makes it more dangerous, not less.

**Question:** The little horn's most notable feature is its mouth. What does that suggest about how arrogance shows itself, and about how we use our own words?

### Daniel 7:9–10 — The Ancient of Days Takes His Seat

"I beheld till the thrones were cast down, and the Ancient of days did sit." The Church's footnote gives the Aramaic sense as thrones "set up." Thrones were being placed for a court, not overthrown. The scene shifts from the sea to heaven, from beasts to a judge.

"Whose garment was white as snow, and the hair of his head like the pure wool: his throne was like the fiery flame, and his wheels as burning fire." White garment and white hair suggest purity, age, and wisdom. The throne has wheels of fire, much like the mobile throne Ezekiel saw beside the river Chebar (compare [Ezekiel 1:15](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/1?lang=eng&id=p15#p15), last week's reading). "A fiery stream issued and came forth from before him: thousand thousands ministered unto him, and ten thousand times ten thousand stood before him: the judgment was set, and the books were opened" (verse 10). John saw "ten thousand times ten thousand" around God's throne ([Revelation 5:11](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/rev/5?lang=eng&id=p11#p11)), and "the books were opened" when the dead were judged ([Revelation 20:12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/rev/20?lang=eng&id=p12#p12)). Malachi spoke of "a book of remembrance" kept for those who fear the Lord ([Malachi 3:16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/mal/3?lang=eng&id=p16#p16)).

Who is "the Ancient of days"? Daniel does not name him. Many Jewish and Christian readers have understood the title as a description of God himself. Revelation even uses similar language for the glorified Christ, whose "head and his hairs were white like wool, as white as snow" ([Revelation 1:14](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/rev/1?lang=eng&id=p14#p14)).

Restoration scripture gives Latter-day Saints a specific identification. The Lord spoke of "Michael, or Adam, the father of all, the prince of all, the ancient of days" ([Doctrine and Covenants 27:11](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/27?lang=eng&id=p11#p11)). He named a site in Missouri "Adam-ondi-Ahman, because ... it is the place where Adam shall come to visit his people, or the Ancient of Days shall sit, as spoken of by Daniel the prophet" ([Doctrine and Covenants 116:1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/116?lang=eng&id=p1#p1)). President Joseph F. Smith's vision of the spirit world calls "Father Adam, the Ancient of Days and father of all" ([Doctrine and Covenants 138:38](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/138?lang=eng&id=p38#p38)). The Bible Dictionary likewise states that latter-day revelation identifies the Ancient of Days as Adam ([Bible Dictionary, "Daniel, book of"](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bd/daniel-book-of?lang=eng)). This identification comes from modern revelation. It is not something Daniel states, and it is best presented that way.

**Question:** Daniel's vision moves from a churning sea to a calm heavenly court where books are opened. How does picturing that court change the way you see the frightening powers of the world?

### Daniel 7:11–12 — The End of the Beasts

"I beheld then because of the voice of the great words which the horn spake: I beheld even till the beast was slain, and his body destroyed, and given to the burning flame." The little horn's boasting is still sounding as the court acts. The fourth beast is destroyed completely. The Church's footnotes compare this with the end of the beast in [Revelation 19:20](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/rev/19?lang=eng&id=p20#p20).

"As concerning the rest of the beasts, they had their dominion taken away: yet their lives were prolonged for a season and time" (verse 12). The earlier kingdoms lose their power but are not immediately wiped out. History does not always end empires all at once; their peoples and influence may continue for a while without ruling. The point is that dominion is "taken away" by the court of heaven, and the timing is God's.

**Question:** The beast was still speaking "great words" while judgment was already being set. What does this teach about the real weight of arrogant words?

### Daniel 7:13–14 — "One Like the Son of Man"

"I saw in the night visions, and, behold, one like the Son of man came with the clouds of heaven, and came to the Ancient of days, and they brought him near before him. And there was given him dominion, and glory, and a kingdom, that all people, nations, and languages, should serve him: his dominion is an everlasting dominion, which shall not pass away, and his kingdom that which shall not be destroyed."

In Aramaic "son of man" means a human being. After four beasts from the sea, this figure is "like" a human, and he comes not from the sea but "with the clouds of heaven." In scripture clouds are the vehicle of God: the Lord "maketh the clouds his chariot" ([Psalm 104:3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ps/104?lang=eng&id=p3#p3)). So the figure is both humanlike and heavenly. The beasts seized power; to him dominion "was given." The formula "all people, nations, and languages," which Nebuchadnezzar and Darius used when they commanded the world (3:4; 4:1; 6:25), now describes those who will serve him. His kingdom is the one chapter 2 promised, "which shall never be destroyed" ([2:44](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/2?lang=eng&id=p44#p44)).

*Christ connection (explicit in scripture and Church teaching):* The Bible Dictionary says simply, "The 'Son of Man' is Jesus Christ." The official lesson notes that many Jews of Jesus's day linked this title with the coming Messiah ([official lesson](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-old-testament-2026/46?lang=eng)). Jesus used it of himself throughout the Gospels: "the Son of man came not to be ministered unto, but to minister" ([Mark 10:45](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/mark/10?lang=eng&id=p45#p45)); "they shall see the Son of man coming in the clouds of heaven" ([Matthew 24:30](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/24?lang=eng&id=p30#p30)). Restoration scripture explains the title's deeper meaning: "in the language of Adam, Man of Holiness is his name, and the name of his Only Begotten is the Son of Man" ([Moses 6:57](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/pgp/moses/6?lang=eng&id=p57#p57)). The Guide to the Scriptures adds that when Jesus called himself the Son of Man, he was openly declaring his relationship to the Father ([Guide to the Scriptures, "Son of Man"](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/gs/son-of-man?lang=eng)).

The most dramatic use came at Jesus's trial. When the high priest asked, "Art thou the Christ, the Son of the Blessed?" Jesus answered, "I am: and ye shall see the Son of man sitting on the right hand of power, and coming in the clouds of heaven." The high priest tore his clothes and called it blasphemy, and the council condemned him to death ([Mark 14:61–64](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/mark/14?lang=eng&id=p61-p64#p61); compare [Matthew 26:63–66](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/26?lang=eng&id=p63-p66#p63)). Hilton's slides build their conclusion around this connection between Daniel 7 and the trial, as did his earlier article on teaching Daniel ([Hilton, "Teaching the Book of Daniel"](https://johnhiltoniii.com/teaching-the-book-of-daniel/)). Jesus, bound and condemned, claimed to be the one to whom the everlasting kingdom would be given.

Stephen, as he was being stoned, saw "the Son of man standing on the right hand of God" ([Acts 7:56](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/acts/7?lang=eng&id=p56#p56)). John saw "one like unto the Son of man" among the candlesticks ([Revelation 1:13](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/rev/1?lang=eng&id=p13#p13)) and wrote, "Behold, he cometh with clouds" ([Revelation 1:7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/rev/1?lang=eng&id=p7#p7)).

Latter-day Saints also read this scene as a future event. Restoration scripture places the Ancient of Days at Adam-ondi-Ahman ([Doctrine and Covenants 116:1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/116?lang=eng&id=p1#p1)), and Chadwick's encyclopedia article describes an expected gathering there before the Savior's coming. In this reading, the Son of Man "came to the Ancient of days," not to be judged by him, but to receive the kingdom.

**Question:** Jesus claimed Daniel 7:13–14 while he stood bound before his accusers. What does it mean to you that the condemned prisoner is the one who receives the everlasting kingdom?

### Daniel 7:15–16 — "Asked Him the Truth of All This"

"I Daniel was grieved in my spirit in the midst of my body, and the visions of my head troubled me." The man who had calmly interpreted kings' dreams was shaken by his own. Revelation does not always bring immediate peace; it can first bring a weight.

So Daniel asked: "I came near unto one of them that stood by, and asked him the truth of all this. So he told me, and made me know the interpretation of the things" (verse 16). Daniel did not guess. He asked a heavenly attendant directly. Nephi did the same. When the Spirit asked him, "What desirest thou?" he answered, "To know the interpretation thereof" ([1 Nephi 11:10–11](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/1-ne/11?lang=eng&id=p10-p11#p10)).

**Question:** When a spiritual experience troubles or confuses you, how can you follow Daniel's example and come near to ask for understanding?

### Daniel 7:17–18 — "The Saints of the Most High Shall Take the Kingdom"

The interpreter began with a short summary: "These great beasts, which are four, are four kings, which shall arise out of the earth." The footnote explains "kings" as kingdoms. In verse 3 they rose from the sea; here "out of the earth." Either way, their origin is below, not above.

"But the saints of the most High shall take the kingdom, and possess the kingdom for ever, even for ever and ever" (verse 18). In verse 14 the kingdom was given to the Son of Man; here the saints receive it. The two belong together. The Son of Man's kingdom is shared with his holy people. In Daniel's setting "the saints of the most High" were the faithful of Israel. New Testament and Restoration writers apply the promise to all who belong to Christ: "Do ye not know that the saints shall judge the world?" ([1 Corinthians 6:2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/1-cor/6?lang=eng&id=p2#p2)).

**Question:** The saints "possess" the kingdom only because it is given to them. How does that shape the way covenant people should think about their role in God's kingdom?

### Daniel 7:19–20 — "Then I Would Know the Truth of the Fourth Beast"

Daniel's follow-up question focused on what troubled him most: "the fourth beast, which was diverse from all the others, exceeding dreadful, whose teeth were of iron, and his nails of brass; which devoured, brake in pieces, and stamped the residue with his feet." He mentions a new detail, "nails of brass," which joins the iron and bronze of chapter 2's statue.

He also asked about "the ten horns that were in his head, and of the other which came up, and before whom three fell; even of that horn that had eyes, and a mouth that spake very great things, whose look was more stout than his fellows" (verse 20). "More stout" means bolder or more arrogant in appearance. The little horn may have started small, but its look was larger than the rest.

**Question:** Daniel asked about the thing that frightened him most. Why is it often wise to bring our biggest fears directly to God rather than avoiding them?

### Daniel 7:21–22 — War Against the Saints, Until the Ancient of Days Came

"I beheld, and the same horn made war with the saints, and prevailed against them." The vision is honest: for a time, the saints lose. Revelation repeats this: "it was given unto him to make war with the saints, and to overcome them" ([Revelation 13:7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/rev/13?lang=eng&id=p7#p7)).

But the next verse begins with "Until": "Until the Ancient of days came, and judgment was given to the saints of the most High; and the time came that the saints possessed the kingdom" (verse 22). The Church's footnotes to the Ancient of Days here point again to [Doctrine and Covenants 27:11](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/27?lang=eng&id=p11#p11) and section 116. The defeat of the saints is real, but it is temporary. Judgment is "given" to them, which may mean both that they are vindicated and that they share in judgment.

**Question:** Where have you seen faithful people "prevailed against" for a season? How does the word "until" in verse 22 change the way you read those seasons?

### Daniel 7:23–25 — "A Time and Times and the Dividing of Time"

The interpreter explained: "The fourth beast shall be the fourth kingdom upon earth, which shall be diverse from all kingdoms, and shall devour the whole earth, and shall tread it down, and break it in pieces." The ten horns "are ten kings that shall arise: and another shall rise after them; and he shall be diverse from the first, and he shall subdue three kings" (verse 24).

Verse 25 describes this last ruler: "he shall speak great words against the most High, and shall wear out the saints of the most High, and think to change times and laws: and they shall be given into his hand until a time and times and the dividing of time." Three things stand out:

- **"Wear out the saints."** The threat is not only open violence but slow exhaustion. The Lord's counsel fits: "be not weary in well-doing, for ye are laying the foundation of a great work" ([Doctrine and Covenants 64:33](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/64?lang=eng&id=p33#p33)).
- **"Think to change times and laws."** Only God "changeth the times and the seasons" ([2:21](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/2?lang=eng&id=p21#p21)). The horn tries to take God's place, but the verb is "think." He only imagines he controls time.
- **"A time and times and the dividing of time."** This is often read as three and a half periods (one, two, and a half). Revelation uses the same phrase ([Revelation 12:14](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/rev/12?lang=eng&id=p14#p14)) and also "forty and two months" ([Revelation 13:5](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/rev/13?lang=eng&id=p5#p5)), which is three and a half years. The point is that the persecution is limited. It has a set length, and it ends.

Who is the little horn? Interpretations differ, and the guide does not need to choose one. *Scripture Helps* reports two common ways of reading the four beasts: Babylon, Medo-Persia, Greece, and Rome; or Babylon, Media, Persia, and Greece. On the second reading, the little horn is often identified with Antiochus IV, the persecuting king whom Hilton's slides connect with the "little horn" of Daniel 8. Chadwick's article, following the first reading, sees the ten horns as the kingdoms that followed Rome. Revelation takes up Daniel's imagery again for a still later conflict (Revelation 13). Jesus himself treated Daniel's prophecy of "the abomination of desolation" as having more than one fulfillment: once in the destruction of Jerusalem and "again" in the last days ([Joseph Smith—Matthew 1:12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/pgp/js-m/1?lang=eng&id=p12#p12), [32](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/pgp/js-m/1?lang=eng&id=p32#p32)). Hilton's class takes its title from that phrase. Whatever the identifications, the vision's own conclusion is plain: every beast loses its dominion, and the everlasting kingdom is given to the Son of Man and his saints (verses 14, 27).

**Question:** The persecution in verse 25 has a limit, "a time and times and the dividing of time." How can you endure a trial differently when you trust that God has set its limits?

### Daniel 7:26–27 — The Kingdom Given to the People of the Saints

"But the judgment shall sit, and they shall take away his dominion, to consume and to destroy it unto the end." The court of verses 9–10 does its work. The horn's power is removed permanently.

"And the kingdom and dominion, and the greatness of the kingdom under the whole heaven, shall be given to the people of the saints of the most High, whose kingdom is an everlasting kingdom, and all dominions shall serve and obey him" (verse 27). Everything the beasts grasped, "the greatness of the kingdom under the whole heaven," is now given to God's people. The final "him" can be read as referring to the Most High, whose kingdom the saints share. Revelation celebrates the same outcome: "The kingdoms of this world are become the kingdoms of our Lord, and of his Christ; and he shall reign for ever and ever" ([Revelation 11:15](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/rev/11?lang=eng&id=p15#p15)).

The official lesson suggests comparing Daniel 7:13–14 with the promises in [Doctrine and Covenants 121:29](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/121?lang=eng&id=p29#p29), [46](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/121?lang=eng&id=p46#p46). Joseph Smith received those promises while imprisoned in Liberty Jail: that "thrones and dominions" will be revealed to all "who have endured valiantly for the gospel of Jesus Christ," and that the faithful will receive "an everlasting dominion" that flows "without compulsory means." The beasts took dominion by force. The saints receive it as a gift.

**Question:** How does the promise of verse 27 help you face the wearing out of verse 25?

### Daniel 7:28 — "I Kept the Matter in My Heart"

"Hitherto is the end of the matter. As for me Daniel, my cogitations much troubled me, and my countenance changed in me: but I kept the matter in my heart." "Cogitations" means thoughts.

The chapter ends not with triumph but with a thoughtful, troubled prophet. Faces have changed throughout this book, Nebuchadnezzar's in rage (3:19), Belshazzar's in terror (5:6). Now Daniel's face changes too, from the weight of what he has seen. Yet he does not discard what he cannot fully understand. He keeps it. Luke uses similar language of Mary, who "kept all these things, and pondered them in her heart" ([Luke 2:19](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/luke/2?lang=eng&id=p19#p19)).

Daniel 1–7 begins with a young man who "purposed in his heart" (1:8) and ends with an old prophet who "kept the matter in [his] heart" (7:28). Between those two verses lies a lifetime of faithfulness.

**Question:** What spiritual truths are you keeping in your heart even though you do not yet understand them fully? How can you keep pondering them faithfully?

## Weekly Study and Discussion Plan

**Monday, November 9 — Daniel 1: Purposed in his heart.** Make two lists: what Daniel and his friends accepted in Babylon (names, school, service) and what they refused. Notice how Daniel asked (verses 8–13). Ponder: What have you already purposed in your heart? What would you like to add?

**Tuesday, November 10 — Daniel 2: Prayer, praise, and the stone.** Trace Daniel's steps in verses 14–23: a calm question, a request for time, prayer with friends, revelation, and praise. Draw the statue and the stone. Read [Doctrine and Covenants 65:2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/65?lang=eng&id=p2#p2) beside verses 44–45. Ponder: How can the "stone" grow through you without hands or force?

**Wednesday, November 11 — Daniel 3: But if not.** Count how often the chapter says "set up," and notice the machinery of pressure. Read verses 16–18 aloud, then read [Alma 14:8–13](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/14?lang=eng&id=p8-p13#p8). Write your own "My God is able ... but if not" sentence about a current concern. Read verse 29 carefully and talk about the difference between God's power and a king's methods.

**Thursday, November 12 — Daniel 4: Looking up.** Mark every "I," "my," and "mine" in verse 30. Then mark what happens in verse 34 when the king "lifted up [his] eyes." Reread verse 27 and choose one act of mercy to the poor for this week.

**Friday, November 13 — Daniel 5: Though thou knewest all this.** Compare Belshazzar with Nebuchadnezzar. Underline verse 22. Ask: What do I already know that I have not let humble me? Read [Moroni 10:32](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/moro/10?lang=eng&id=p32#p32) as a hopeful answer to "Thou art weighed in the balances, and art found wanting" ([Daniel 5:27](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/5?lang=eng&id=p27#p27)).

**Saturday, November 14 — Daniel 6: Windows open.** Look closely at every phrase in verse 10, especially "as he did aforetime." Compare it with [Mosiah 24:10–12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/mosiah/24?lang=eng&id=p10-p12#p10). Plan a consistent time and place for prayer. Read verse 24 thoughtfully and discuss why the scriptures sometimes report deeds they do not approve.

**Sunday, November 15 — Daniel 7: The Son of Man and the kingdom.** Draw the vision in two panels: the sea and the beasts, then the heavenly court and the Son of Man. Read verses 13–14, then [Mark 14:61–64](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/mark/14?lang=eng&id=p61-p64#p61). Ponder: What changes when you believe that the everlasting kingdom belongs to Jesus Christ and is shared with his saints?

**For a 45-minute class or family discussion:**

1. *Opening (5 minutes):* Read Daniel 1:8 and 7:28. Ask: How does a life that begins by purposing in the heart end by keeping God's word in the heart?
2. *Living in Babylon (10 minutes):* From chapter 1, list what the four young men accepted and what they refused. Discuss how to tell the difference in modern settings.
3. *Revelation and gratitude (8 minutes):* Read 2:17–23. Notice that Daniel prayed with friends and praised God before acting. Invite brief experiences of answered prayer, without pressuring anyone to share.
4. *But if not (10 minutes):* Read 3:16–18 and 6:10. Discuss faith that trusts God's power without dictating outcomes, and the role of long habits ("as he did aforetime").
5. *Pride and humility (7 minutes):* Put 4:30, 4:34, and 5:22 side by side. What is the difference between Nebuchadnezzar and Belshazzar?
6. *The Son of Man (5 minutes):* Read 7:13–14 and Mark 14:61–62. Close by testifying of Jesus Christ as the one to whom the everlasting kingdom is given.

**With children:** Retell chapters 1, 3, and 6 with simple props: a plate of vegetables and a cup of water, three paper figures plus a fourth in a paper "fire," and a cardboard window facing a picture of a temple. After each story, ask: "What did they choose? How did Heavenly Father help?" Build a tall tower of blocks and roll a small stone into its base to show the kingdom of God in Daniel 2, then talk about how the Church of Jesus Christ has grown. Choose a set time for a short family prayer each day this week, and when you pray, remember that Daniel prayed "as he did aforetime" ([Daniel 6:10](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/dan/6?lang=eng&id=p10#p10)).
