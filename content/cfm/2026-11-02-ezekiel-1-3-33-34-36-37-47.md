# A New Spirit Will I Put within You

This guide covers the *Come, Follow Me* reading for **November 2–8, 2026**: Ezekiel 1–3; 33–34; 36–37; 47 ([official lesson](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-old-testament-2026/45?lang=eng)). It is a personal Latter-day Saint study guide. It is not an official publication of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. All scripture quotations come from the King James Version and other standard works of the Church. The guide goes through all 218 assigned verses in order, in small groups. Each group gets an explanation, cross-references, labeled Christ connections where they fit, and a question for personal study or discussion.

## Historical and Literary Context

**A priest without a temple.** The book opens beside "the river of Chebar" in "the land of the Chaldeans" ([Ezekiel 1:1–3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/1?lang=eng&id=p1-p3#p1)). Ezekiel had been carried into Babylonia in the deportation that took King Jehoiachin, his household, his officials, and Judah's craftsmen and leading men ([2 Kings 24:10–16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/2-kgs/24?lang=eng&id=p10-p16#p10)). That deportation is conventionally dated to about 597 B.C. The Bible Dictionary identifies Ezekiel as a priest of the family of Zadok who settled at Tel Abib on the Chebar, and it dates his prophesying to about 592–570 B.C. ([Bible Dictionary, "Ezekiel"](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bd/ezekiel?lang=eng)). A priest's whole life was arranged around the sanctuary: its offerings, its calendar, and its lines between holy and common. Ezekiel was called far from that sanctuary, among displaced people who could still remember Zion but could not get to it: "By the rivers of Babylon, there we sat down, yea, we wept, when we remembered Zion" ([Psalm 137:1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ps/137?lang=eng&id=p1#p1)).

**Dates and their limits.** Ezekiel dates his visions with unusual care, usually counting from Jehoiachin's captivity. His call came in "the fifth year of king Jehoiachin's captivity" ([Ezekiel 1:2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/1?lang=eng&id=p2#p2)), around 593/592 B.C. Conversions to our calendar are approximate, and reference works differ by about a year: some give 592–570, others 593–571. Verse 1 also mentions "the thirtieth year," but the text does not say what that count measures, and the question is not settled. Other dated visions include the "sixth year" ([Ezekiel 8:1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/8?lang=eng&id=p1#p1)) and the "twelfth year of our captivity," when a fugitive arrives with news of Jerusalem's fall ([Ezekiel 33:21](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/33?lang=eng&id=p21#p21)). The temple vision is dated to the twenty-fifth year of exile, "in the fourteenth year after that the city was smitten" ([Ezekiel 40:1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/40?lang=eng&id=p1#p1)), about 573 B.C. Jerusalem itself fell about 587/586 B.C. ([2 Kings 25:1–10](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/2-kgs/25?lang=eng&id=p1-p10#p1)).

**Neighbors in prophecy.** While Ezekiel spoke among the exiles, Jeremiah was warning Jerusalem and Daniel was serving in the Babylonian court. Lehi left Jerusalem after prophesying in "the commencement of the first year of the reign of Zedekiah" ([1 Nephi 1:4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/1-ne/1?lang=eng&id=p4#p4)), a few years before Ezekiel's call. These prophets addressed different audiences in the same crisis, and reading them side by side shows how widely the Lord was working at that moment.

**The shape of the book.** The Bible Dictionary divides Ezekiel into four parts: judgment on Jerusalem (chapters 1–24), oracles against the nations (25–32), restoration (33–39), and the visionary temple (40–48). This week's selection covers the call (1–3), the turning point when news of Jerusalem's fall arrives (33), three chapters of promise (34, 36, 37), and the temple river (47). The chapters in between matter for context. In chapters 8–11 Ezekiel watches the Lord's glory leave the defiled temple. John Hilton III's class slides arrange this week's material as the glory departing ([Ezekiel 11:22–23](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/11?lang=eng&id=p22-p23#p22)), then returning to the visionary temple ([Ezekiel 43:4–5](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/43?lang=eng&id=p4-p5#p4)), then life-giving water flowing out of it ([Hilton, Class 44](https://johnhiltoniii.com/findingchrist/class-44-from-dry-bones-to-living-water-ezekiel-1-3-33-34-36-37-47/)). That arc helps the selected chapters read as one story.

**Recurring features worth tracking:**

- **"Likeness" and "appearance."** Ezekiel hedges almost everything he sees. He describes what the vision was *like* and does not claim to capture what it *is*.
- **"Son of man."** God addresses Ezekiel this way more than ninety times, stressing that he is human.
- **"The hand of the Lord."** This phrase marks moments when God's power overtakes the prophet (1:3; 3:14, 22; 33:22; 37:1).
- **"Ye shall know that I am the Lord."** This recognition formula closes many oracles. The point of judgment and of restoration alike is that Israel and the nations come to know who God is.
- **Quoting the people.** Ezekiel often repeats what the exiles are saying ("Thus ye speak," 33:10; "Yet the children of thy people say," 33:17; "they say, Our bones are dried," 37:11) and then answers them. The book is a conversation with grieving, defensive, and sometimes despairing people.
- **Sign-acts.** Ezekiel eats a scroll, is shut in his house, and joins two sticks. His own body and actions carry the message.

**Reading in layers.** This guide tries to keep four kinds of statement apart: what the ancient text says to its first audience; what official Latter-day Saint sources teach about it; what individual scholars propose; and devotional applications, including readings that see Jesus Christ in the passage. These layers can support each other, but they are not the same thing, and the guide labels them as it goes.

## Significant Insights

1. **God's glory is not confined to a place.** Ezekiel saw "visions of God" in Babylon, beside an irrigation channel, among captives ([Ezekiel 1:1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/1?lang=eng&id=p1#p1)). The throne he saw had wheels. For exiles who thought they were cut off from God along with the temple, this was the first and most needed message: the Lord can come to where his people are.

2. **Real revelation and honest limits go together.** Ezekiel piles up qualifiers ("the appearance of the likeness of the glory of the Lord," [1:28](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/1?lang=eng&id=p28#p28)). The text practices a humility readers should copy. Jonn Claybaugh's study aid for the Interpreter Foundation asks for similar humility. It cautions against confident speculation about Ezekiel's visionary temple: who will build it, where, how it will function, and when ([Claybaugh, Lesson 44](https://interpreterfoundation.org/cfm-study-aids-old-testament-2022-lesson-44/)).

3. **The prophet is mortal and stands only because the Spirit lifts him.** "Son of man, stand upon thy feet," God says, and then "the spirit entered into me ... and set me upon my feet" ([2:1–2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/2?lang=eng&id=p1-p2#p1)). The command and the enabling power arrive together. The same pattern returns when God gives Israel a new heart and puts his own spirit within them ([36:26–27](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/36?lang=eng&id=p26-p27#p26)), and again when the slain in the valley stand "upon their feet" ([37:10](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/37?lang=eng&id=p10#p10)).

4. **Responsibility is shared but not merged.** The watchman must warn; the hearer must choose. Neither can do the other's part ([3:17–21](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/3?lang=eng&id=p17-p21#p17); [33:1–9](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/33?lang=eng&id=p1-p9#p1)). This keeps disciples from two errors: indifference ("their choices are none of my business") and a savior complex ("their choices are my fault").

5. **God's justice follows the direction of a life, and he pleads for return.** Ezekiel 33 rejects a ledger view of righteousness. Old good deeds do not buy permission to sin, and old sins do not block a person who turns, makes restitution, and walks "in the statutes of life" ([33:15](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/33?lang=eng&id=p15#p15)). Behind it all is God's oath: "I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked" ([33:11](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/33?lang=eng&id=p11#p11)).

6. **Leadership is judged by what happens to the weak.** The shepherds of chapter 34 fed themselves, and the sheep were scattered. The Lord promises to search for his sheep himself. He also judges the strong sheep who trample the pasture and push the sick aside ([34:16–22](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/34?lang=eng&id=p16-p22#p16)). His care and his justice are one act.

7. **Restoration is God's initiative, and it is concrete.** God acts "for mine holy name's sake," not because Israel earned it ([36:22](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/36?lang=eng&id=p22#p22)). His gift of a new heart enables a living, obedient response. He promises tilled fields, rebuilt cities, a reunited people, and a sanctuary in their midst. These promises are not only images for private feelings.

8. **Life flows from God's presence.** The valley of bones lives by God's word and breath. The river from the sanctuary makes the Dead Sea's waters live and feeds trees whose leaves are "for medicine" ([47:12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/47?lang=eng&id=p12#p12)). The final chapter also gives inheritance to resident strangers and their children ([47:22–23](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/47?lang=eng&id=p22-p23#p22)), so the restored land has room for more than the native-born.

## Ezekiel 1: The Glory of the Lord Among the Exiles

Read the chapter: [Ezekiel 1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/1?lang=eng).

### Ezekiel 1:1–3 — Heavens Opened Among the Captives

The book starts with a double date. Verse 1 speaks in Ezekiel's own voice: "in the thirtieth year, in the fourth month, in the fifth day of the month, as I was among the captives." Verse 2 ties that day to "the fifth year of king Jehoiachin's captivity." Verse 3 then switches to third person: "The word of the Lord came expressly unto Ezekiel the priest, the son of Buzi." The shift may be a framing note added when the prophecies were collected, but the text doesn't explain it. Either way, the reader learns three things at once: who he is (a priest, son of Buzi), where he is ("the land of the Chaldeans by the river Chebar"), and what happened (the heavens opened, the word came, and "the hand of the Lord was there upon him").

Note where this happens: "among the captives." Ezekiel was not in a sanctuary when he saw God. "Expressly" stresses that the word truly and directly came. "The hand of the Lord" will recur at every major turn of the book, telling the reader that these experiences overtook Ezekiel and were not something he produced. Another prophet of the same years had a similar opening experience in Jerusalem, when Lehi "thought he saw God sitting upon his throne" ([1 Nephi 1:8](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/1-ne/1?lang=eng&id=p8#p8)).

**Question:** Ezekiel's call came in a place of loss, not in a holy building. Where have you found that God met you in circumstances you would not have chosen?

### Ezekiel 1:4 — A Storm Out of the North

Ezekiel first sees weather: "a whirlwind came out of the north, a great cloud, and a fire infolding itself." The fire seems to flash and fold back on itself, a brightness surrounds the cloud, and at the center glows something "as the colour of amber." The KJV word "amber" names a gleaming substance whose exact identity is uncertain. The impression is radiant metal or light inside the fire.

The north mattered to Judah. Invaders reached the land from the north, and Jeremiah had warned, "Out of the north an evil shall break forth" ([Jeremiah 1:14](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/1?lang=eng&id=p14#p14)). But storm, cloud, and fire are also the signs of God's coming at Sinai ([Exodus 19:16–18](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ex/19?lang=eng&id=p16-p18#p16)). From the direction the people associated with disaster, glory arrives.

**Question:** What changes when you notice that God's presence can come from the same direction as the trouble you feared?

### Ezekiel 1:5–8 — Four Living Creatures

Out of the fire come "the likeness of four living creatures." Overall "they had the likeness of a man," yet each had four faces and four wings. Their feet were "straight feet," their soles "like the sole of a calf's foot," and they "sparkled like the colour of burnished brass." Under their wings, on all four sides, were "the hands of a man."

The description holds two impressions together: these beings are somewhat like humans and plainly more than human. Hoof-like feet suggest sure footing, polished metal suggests brilliance, and hands suggest the ability to act. Later a cherub's hand takes fire from among the wheels ([Ezekiel 10:7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/10?lang=eng&id=p7#p7)). Ezekiel himself later identifies these creatures: "I knew that they were the cherubims" ([Ezekiel 10:20](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/10?lang=eng&id=p20#p20)). As a priest he knew cherubim as the figures over the ark and in the temple's most holy place ([Exodus 25:18–22](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ex/25?lang=eng&id=p18-p22#p18); [1 Kings 6:23–28](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/1-kgs/6?lang=eng&id=p23-p28#p23)). Now he sees the guardians of God's throne in Babylon. Interpreters should hold their symbolism loosely. Scripture Helps cautions that the Lord has not revealed the vision's full meaning and that it is unclear whether Doctrine and Covenants 77's explanation of John's beasts applies to Ezekiel's creatures ([Scripture Helps](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/scripture-helps-old-testament/43-ezekiel-1-3-33-34-36-37?lang=eng); compare [D&C 77:2–3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/77?lang=eng&id=p2-p3#p2)).

**Question:** How can you study a vision's concrete details carefully without forcing every detail into a single fixed meaning?

### Ezekiel 1:9–12 — Faces, Wings, and Straight Movement

Each creature's wings "were joined one to another." The creatures "turned not when they went; they went every one straight forward." Verse 10 names the faces: each of the four "had the face of a man"; the lion's face was "on the right side," the ox's face "on the left side," and each also "had the face of an eagle." Of each creature's wings, two stretched upward and touched the next creature's wings, and two "covered their bodies." Verse 12 gives the governing principle: "whither the spirit was to go, they went."

Four faces explain how the creatures could go any direction without turning: some face always pointed the way they were moving. Because their wings were joined, the four also moved as one unit, which prepares for the unity stressed in verses 19–21. Covering wings recall Isaiah's seraphim ([Isaiah 6:2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/6?lang=eng&id=p2#p2)), and John later sees four creatures who together bear the same four likenesses ([Revelation 4:7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/rev/4?lang=eng&id=p7#p7)). Noe Correa notes that Assyrian palace sculptures combining human faces, wings, and animal bodies were part of the visual world of Mesopotamia, where Ezekiel lived. He proposes that the creatures and wheels form a divine throne-chariot, with such powerful-looking beings serving at God's command and not leaving him ([Correa, *Old Testament Minute: Ezekiel*](https://scripturecentral.org/archive/books/book-chapter/ezekiel-13-3334-3637-47)). Taken as a scholar's proposal, it helps explain why the vision would speak to exiles in Babylon.

**Question:** The creatures move "straight forward" wherever the spirit goes. What would that kind of single-minded responsiveness look like in your discipleship this week?

### Ezekiel 1:13–14 — Coals, Lamps, and Lightning

The creatures look "like burning coals of fire, and like the appearance of lamps." Something moves "up and down among the living creatures," the fire is bright, and "out of the fire went forth lightning." The creatures themselves "ran and returned as the appearance of a flash of lightning."

The scene is full of motion and fire. Fire in scripture can purify, as when a seraph touches Isaiah's lips with a live coal ([Isaiah 6:6–7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/6?lang=eng&id=p6-p7#p6)), and it can judge. Later Ezekiel sees coals taken from between the cherubim and scattered over Jerusalem ([Ezekiel 10:2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/10?lang=eng&id=p2#p2)). The same holy fire that judges can cleanse. The lightning-speed movement says that God's servants do not drag their feet.

**Question:** When have you felt God's holiness as both a judgment on what is wrong in you and an invitation to be cleansed?

### Ezekiel 1:15–18 — Wheels Within Wheels, Full of Eyes

Beside each creature Ezekiel sees "one wheel upon the earth." The wheels look like "the colour of a beryl," a gemstone whose exact shade is uncertain, and "their appearance and their work was as it were a wheel in the middle of a wheel." They go "upon their four sides" without turning. Their rims ("rings") are "so high that they were dreadful," and "full of eyes round about them four."

Ezekiel describes appearance, not engineering. "A wheel in the middle of a wheel" fits his point that the wheels could move in any direction without turning, but readers should not turn this into a technical device. The wheels and the four-faced creatures share the same freedom of movement. In KJV English "dreadful" means awe-inspiring. The eyes are the most arresting detail: the throne of God sees in every direction. "For the eyes of the Lord run to and fro throughout the whole earth" ([2 Chronicles 16:9](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/2-chr/16?lang=eng&id=p9#p9); compare [Revelation 4:6–8](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/rev/4?lang=eng&id=p6-p8#p6)). Exiles who felt unseen are told that nothing is hidden from God. That is comfort and warning at once.

**Question:** Does the thought that God sees "round about" feel more like comfort or like warning to you right now? Why?

### Ezekiel 1:19–21 — One Spirit Moving Creatures and Wheels

Three verses stress how closely the wheels and creatures move together. When the creatures go, the wheels go. When the creatures rise "from the earth," the wheels rise. "Whithersoever the spirit was to go, they went." Twice the reason is given: "the spirit of the living creature was in the wheels." Verse 21 sums it up: "When those went, these went; and when those stood, these stood."

The repetition stresses unity. Creatures and wheels obey one directing spirit. Scripture elsewhere speaks of "the chariot of the cherubims" in the temple's design ([1 Chronicles 28:18](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/1-chr/28?lang=eng&id=p18#p18)), and here that idea comes to life. God's throne is mobile. In chapters 10–11 the same mobile throne will leave the temple and stop on the mountain east of the city ([Ezekiel 11:23](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/11?lang=eng&id=p23#p23)). A God who can leave a defiled sanctuary is also a God who can come to his scattered people.

**Question:** What would it mean for a family, quorum, or class to move "when those went, these went," united by one Spirit instead of pulled apart by competing wills?

### Ezekiel 1:22–25 — The Firmament and the Voice

Above the creatures' heads stretches "the likeness of the firmament," gleaming "as the colour of the terrible crystal." Under it their wings are spread straight, and each creature covers its body with its other two wings. When they move, the wings sound "like the noise of great waters, as the voice of the Almighty, the voice of speech, as the noise of an host." When they stop, "they let down their wings," and "there was a voice from the firmament that was over their heads."

The vision is built in layers: creatures and wheels below, a crystal platform above them, and something higher still. "Firmament" recalls the expanse of creation ([Genesis 1:6–8](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/gen/1?lang=eng&id=p6-p8#p6)). The comparisons for the sound escalate (waters, the Almighty, speech, an army), as if no single comparison will do. Then the wings drop, everything goes still, and a voice speaks. Stillness comes before the word. *Christ connection (Latter-day Saint reading):* When the Lord appeared in the Kirtland Temple, "his voice was as the sound of the rushing of great waters, even the voice of Jehovah" ([D&C 110:3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/110?lang=eng&id=p3#p3)). Latter-day Saints identify Jehovah as Jesus Christ, and John describes the glorified Christ's voice "as the sound of many waters" ([Revelation 1:15](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/rev/1?lang=eng&id=p15#p15)).

**Question:** The voice speaks when the wings are let down and all is still. What helps you become still enough to hear?

### Ezekiel 1:26–28 — The Likeness of the Glory of the Lord

Above the firmament is "the likeness of a throne, as the appearance of a sapphire stone," and on it "the likeness as the appearance of a man." From the waist up and the waist down Ezekiel sees amber-like brightness and fire. Around it all the brightness is "as the appearance of the bow that is in the cloud in the day of rain." Then he names what he has seen: "This was the appearance of the likeness of the glory of the Lord. And when I saw it, I fell upon my face, and I heard a voice of one that spake."

The careful wording ("appearance," "likeness") reaches its peak here. Ezekiel does not say he saw the Lord directly described; he saw the appearance of the likeness of the Lord's glory. The human form on the throne fits the creation of humankind in God's image ([Genesis 1:26–27](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/gen/1?lang=eng&id=p26-p27#p26)), and the sapphire echoes the elders' vision at Sinai of "a paved work of a sapphire stone" under God's feet ([Exodus 24:10](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ex/24?lang=eng&id=p10#p10)). The rainbow is especially tender. It recalls the covenant sign after the flood, "I do set my bow in the cloud" ([Genesis 9:13](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/gen/9?lang=eng&id=p13#p13)). Majesty is circled by a sign of mercy and covenant. Ezekiel falls on his face, as Isaiah cried "Woe is me!" ([Isaiah 6:5](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/6?lang=eng&id=p5#p5)) and Moses "fell unto the earth" ([Moses 1:9](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/pgp/moses/1?lang=eng&id=p9#p9)). *Christ connection (Christian reading):* Christians see in the enthroned figure in human form a pointer toward God revealed in Jesus Christ, whom John also saw in glory before falling "at his feet as dead" ([Revelation 1:13–17](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/rev/1?lang=eng&id=p13-p17#p13)).

**Question:** Why might it matter that the rainbow, a covenant sign of mercy, surrounds the throne of a God about to announce hard judgments?

## Ezekiel 2: A Son of Man Sent to a Rebellious House

Read the chapter: [Ezekiel 2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/2?lang=eng).

### Ezekiel 2:1–2 — Stand upon Thy Feet

The voice's first words to the fallen prophet are a command: "Son of man, stand upon thy feet, and I will speak unto thee." Ezekiel cannot get up alone. "The spirit entered into me when he spake unto me, and set me upon my feet, that I heard him that spake unto me."

"Son of man" will be Ezekiel's name through the whole book. Coming right after the blazing glory of chapter 1, the address sets the prophet's plain humanity beside God's majesty. But God does not leave him lying in the dust. The command to stand and the power to stand come together, and the reason for standing is to hear: the spirit set him on his feet "that I heard him that spake unto me." Paul's call follows a similar pattern: "rise, and stand upon thy feet" ([Acts 26:16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/acts/26?lang=eng&id=p16#p16)).

**Question:** Where in your life is God asking you to "stand," and how have you felt his Spirit give the strength you lacked?

### Ezekiel 2:3–5 — Sent to a Rebellious House

God sends Ezekiel "to the children of Israel, to a rebellious nation that hath rebelled against me: they and their fathers have transgressed against me, even unto this very day." They are "impudent children and stiffhearted": brazen-faced and unbending. His message is to start with the formula "Thus saith the Lord God." Whatever they do, "whether they will hear, or whether they will forbear, ... yet shall know that there hath been a prophet among them."

The audience is Ezekiel's own community: the exiled Israelites of his day, along with Judah's people back home. This is a covenant indictment of a particular generation and its history. It is not a verdict on all Jewish people in every age. The commission also defines success apart from results. Ezekiel succeeds if he delivers "Thus saith the Lord God," whether or not people listen. The authority sits in the Lord's word, not in the messenger's personality or office, which is why the text does not give any leader unlimited authority just because people resist him. The promise that they will "know" a prophet was among them returns in [33:33](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/33?lang=eng&id=p33#p33), tied to the moment "when this cometh to pass": events, not applause, confirm the prophetic word.

**Question:** How would your service change if you measured faithfulness by whether you delivered the Lord's message rather than by whether people responded?

### Ezekiel 2:6–7 — Briers, Thorns, and Scorpions

Three times the Lord says, "be not afraid." Ezekiel must not fear their words or "be dismayed at their looks," even "though briers and thorns be with thee, and thou dost dwell among scorpions." He must "speak my words unto them, whether they will hear, or whether they will forbear: for they are most rebellious."

The images describe what this ministry will feel like: scratches, snags, stings. They give Ezekiel no permission to treat people as vermin; they prepare him for pain. The repeated phrase about faces ("their looks") is telling. Disapproving expressions can silence a messenger as surely as threats. *Christ connection:* Jesus prepared his disciples in a similar way: "I send you forth as sheep in the midst of wolves" ([Matthew 10:16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/10?lang=eng&id=p16#p16)). God's servants are warned that the work may hurt, and they are not told to answer hurt with contempt.

**Question:** Whose "looks" tend to silence you when you should speak kindly but truthfully? How can you hold onto courage without turning hostile?

### Ezekiel 2:8–10 — Do Not Be Rebellious; the Scroll Spread Out

Before Ezekiel speaks to rebels, he is tested himself: "Be not thou rebellious like that rebellious house: open thy mouth, and eat that I give thee." A hand reaches out holding "a roll of a book." It is spread before him, "written within and without," and its contents are "lamentations, and mourning, and woe."

The first person who must obey is the prophet. The scroll is "written within and without," filled on both sides, which may suggest how full the message is. The contents are grief. Ezekiel is not being handed easy material. Compare the sealed book "written within and on the backside" in [Revelation 5:1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/rev/5?lang=eng&id=p1#p1).

**Question:** Why might God test a messenger's own obedience before sending him to call others to obey?

## Ezekiel 3: The Scroll, the Silence, and the Watchman

Read the chapter: [Ezekiel 3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/3?lang=eng).

### Ezekiel 3:1–3 — Sweet as Honey

The chapter break interrupts a continuous scene. "Eat this roll, and go speak unto the house of Israel." Ezekiel opens his mouth, and God "caused me to eat that roll." He is told to "cause thy belly to eat, and fill thy bowels with this roll." Then comes a surprise: "it was in my mouth as honey for sweetness."

The order of the command is "Eat this roll, and go speak": the message goes in before it goes out. Even the eating is God's act: "he caused me to eat that roll." The sweetness is not pleasure in the coming suffering, since the scroll is full of mourning. It is the sweetness of God's word itself, the satisfaction of receiving and obeying the Lord: "How sweet are thy words unto my taste! yea, sweeter than honey to my mouth!" ([Psalm 119:103](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ps/119?lang=eng&id=p103#p103); compare [Jeremiah 15:16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/15?lang=eng&id=p16#p16)). John's similar scroll was sweet in his mouth but bitter in his belly ([Revelation 10:10](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/rev/10?lang=eng&id=p10#p10)), and Ezekiel will soon feel "bitterness" too (verse 14). *Christ connection:* Jesus called himself "the bread of life" ([John 6:35](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/john/6?lang=eng&id=p35#p35)) and said his words "are spirit, and they are life" ([John 6:63](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/john/6?lang=eng&id=p63#p63)). God's word is meant to be taken in, not just held.

**Question:** What is the difference between knowing about a scripture and "filling your bowels" with it? What passage has become part of you that way?

### Ezekiel 3:4–7 — Not a People of Strange Speech

Ezekiel goes to the house of Israel, "not sent to a people of a strange speech and of an hard language." God adds a painful observation: "Surely, had I sent thee to them, they would have hearkened unto thee. But the house of Israel will not hearken unto thee; for they will not hearken unto me." All of them, he says, "are impudent and hardhearted."

The obstacle is not language. Ezekiel shares his audience's tongue, history, and covenant. Familiarity, though, can harden. Foreigners might have listened. God also reframes the rejection: they are refusing the Lord, not just Ezekiel, as he once told Samuel, "they have not rejected thee, but they have rejected me" ([1 Samuel 8:7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/1-sam/8?lang=eng&id=p7#p7)). That relieves the messenger of false guilt. *Christ connection:* Jesus made a similar contrast when he said the men of Nineveh, who repented at Jonah's preaching, would rise in judgment against his own generation ([Matthew 12:41](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/12?lang=eng&id=p41#p41)).

**Question:** Why is it sometimes harder to hear truth from people who share our background? How can we guard against that kind of hardness?

### Ezekiel 3:8–9 — A Forehead Harder than Flint

God answers the people's hardness with a hardness of his own making: "I have made thy face strong against their faces, and thy forehead strong against their foreheads. As an adamant harder than flint have I made thy forehead: fear them not."

The people are "impudent" (hard-faced) and "hardhearted." God does not make Ezekiel soft, so that he folds, or cruel, so that he matches their contempt. He makes him steady. The hard forehead is courage that does not flinch. *Christ connection:* Isaiah's servant says, "therefore have I set my face like a flint" ([Isaiah 50:7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/50?lang=eng&id=p7#p7)), and Luke reports that Jesus "steadfastly set his face to go to Jerusalem" ([Luke 9:51](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/luke/9?lang=eng&id=p51#p51)). Firmness in God's service can go together with deep compassion.

**Question:** What is the difference between a "strong forehead" and a hard heart? Which one are you more tempted toward in conflict?

### Ezekiel 3:10–11 — Receive in Thine Heart, Then Go

"All my words that I shall speak unto thee receive in thine heart, and hear with thine ears." Only then: "go, get thee to them of the captivity, unto the children of thy people," and say, "Thus saith the Lord God; whether they will hear, or whether they will forbear."

The order matters: heart and ears first, then feet and mouth. Note also the phrase "the children of thy people." These rebels are Ezekiel's own kin. He is not an outsider looking down. He is one of the exiles, sent to his family.

**Question:** Before you teach, counsel, or correct someone, what does it look like to "receive in thine heart" what God would have you say?

### Ezekiel 3:12–14 — Lifted Up in Bitterness

"The spirit took me up," and behind him Ezekiel hears "a voice of a great rushing, saying, Blessed be the glory of the Lord from his place." He hears the wings touching and the wheels rumbling as the vision moves off. Then he admits his state of mind: "I went in bitterness, in the heat of my spirit; but the hand of the Lord was strong upon me."

The scene closes with praise ("Blessed be the glory of the Lord"), but Ezekiel does not leave glowing. He leaves bitter and hot. The text does not tidy up his feelings. What carries him is not his mood but "the hand of the Lord," which "was strong upon me." Jeremiah described a similar inner fire: "his word was in mine heart as a burning fire shut up in my bones" ([Jeremiah 20:9](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/20?lang=eng&id=p9#p9)).

**Question:** How does Ezekiel's honesty about his feelings help you when obedience doesn't come with pleasant emotions?

### Ezekiel 3:15 — Sitting Where They Sat

Ezekiel arrives at "Tel-abib, that dwelt by the river of Chebar." There, he says, "I sat where they sat, and remained there astonished among them seven days."

Before he says a word as watchman, Ezekiel sits with the exiles. "Astonished" suggests he was stunned and overwhelmed. It does not give us a medical diagnosis. Seven days was a familiar span for mourning, and Job's friends "sat down with him upon the ground seven days and seven nights, and none spake a word unto him" ([Job 2:13](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/job/2?lang=eng&id=p13#p13)). Whatever else the week meant, it put Ezekiel in his people's place before he spoke to them. *Christ connection:* The Savior did not warn humanity from a distance. He came among us and is "touched with the feeling of our infirmities" ([Hebrews 4:15](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/heb/4?lang=eng&id=p15#p15)).

**Question:** Whom might you need to "sit where they sit" with before you have anything useful to say?

### Ezekiel 3:16–17 — Made a Watchman

"At the end of seven days" the word comes: "Son of man, I have made thee a watchman unto the house of Israel: therefore hear the word at my mouth, and give them warning from me."

A watchman stood on a wall or tower to spot danger early ([Isaiah 62:6](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/62?lang=eng&id=p6#p6)). The key words are "from me." Ezekiel doesn't invent the warnings; he hears them "at my mouth" and passes them on. The appointment also comes "at the end of seven days" spent sitting among the exiles, so the watchman is first a neighbor. The official lesson invites readers to consider what the watchman and shepherd images teach about leadership in homes, missionary work, and ministering ([Come, Follow Me](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-old-testament-2026/45?lang=eng)).

**Question:** In what relationships have you been given a "tower," some vantage point from which you can see dangers others might miss?

### Ezekiel 3:18–19 — Warning the Wicked

God lays out two cases. In the first, God has said to the wicked, "Thou shalt surely die," and the watchman stays silent. Then "the same wicked man shall die in his iniquity; but his blood will I require at thine hand." In the second, the watchman warns and the wicked man does not turn. He still "shall die in his iniquity; but thou hast delivered thy soul."

In both cases the wicked man dies "in his iniquity," his own. The watchman's silence does not make him innocent, and the watchman's warning does not force him to change. The watchman answers only for his own duty. "His blood will I require at thine hand" holds the watchman accountable for neglect. It does not transfer the sinner's guilt, and it does not teach that a careless friend or member literally causes another person's eternal condemnation. The context is covenant life and death for a people facing catastrophe, a setting the Lord later develops in [Ezekiel 18:20](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/18?lang=eng&id=p20#p20): "The soul that sinneth, it shall die."

**Question:** How does this passage free you both from indifference toward others and from carrying responsibility for choices that are not yours?

### Ezekiel 3:20–21 — When the Righteous Turn

The warnings are not only for the obviously wicked. "When a righteous man doth turn from his righteousness, and commit iniquity, and I lay a stumblingblock before him, he shall die." If he was not warned, "his righteousness which he hath done shall not be remembered; but his blood will I require at thine hand." But if the watchman warns him, "that the righteous sin not, and he doth not sin, he shall surely live, because he is warned."

The "stumblingblock" is hard to interpret. In context it seems to be the obstacle or consequence that exposes a choice already made, the point where a person's drift meets reality. It does not mean God traps the innocent. The positive case is lovely: a timely warning keeps a good person from falling. Prevention is part of the watchman's work too. "Let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall" ([1 Corinthians 10:12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/1-cor/10?lang=eng&id=p12#p12)).

**Question:** When has a gentle warning from someone kept you from a mistake? How might you offer that kind of warning without being heavy-handed?

### Ezekiel 3:22–23 — The Glory in the Plain

"The hand of the Lord was there upon me; and he said unto me, Arise, go forth into the plain, and I will there talk with thee." Ezekiel goes, and "behold, the glory of the Lord stood there, as the glory which I saw by the river of Chebar: and I fell on my face."

The glory comes again, and it is the same glory. What Ezekiel saw at the start was not a one-time burst. God meets him again in an ordinary plain, and again Ezekiel falls on his face. Each new assignment is grounded in a renewed sense of who is sending him.

**Question:** How does returning to your first spiritual experiences help you face new and harder assignments?

### Ezekiel 3:24–25 — Shut In and Bound

Once more "the spirit entered into me, and set me upon my feet." But the command now seems to contradict the earlier ones: "Go, shut thyself within thine house." He is told, "they shall put bands upon thee, and shall bind thee with them, and thou shalt not go out among them."

He was sent to speak, and now he is told to stay inside. The "bands" may be literal restraints put on by the people, or a symbolic way of describing how their opposition will confine him. The text does not say. Later in the book the elders come to *his* house to sit before him ([Ezekiel 8:1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/8?lang=eng&id=p1#p1); [14:1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/14?lang=eng&id=p1#p1)). The place of confinement becomes a place where people seek the word.

**Question:** Have you experienced a season when your service felt confined or restricted? How did the Lord still use that season?

### Ezekiel 3:26–27 — A Tongue That Cleaves, a Mouth the Lord Opens

"I will make thy tongue cleave to the roof of thy mouth, that thou shalt be dumb, and shalt not be to them a reprover." Yet: "when I speak with thee, I will open thy mouth, and thou shalt say unto them, Thus saith the Lord God; He that heareth, let him hear; and he that forbeareth, let him forbear."

This is one of the book's puzzles. Ezekiel speaks many oracles in chapters 4–24, so the "dumbness" was probably not unbroken total silence. A reasonable reading is that he could speak only when God gave him a word, and could not act as an ordinary "reprover," a mediator arguing with the people on his own initiative. The book later links the opening of his mouth to the arrival of the fugitive from Jerusalem ([24:25–27](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/24?lang=eng&id=p25-p27#p25); [33:22](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/33?lang=eng&id=p22#p22)). How long and how literal the silence was is not settled, and it is better to leave that open than to claim certainty. *Christ connection:* "He that heareth, let him hear" anticipates Jesus' repeated "He that hath ears to hear, let him hear" ([Matthew 11:15](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/11?lang=eng&id=p15#p15)), an invitation that respects the listener's agency.

**Question:** What might it mean for you to speak only when you have something from the Lord to say, and to stay quiet when you only have your own opinions?

## Ezekiel 33: The Watchman Renewed and the City Fallen

Read the chapter: [Ezekiel 33](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/33?lang=eng).

### Ezekiel 33:1–3 — The Watchman of the Land

The word comes "again." Ezekiel is to tell "the children of thy people" a civic parable. When God brings "the sword upon a land," the people "take a man of their coasts" (their territory) "and set him for their watchman." If he "seeth the sword come upon the land, he blow the trumpet, and warn the people."

Chapter 33 brings back the watchman image of chapter 3 at the hinge of the book, just before news of Jerusalem's fall arrives (verse 21). In the parable the people themselves choose and post the watchman, which assumes a shared stake in being warned. The sword is God's ("When I bring the sword"), a reminder that the coming disaster is covenant judgment, not chance.

**Question:** Why do you think the Lord restates the watchman commission at the turning point between judgment and restoration?

### Ezekiel 33:4–6 — Whose Blood Is on Whose Head

Whoever "heareth the sound of the trumpet, and taketh not warning" is responsible: "his blood shall be upon his own head." The point is repeated: "He heard the sound of the trumpet, and took not warning." But "he that taketh warning shall deliver his soul." If the watchman sees the sword and does not blow, the person taken "is taken away in his iniquity; but his blood will I require at the watchman's hand."

The idiom "his blood ... upon his own head" (compare [Joshua 2:19](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/josh/2?lang=eng&id=p19#p19)) puts responsibility where it belongs. Verse 6 is careful: even an unwarned person dies "in his iniquity." He is not counted innocent, yet the negligent watchman is accountable for his own failure. Both parties answer for their own part.

**Question:** What "trumpets" have you heard recently, and what would it look like to "take warning" in a concrete way?

### Ezekiel 33:7–9 — "So Thou … I Have Set Thee a Watchman"

The parable now applies to Ezekiel: "So thou, O son of man, I have set thee a watchman unto the house of Israel." The cases from chapter 3 come back almost word for word, with the urgent address "O wicked man, thou shalt surely die." Silence leaves blood on Ezekiel's hands; warning delivers his soul whether or not the hearer turns.

Why repeat it? The commission doesn't expire when the message changes. After Jerusalem falls, Ezekiel's message turns largely toward hope, and a watchman is still needed: people can refuse hope as easily as warning. *Latter-day Saint cross-reference:* In a modern parable the Lord describes a vineyard lost because "the watchman upon the tower would have seen the enemy while he was yet afar off" had the tower been built ([D&C 101:45–54](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/101?lang=eng&id=p45-p54#p45)).

**Question:** How can we keep listening to prophetic warnings even in seasons when the main message is comfort and hope?

### Ezekiel 33:10–11 — "How Should We Then Live?"

Ezekiel quotes the exiles: "If our transgressions and our sins be upon us, and we pine away in them, how should we then live?" God answers with an oath: "As I live, saith the Lord God, I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked; but that the wicked turn from his way and live: turn ye, turn ye from your evil ways; for why will ye die, O house of Israel?"

The tone has shifted. Earlier the people were defiant; now they are despairing. They believe their sins have sealed their fate and they are wasting away. God's reply is one of the most tender verses in the Old Testament. He swears by his own life. He doubles the imperative ("turn ye, turn ye") the way a parent repeats a child's name. And he asks a question that is really a plea: "why will ye die?" It restates [Ezekiel 18:23](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/18?lang=eng&id=p23#p23) and [Ezekiel 18:32](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/18?lang=eng&id=p32#p32). *Christ connection:* The Lord is "not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance" ([2 Peter 3:9](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/2-pet/3?lang=eng&id=p9#p9)). Jesus' father of the prodigal ran to meet his son ([Luke 15:20](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/luke/15?lang=eng&id=p20#p20)).

**Question:** If you or someone you love feels like they are "pining away" in past sins, how does God's oath in verse 11 answer that despair?

### Ezekiel 33:12–13 — Trusting in One's Own Righteousness

"The righteousness of the righteous shall not deliver him in the day of his transgression." Equally, "as for the wickedness of the wicked, he shall not fall thereby in the day that he turneth from his wickedness." If a righteous person "trust to his own righteousness, and commit iniquity, all his righteousnesses shall not be remembered."

God is not keeping a scorecard where old credits cover new debts. "Trust to his own righteousness" names a specific danger: leaning on one's record as though it made present sin safe. Verse 13 opens with God's own promise, "When I shall say to the righteous, that he shall surely live," and then shows the promise being misused: "if he trust to his own righteousness, and commit iniquity." In the sentence the trust comes before the iniquity. Confidence in a past record is what makes the new sin feel safe. Past faithfulness is real and good, but it is not a license. What matters is the present direction of a person's life and covenant relationship.

**Question:** What forms can "trusting to our own righteousness" take for long-time Church members, and how can we avoid it?

### Ezekiel 33:14–16 — Restoring the Pledge

When God tells the wicked, "Thou shalt surely die," and that person turns and does "that which is lawful and right," the turning is spelled out: "If the wicked restore the pledge, give again that he had robbed, walk in the statutes of life, without committing iniquity; he shall surely live, he shall not die." Then: "None of his sins that he hath committed shall be mentioned unto him."

Repentance here is practical. A "pledge" was collateral taken for a loan, sometimes a poor person's cloak, which the law required be returned before sundown ([Exodus 22:26–27](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ex/22?lang=eng&id=p26-p27#p26)). Turning means giving back what was wrongly held, returning what was stolen, and then walking in "the statutes of life," laws that lead to life. The result is complete: his sins will not be "mentioned unto him." The modern revelation agrees: "I, the Lord, remember them no more" ([D&C 58:42](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/58?lang=eng&id=p42#p42)). *Christ connection:* When Zacchaeus pledged to restore fourfold what he had taken, Jesus said, "This day is salvation come to this house" ([Luke 19:8–9](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/luke/19?lang=eng&id=p8-p9#p8)).

**Question:** Is there a "pledge" you need to restore, an apology, a repayment, or a correction, as part of turning?

### Ezekiel 33:17–20 — "The Way of the Lord Is Not Equal"

"Yet the children of thy people say, The way of the Lord is not equal." God turns the complaint around: "but as for them, their way is not equal." He repeats the principle (the righteous who turn to iniquity die; the wicked who turn to righteousness live) and closes: "O ye house of Israel, I will judge you every one after his ways."

"Not equal" means unfair or not level. The people object that a lifetime of goodness can be undone or a lifetime of evil forgiven. God's answer is that judging people by where they actually stand and walk is what fairness is. Earlier the exiles had complained that they suffered for their fathers' sins ([Ezekiel 18:2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/18?lang=eng&id=p2#p2)). Here God insists on individual justice: "every one after his ways." He offers no new argument. He restates the same two cases (verses 18–19) and turns the charge back on the accusers: "their way is not equal." The plural "ways" points to the path a person is walking, not a single isolated act.

**Question:** Why might it seem unfair that a person who repents late receives full mercy? What does that objection reveal about how we think of righteousness?

### Ezekiel 33:21–22 — "The City Is Smitten"

"In the twelfth year of our captivity, in the tenth month, in the fifth day of the month," a survivor arrives: "one that had escaped out of Jerusalem came unto me, saying, The city is smitten." The evening before, "the hand of the Lord was upon me," and by morning, when the man came, "my mouth was opened, and I was no more dumb."

This is the book's turning point. The judgment Ezekiel had announced for years has happened ([2 Kings 25:8–10](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/2-kgs/25?lang=eng&id=p8-p10#p8)). News traveled slowly, and the fugitive arrives well after the fall. The opening of Ezekiel's mouth fulfills [Ezekiel 24:27](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/24?lang=eng&id=p27#p27): "In that day shall thy mouth be opened to him which is escaped." Now that the worst has happened, the prophet speaks more freely, and much of what follows is restoration.

**Question:** How might a community's grief over a loss open hearts to a message of hope that was unwelcome before?

### Ezekiel 33:23–26 — "Abraham Was One"

The next oracle concerns "they that inhabit those wastes of the land of Israel," the survivors still in ruined Judah after the city fell. Their slogan is: "Abraham was one, and he inherited the land: but we are many; the land is given us for inheritance." God answers with charges: "Ye eat with the blood, and lift up your eyes toward your idols, and shed blood." "Ye stand upon your sword, ye work abomination, and ye defile every one his neighbour's wife." Twice he asks, "shall ye possess the land?"

These people were the remnant left in Judah, not the northern kingdom. They turned covenant history into a numbers argument: if one man inherited the land, surely many deserve it. But Abraham's inheritance came by God's call and Abraham's faithfulness: "I called him alone, and blessed him" ([Isaiah 51:2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/51?lang=eng&id=p2#p2)). Eating meat with the blood broke a basic covenant command ([Leviticus 17:10–14](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/lev/17?lang=eng&id=p10-p14#p10)), and to "stand upon your sword" is to rely on violence. Heritage does not excuse bloodshed, idolatry, or adultery. *Christ connection:* Jesus told those who appealed to their ancestry, "If ye were Abraham's children, ye would do the works of Abraham" ([John 8:39](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/john/8?lang=eng&id=p39#p39)).

**Question:** In what ways might people today rely on religious heritage or membership while ignoring the conduct that covenant requires?

### Ezekiel 33:27–29 — Wastes, Fields, Caves

God swears: those "in the wastes shall fall by the sword," those "in the open field" will be given "to the beasts to be devoured," and those "in the forts and in the caves shall die of the pestilence." "I will lay the land most desolate, and the pomp of her strength shall cease; and the mountains of Israel shall be desolate, that none shall pass through." "Then shall they know that I am the Lord."

Sword, beasts, and pestilence are the covenant curses of [Leviticus 26:22–25](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/lev/26?lang=eng&id=p22-p25#p22). Every hiding place (ruins, fields, fortresses, caves) fails. "The pomp of her strength," the land's pride and confidence, comes to an end. These verses describe God's covenant judgment on a particular people at a particular time. They are not a tool for diagnosing why someone today is suffering. Chapter 36 will speak to these same "mountains of Israel" with promises of renewal.

**Question:** What earthly sources of confidence ("the pomp of her strength") do you tend to rely on, and what happens when they fail?

### Ezekiel 33:30–31 — Talking by the Walls

Now the people talk about Ezekiel "by the walls and in the doors of the houses," saying to one another, "Come, I pray you, and hear what is the word that cometh forth from the Lord." They come "as the people cometh," sit "as my people," and "hear thy words, but they will not do them: for with their mouth they shew much love, but their heart goeth after their covetousness."

Once his warnings have come true, Ezekiel becomes popular. His home becomes a gathering place, and people invite their neighbors. But the gap between appearance and reality is wide. They sit like God's people and talk affectionately, but their hearts are on gain. Isaiah described the same thing: "this people draw near me with their mouth, and with their lips do honour me, but have removed their heart far from me" ([Isaiah 29:13](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/29?lang=eng&id=p13#p13)).

**Question:** How can we tell when attending, listening, and talking about God's word has become a substitute for doing it?

### Ezekiel 33:32–33 — A Very Lovely Song

"Lo, thou art unto them as a very lovely song of one that hath a pleasant voice, and can play well on an instrument: for they hear thy words, but they do them not." But "when this cometh to pass, (lo, it will come,) then shall they know that a prophet hath been among them."

The people enjoy Ezekiel's prophecy the way they would enjoy a fine performance. The problem is not music, art, or beauty. Scripture loves song. The problem is treating God's word as entertainment that asks nothing of us. Verse 33 echoes [Ezekiel 2:5](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/2?lang=eng&id=p5#p5): events will prove a prophet was among them. "Be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only" ([James 1:22](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/james/1?lang=eng&id=p22#p22)). *Christ connection:* Jesus ended his great sermon with the wise man who "heareth these sayings of mine, and doeth them" ([Matthew 7:24](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/7?lang=eng&id=p24#p24)).

**Question:** After a moving talk, lesson, or song, what is one practice that helps you turn admiration into action?

## Ezekiel 34: False Shepherds and the Lord Who Shepherds

Read the chapter: [Ezekiel 34](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/34?lang=eng).

### Ezekiel 34:1–3 — Shepherds Who Feed Themselves

"Woe be to the shepherds of Israel that do feed themselves! should not the shepherds feed the flocks?" The charges are specific: "Ye eat the fat, and ye clothe you with the wool, ye kill them that are fed: but ye feed not the flock."

In the ancient Near East "shepherd" was a common title for kings and rulers. God told David, "Thou shalt feed my people Israel" ([2 Samuel 5:2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/2-sam/5?lang=eng&id=p2#p2)), and Jeremiah had already pronounced woe on the shepherds who scattered the flock ([Jeremiah 23:1–4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/23?lang=eng&id=p1-p4#p1)). Shepherds could fairly use wool and meat. The sin is reversing the purpose: the flock existed for the shepherds' benefit, when shepherds exist for the flock.

**Question:** In what stewardships might any of us drift into "feeding ourselves," taking the benefits of a role while neglecting its purpose?

### Ezekiel 34:4 — Force and Cruelty

One verse lists five failures: "The diseased have ye not strengthened, neither have ye healed that which was sick, neither have ye bound up that which was broken, neither have ye brought again that which was driven away, neither have ye sought that which was lost." The summary is stark: "but with force and with cruelty have ye ruled them."

The list is precise because needs are precise. The weak need strengthening, the sick healing, the injured binding, the scattered retrieving, the lost searching. A shepherd who treats every sheep the same has not paid attention. The verse ends by naming what these rulers gave instead of care: "force" and "cruelty." Verse 16 will answer this list as the Lord takes up the neglected work himself.

**Question:** Think of the people you minister to or care for. Which of these five needs do you see, and which have you overlooked?

### Ezekiel 34:5–6 — Scattered with No Shepherd

"They were scattered, because there is no shepherd: and they became meat to all the beasts of the field." "My sheep wandered through all the mountains, and upon every high hill: yea, my flock was scattered upon all the face of the earth, and none did search or seek after them."

The text blames the scattering on the leaders' neglect, not on the sheep. That keeps readers from blaming victims. God calls the flock "my sheep" and "my flock." They belong to him even when their shepherds fail them. "Every high hill" may also hint at the hilltop shrines where Israel worshipped idols ([Ezekiel 6:13](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/6?lang=eng&id=p13#p13)), but the main picture is sheep wandering with no one looking for them. *Christ connection:* Jesus "was moved with compassion" on crowds "scattered abroad, as sheep having no shepherd" ([Matthew 9:36](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/9?lang=eng&id=p36#p36)).

**Question:** Why is it important that God calls them "my sheep" even when they are scattered and wandering?

### Ezekiel 34:7–10 — "I Am Against the Shepherds"

Twice: "Therefore, ye shepherds, hear the word of the Lord." God swears: because "my flock became a prey" and "the shepherds fed themselves, and fed not my flock," therefore "I am against the shepherds; and I will require my flock at their hand, and cause them to cease from feeding the flock." Then: "I will deliver my flock from their mouth, that they may not be meat for them."

The last phrase is chilling. The shepherds have become predators, and the sheep need rescue "from their mouth." "I will require my flock at their hand" echoes the watchman's accountability ("his blood will I require at thine hand"). God removes failed shepherds; he does not leave the flock to them. Accountability for leaders and protection for the vulnerable go together.

**Question:** What does this passage teach about how God views leaders who harm those they are supposed to protect?

### Ezekiel 34:11–12 — "I, Even I, Will Both Search My Sheep"

"Behold, I, even I, will both search my sheep, and seek them out." "As a shepherd seeketh out his flock in the day that he is among his sheep that are scattered; so will I seek out my sheep, and will deliver them out of all places where they have been scattered in the cloudy and dark day."

"I, even I" is emphatic. God does not appoint another committee; he goes himself. "The cloudy and dark day" is the day of disaster, which for Ezekiel's hearers meant Jerusalem's fall (compare [Joel 2:2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/joel/2?lang=eng&id=p2#p2)). The good shepherd is "among his sheep," not watching from a distance. *Christ connection:* "For the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost" ([Luke 19:10](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/luke/19?lang=eng&id=p10#p10)). In Jesus' parable the shepherd leaves the ninety and nine for the one ([Luke 15:4–7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/luke/15?lang=eng&id=p4-p7#p4)).

**Question:** When have you felt the Lord personally seek you out on a "cloudy and dark day"?

### Ezekiel 34:13–15 — Good Pasture and Rest

"I will bring them out from the people, and gather them from the countries, and will bring them to their own land, and feed them upon the mountains of Israel by the rivers." "I will feed them in a good pasture, and upon the high mountains of Israel shall their fold be: there shall they lie in a good fold, and in a fat pasture shall they feed." "I will feed my flock, and I will cause them to lie down."

The promise is both a gathering and a real return to "their own land," to "the mountains of Israel" that chapter 36 will address. The word "feed" appears four times in these three verses, answering the shepherds who "feed not the flock" (verse 3). The goal is not only rescue but rest: "I will cause them to lie down." "He maketh me to lie down in green pastures" ([Psalm 23:2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ps/23?lang=eng&id=p2#p2)).

**Question:** The Lord promises not only to feed his flock but to "cause them to lie down." What keeps you from resting in his care, and what would help you receive that rest?

### Ezekiel 34:16 — Seeking the Lost, Destroying the Fat and Strong

"I will seek that which was lost, and bring again that which was driven away, and will bind up that which was broken, and will strengthen that which was sick." This reverses verse 4 point by point. Then: "but I will destroy the fat and the strong; I will feed them with judgment."

God does personally everything the false shepherds failed to do. The KJV's "destroy the fat and the strong" should not be softened or skipped. Verses 20–21 explain who the fat and strong are: sheep who push the weak aside with shoulder and horn. To "feed them with judgment" is to deal with them justly. God's tenderness toward the broken and his firmness toward their oppressors are the same love.

**Question:** Why can't true shepherding offer healing to the weak without also dealing justly with those who harm them?

### Ezekiel 34:17–19 — Trampled Pasture, Fouled Water

"And as for you, O my flock ... Behold, I judge between cattle and cattle, between the rams and the he goats." "Seemeth it a small thing unto you to have eaten up the good pasture, but ye must tread down with your feet the residue of your pastures? and to have drunk of the deep waters, but ye must foul the residue with your feet?" The rest of the flock must "eat that which ye have trodden" and "drink that which ye have fouled."

Judgment now moves inside the flock. In KJV English "cattle" often means livestock in general, and here the context is sheep and goats. Rams and he-goats are all ritually clean animals, so the issue is not purity. It is how the strong behave. They take the best and then ruin what is left so no one else can use it. *Christ connection (with care):* Jesus also spoke of separating sheep from goats ([Matthew 25:32–33](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/25?lang=eng&id=p32-p33#p32)). In Ezekiel the line runs between oppressors and the oppressed within one flock.

**Question:** Are there ways, in families, wards, workplaces, or communities, that the strong can "foul the water" for others without meaning to?

### Ezekiel 34:20–22 — Fat Cattle and Lean

"Behold, I, even I, will judge between the fat cattle and between the lean cattle. Because ye have thrust with side and with shoulder, and pushed all the diseased with your horns, till ye have scattered them abroad; therefore will I save my flock, and they shall no more be a prey."

The picture is of bullying: shoulders shoving, horns butting the sick until they scatter. Some of the scattering in verse 6 was caused by fellow sheep, not only by absent shepherds. God takes the side of the lean and diseased and promises to "save my flock." Salvation here includes protection from those within the community who prey on the weak.

**Question:** How can disciples notice and stop the quiet "shoving" that pushes vulnerable people to the edges of a community?

### Ezekiel 34:23–24 — One Shepherd, My Servant David

"I will set up one shepherd over them, and he shall feed them, even my servant David; he shall feed them, and he shall be their shepherd. And I the Lord will be their God, and my servant David a prince among them."

David had died centuries before Ezekiel. The promise picks up God's covenant with David's house ([2 Samuel 7:12–16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/2-sam/7?lang=eng&id=p12-p16#p12)) and other prophets' hope for a coming Davidic ruler ([Jeremiah 23:5](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/23?lang=eng&id=p5#p5); [Hosea 3:5](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/hosea/3?lang=eng&id=p5#p5)). Many failed shepherds give way to "one shepherd." Scripture Helps identifies this promised David as Jesus Christ, David's descendant. *Christ connection:* "I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep" ([John 10:11](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/john/10?lang=eng&id=p11#p11)), and "there shall be one fold, and one shepherd" ([John 10:16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/john/10?lang=eng&id=p16#p16)). Christians see in Jesus both the Lord who seeks his sheep (verse 11) and the Davidic shepherd he appoints.

**Question:** How does knowing Jesus as the "one shepherd" shape the way you serve those entrusted to you?

### Ezekiel 34:25–26 — Covenant of Peace and Showers of Blessing

"I will make with them a covenant of peace, and will cause the evil beasts to cease out of the land: and they shall dwell safely in the wilderness, and sleep in the woods." "I will make them and the places round about my hill a blessing; and I will cause the shower to come down in his season; there shall be showers of blessing."

Sheep that could once be devoured (verse 5) now sleep safely even in the woods. "My hill" points to the Lord's holy mountain, the place of his presence. Rain "in his season" was life itself in an arid land, echoing the covenant blessings of [Leviticus 26:4–6](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/lev/26?lang=eng&id=p4-p6#p4). Compare the "covenant of my peace" in [Isaiah 54:10](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/54?lang=eng&id=p10#p10). This is a covenant promise to a restored people. It does not guarantee that every faithful person will be safe or prosperous right away.

**Question:** What "showers of blessing" have come to you "in his season," maybe not when you wanted them but when they could nourish growth?

### Ezekiel 34:27–28 — Broken Bands of the Yoke

"The tree of the field shall yield her fruit, and the earth shall yield her increase, and they shall be safe in their land, and shall know that I am the Lord, when I have broken the bands of their yoke, and delivered them out of the hand of those that served themselves of them." "They shall no more be a prey to the heathen ... but they shall dwell safely, and none shall make them afraid."

"Broken the bands of their yoke" echoes the Exodus: "I have broken the bands of your yoke, and made you go upright" ([Leviticus 26:13](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/lev/26?lang=eng&id=p13#p13)). Restoration is a new exodus. "Those that served themselves of them" were exploiters who used Israel as slaves. "None shall make them afraid" appears again in [Micah 4:4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/micah/4?lang=eng&id=p4#p4). *Christ connection:* Jesus offers a different yoke: "For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light" ([Matthew 11:30](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/11?lang=eng&id=p30#p30)).

**Question:** What "bands of a yoke" has the Lord broken in your life, or might he be offering to break now?

### Ezekiel 34:29–31 — A Plant of Renown; "Ye … Are Men"

"I will raise up for them a plant of renown, and they shall be no more consumed with hunger in the land, neither bear the shame of the heathen any more." "Thus shall they know that I the Lord their God am with them." Then the chapter explains its own metaphor: "And ye my flock, the flock of my pasture, are men, and I am your God."

"A plant of renown" is uncertain. In context it most naturally means a famous, abundant planting that ends hunger. Some Christian readers connect it with the messianic "Branch" ([Isaiah 11:1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/11?lang=eng&id=p1#p1); [Jeremiah 23:5](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/23?lang=eng&id=p5#p5)). That is a possible devotional reading, not a settled identification. Verse 31 is plain: the flock is people, real men and women whom God claims as his own. The whole chapter is about how actual human beings are treated.

**Question:** How does verse 31 ("ye ... are men, and I am your God") keep this chapter from being only a pleasant pastoral picture?

## Ezekiel 36: Mountains Restored and a New Heart

Read the chapter: [Ezekiel 36](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/36?lang=eng).

### Ezekiel 36:1–3 — Mountains of Israel, Hear

"Prophesy unto the mountains of Israel, and say, Ye mountains of Israel, hear the word of the Lord." The enemy has gloated, "Aha, even the ancient high places are ours in possession." Because "they have made you desolate, and swallowed you up on every side," the mountains have become a possession of other nations, "taken up in the lips of talkers," and "an infamy of the people."

Earlier Ezekiel prophesied *against* these mountains for their idolatry ([Ezekiel 6:2–3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/6?lang=eng&id=p2-p3#p2)). Now he speaks *to* them with consolation. The land is addressed as if it could hear, but it is still real land: hills, fields, and ruined towns. "Aha" is the sound of mockery, and the land has become gossip on everyone's lips.

**Question:** What changes when God speaks words of comfort to the very place he once spoke against?

### Ezekiel 36:4–5 — Idumea and the Residue of the Heathen

God addresses "the mountains, and ... the hills, to the rivers, and to the valleys, to the desolate wastes, and to the cities that are forsaken," which "became a prey and derision." "In the fire of my jealousy have I spoken against the residue of the heathen, and against all Idumea, which have appointed my land into their possession with the joy of all their heart, with despiteful minds."

The list of landforms is thorough: no part of the land is forgotten. Idumea is Edom, Judah's neighbor to the south and southeast, condemned in the previous chapter (Ezekiel 35) and in Obadiah for exploiting Judah's disaster ([Obadiah 1:10–14](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/obad/1?lang=eng&id=p10-p14#p10); [Psalm 137:7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ps/137?lang=eng&id=p7#p7)). The charge is specific: they seized the land "with the joy of all their heart" and "despiteful minds." This is an ancient grievance between ancient peoples. It provides no basis for hatred of any modern group and no guide to modern borders. "Jealousy" here means God's protective zeal for his covenant people.

**Question:** Why might God care not only that Edom took advantage of Judah but that it did so "with the joy of all their heart"?

### Ezekiel 36:6–7 — "I Have Lifted Up Mine Hand"

"I have spoken in my jealousy and in my fury, because ye have borne the shame of the heathen: Therefore thus saith the Lord God; I have lifted up mine hand, Surely the heathen that are about you, they shall bear their shame."

To lift up one's hand is to swear an oath: "For I lift up my hand to heaven, and say, I live for ever" ([Deuteronomy 32:40](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/deut/32?lang=eng&id=p40#p40)). The same gesture comes back in the land promise of [Ezekiel 47:14](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/47?lang=eng&id=p14#p14). The shame Israel carried will be lifted and placed on those who mocked. God's honor and his people's dignity are tied together.

**Question:** How does it feel to know that God takes personally the shame his people have suffered?

### Ezekiel 36:8–11 — Better than at Your Beginnings

"But ye, O mountains of Israel, ye shall shoot forth your branches, and yield your fruit to my people of Israel; for they are at hand to come." "For, behold, I am for you, and I will turn unto you, and ye shall be tilled and sown." God will "multiply men upon you, all the house of Israel, even all of it," cities will be inhabited, wastes rebuilt, man and beast increase, and "I will settle you after your old estates, and will do better unto you than at your beginnings."

"I am for you" reverses earlier oracles where God said, "I ... am against thee" ([Ezekiel 5:8](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/5?lang=eng&id=p8#p8)). The trees put out branches before the exiles return, as if the land is getting ready for them. "All the house of Israel, even all of it" includes north and south. The most striking line goes beyond restoring what was lost: God will do "better ... than at your beginnings."

**Question:** Where might the Lord intend not just to repair what was lost in your life but to do "better ... than at your beginnings"?

### Ezekiel 36:12–15 — A Land That Devours No More

"I will cause men to walk upon you, even my people Israel; and they shall possess thee, and thou shalt be their inheritance, and thou shalt no more henceforth bereave them of men." The nations have said, "Thou land devourest up men, and hast bereaved thy nations." Now: "thou shalt devour men no more," nor "hear in thee the shame of the heathen," nor "cause thy nations to fall any more."

The accusation that the land "devourest up men" goes back to Israel's beginnings, when the frightened spies called Canaan "a land that eateth up the inhabitants thereof" ([Numbers 13:32](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/num/13?lang=eng&id=p32#p32)). After war, famine, and exile, neighbors could say the land was cursed. God reverses the accusation. The land will become a place of life, and the stigma will be removed.

**Question:** What labels or accusations ("this place devours people," "this family always fails") might God want to lift from your situation?

### Ezekiel 36:16–19 — Defiled Land, Scattered People

A new oracle: "when the house of Israel dwelt in their own land, they defiled it by their own way and by their doings: their way was before me as the uncleanness of a removed woman." So "I poured my fury upon them for the blood that they had shed upon the land, and for their idols wherewith they had polluted it." "I scattered them among the heathen ... according to their way and according to their doings I judged them."

The simile draws on the law's ritual-purity categories, in which a woman during menstruation was in a temporary state of ritual impurity ([Leviticus 15:19](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/lev/15?lang=eng&id=p19#p19)). That condition was natural and was not a sin. The comparison says nothing about women's moral worth. It is borrowed to describe Israel's *defiling* of the land, and verse 18 names the actual offenses: bloodshed and idolatry. Exile followed "according to their doings."

**Question:** Verse 18 names bloodshed and idolatry as what defiled the land. Why do you think violence and false worship are so often linked in scripture?

### Ezekiel 36:20–21 — A Holy Name Profaned

"When they entered unto the heathen, whither they went, they profaned my holy name, when they said to them, These are the people of the Lord, and are gone forth out of his land. But I had pity for mine holy name, which the house of Israel had profaned among the heathen."

The nations looked at the exiles and drew a conclusion: their God could not keep them in his own land. Israel's scattering made the Lord look weak or faithless. The name was profaned by the situation and by Israel's continued conduct. "Pity for mine holy name" means God cares about his reputation, and not out of vanity. His name stands for who he truly is, and the nations' false conclusion keeps them from knowing him. *Christ connection:* Jesus taught his disciples to pray first, "Hallowed be thy name" ([Matthew 6:9](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/6?lang=eng&id=p9#p9)).

**Question:** How do the lives of people who take Christ's name affect how others see him?

### Ezekiel 36:22–24 — For Mine Holy Name's Sake

"I do not this for your sakes, O house of Israel, but for mine holy name's sake, which ye have profaned among the heathen." "I will sanctify my great name ... and the heathen shall know that I am the Lord ... when I shall be sanctified in you before their eyes." Then: "I will take you from among the heathen, and gather you out of all countries, and will bring you into your own land."

This is grace without flattery. God does not restore Israel because they earned it; he acts because of who he is. That is good news, because the restoration doesn't depend on merit. His holiness will be shown "in you": the restored people themselves will be the evidence the nations see.

**Question:** Why might it be comforting, not insulting, that God restores his people "not for your sakes" but for his own name's sake?

### Ezekiel 36:25 — Clean Water

"Then will I sprinkle clean water upon you, and ye shall be clean: from all your filthiness, and from all your idols, will I cleanse you."

This is priestly language. The law used sprinkled water to purify people and objects defiled by contact with death ([Numbers 19:17–19](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/num/19?lang=eng&id=p17-p19#p17)), and David prayed, "Purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean" ([Psalm 51:7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ps/51?lang=eng&id=p7#p7)). The actor is God: "I will sprinkle," "I will cleanse." The cleansing also covers idols. God removes the impurity and the false loyalties that caused it. *Latter-day Saint application:* Readers naturally think of baptism and covenant renewal. Those are applications of the verse, not a claim that it describes one ordinance alone.

**Question:** What "idols," competing loyalties or habits, would you ask the Lord to wash away?

### Ezekiel 36:26–27 — A New Heart and a New Spirit

"A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh. And I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes, and ye shall keep my judgments, and do them."

In the Old Testament the heart is often where understanding and choice live; Solomon asked for "an understanding heart" ([1 Kings 3:9](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/1-kgs/3?lang=eng&id=p9#p9)). A "stony heart" does not respond. Zechariah described people who "made their hearts as an adamant stone" ([Zechariah 7:12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/zech/7?lang=eng&id=p12#p12)). "Flesh" here does not mean the carnal nature, as it sometimes does in Paul. It means living tissue that feels and responds. Earlier Ezekiel told the people to "make you a new heart and a new spirit" ([Ezekiel 18:31](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/18?lang=eng&id=p31#p31)). Here God gives what he commanded, and his spirit produces real obedience: "ye shall keep my judgments, and do them." Grace makes the response possible without replacing it. Compare the "mighty change ... in our hearts" in [Mosiah 5:2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/mosiah/5?lang=eng&id=p2#p2), "fleshy tables of the heart" in [2 Corinthians 3:3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/2-cor/3?lang=eng&id=p3#p3), and the new covenant written on the heart in [Jeremiah 31:33](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/31?lang=eng&id=p33#p33). *Christ connection:* Jesus taught that one must be "born of water and of the Spirit" ([John 3:5](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/john/3?lang=eng&id=p5#p5)). Water and spirit appear together here too.

**Question:** What is one sign that your heart is becoming more "flesh," more responsive to God and to others, than it was before?

### Ezekiel 36:28–30 — My People, Your God; No Famine

"Ye shall dwell in the land that I gave to your fathers; and ye shall be my people, and I will be your God." "I will also save you from all your uncleannesses: and I will call for the corn, and will increase it, and lay no famine upon you. And I will multiply the fruit of the tree, and the increase of the field, that ye shall receive no more reproach of famine among the heathen."

"Ye shall be my people, and I will be your God" is the heart of the covenant ([Exodus 6:7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ex/6?lang=eng&id=p7#p7)). The spiritual renewal of verses 25–27 comes with a renewed life on the land. God "calls for" grain the way a master calls a servant, and famine, which once shamed Israel among the nations, ends. These are promises to a restored covenant people, not a guarantee of harvests to any one person.

**Question:** Why do you think Ezekiel keeps spiritual renewal and the restoration of ordinary life (food, fields, homes) so closely tied together?

### Ezekiel 36:31–32 — Remembering and Being Ashamed

"Then shall ye remember your own evil ways, and your doings that were not good, and shall lothe yourselves in your own sight for your iniquities and for your abominations. Not for your sakes do I this, saith the Lord God, be it known unto you: be ashamed and confounded for your own ways, O house of Israel."

The order is surprising. Honest shame comes *after* God's cleansing and gifts. It is not the price Israel pays to earn them. Grace brings clear sight, and clear sight brings sorrow over what was done. This is not a call to lasting self-hatred. It is the honest remembering that keeps a forgiven people from going back. Paul called it "godly sorrow" that "worketh repentance to salvation" ([2 Corinthians 7:10](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/2-cor/7?lang=eng&id=p10#p10)).

**Question:** How is the honest remembrance that follows forgiveness different from the shame that keeps people from coming to God in the first place?

### Ezekiel 36:33–36 — Like the Garden of Eden

"In the day that I shall have cleansed you from all your iniquities I will also cause you to dwell in the cities, and the wastes shall be builded." The desolate land "shall be tilled, whereas it lay desolate in the sight of all that passed by." Passersby will say, "This land that was desolate is become like the garden of Eden." The nations "shall know that I the Lord build the ruined places, and plant that that was desolate: I the Lord have spoken it, and I will do it."

Cleansing comes first, then rebuilding. The same travelers who once saw the ruins become witnesses of renewal. "Like the garden of Eden" echoes Isaiah's promise that the Lord "will make her wilderness like Eden" ([Isaiah 51:3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/51?lang=eng&id=p3#p3)). The closing line, "I the Lord have spoken it, and I will do it," rests the whole promise on God's faithfulness.

**Question:** What "ruined places" in your life or community could become, through the Lord's work, a witness that he builds and plants?

### Ezekiel 36:37–38 — Inquired Of; Flocks of Men

"I will yet for this be inquired of by the house of Israel, to do it for them; I will increase them with men like a flock. As the holy flock, as the flock of Jerusalem in her solemn feasts; so shall the waste cities be filled with flocks of men."

Earlier God refused to be "enquired of" by idolatrous elders ([Ezekiel 14:3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/14?lang=eng&id=p3#p3); [20:3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/20?lang=eng&id=p3#p3)). Now he invites prayer, even for what he has already promised. A promised blessing still comes through relationship. The picture of crowds is festive: flocks driven to Jerusalem for sacrifice at the holy feasts become an image of people filling the once-empty towns. This promises restored community, not a formula for getting what we want.

**Question:** Why would God want to be "inquired of" for blessings he has already promised?

## Ezekiel 37: Breath for Dry Bones, One Stick in the Hand

Read the chapter: [Ezekiel 37](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/37?lang=eng).

### Ezekiel 37:1–3 — "Can These Bones Live?"

"The hand of the Lord was upon me, and carried me out in the spirit of the Lord, and set me down in the midst of the valley which was full of bones." God "caused me to pass by them round about": "very many in the open valley; and, lo, they were very dry." Then the question: "Son of man, can these bones live?" Ezekiel answers, "O Lord God, thou knowest."

For a priest this scene was horrifying. Touching "a bone of a man" brought ritual impurity ([Numbers 19:16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/num/19?lang=eng&id=p16#p16)), and unburied bones meant disgrace and defeat. God makes him walk among them and take in how many there are and how dry. "Very dry" means long dead, past any human hope. Ezekiel's answer is neither doubt nor bravado. He hands the question back to the only one who can answer it.

**Question:** When you face something that looks beyond hope, what would it mean to answer honestly, "O Lord God, thou knowest"?

### Ezekiel 37:4–6 — "O Ye Dry Bones, Hear"

"Prophesy upon these bones, and say unto them, O ye dry bones, hear the word of the Lord." God promises: "I will cause breath to enter into you, and ye shall live: And I will lay sinews upon you, and will bring up flesh upon you, and cover you with skin, and put breath in you, and ye shall live; and ye shall know that I am the Lord."

Preaching to bones seems absurd, which is the point. God's word gives life to what cannot respond on its own, as it called creation into being. The promised sequence (sinews, flesh, skin, breath) runs decay backward. The goal, as so often in Ezekiel, is knowledge of God: "ye shall know that I am the Lord."

**Question:** Is there a situation where you have stopped "prophesying," stopped bringing God's word, because it seemed pointless?

### Ezekiel 37:7–8 — Bone to His Bone, but No Breath

"So I prophesied as I was commanded: and as I prophesied, there was a noise, and behold a shaking, and the bones came together, bone to his bone." Then "the sinews and the flesh came up upon them, and the skin covered them above: but there was no breath in them."

The miracle comes in two stages. First structure: rattling, shaking, bones joining. The result is a valley of intact corpses, complete in form and still dead. The pause is instructive. In creation God formed man and *then* breathed into him ([Genesis 2:7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/gen/2?lang=eng&id=p7#p7)). Restored structure without God's breath is not yet life. *Latter-day Saint cross-reference:* Alma teaches that in the resurrection "every limb and joint shall be restored to its body" ([Alma 40:23](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/40?lang=eng&id=p23#p23)).

**Question:** Where might you or your community have the "bones and sinews" of faith, the forms and structures, but still need breath?

### Ezekiel 37:9–10 — Breath from the Four Winds

"Prophesy unto the wind, prophesy, son of man, and say to the wind, Thus saith the Lord God; Come from the four winds, O breath, and breathe upon these slain, that they may live." "So I prophesied as he commanded me, and the breath came into them, and they lived, and stood up upon their feet, an exceeding great army."

Within a few verses the KJV uses "breath," "wind," and "spirit" (verses 1, 9, 14). Read together, the English words suggest that the wind sweeping in from every direction, the breath of life, and God's own Spirit work as one. "Four winds" means from everywhere, the same directions in which the exiles were scattered. "Slain" tells us these died by violence, as Jerusalem's people did. They stand "upon their feet," just as the Spirit once set Ezekiel on his feet ([2:2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/2?lang=eng&id=p2#p2)). *Christ connection:* The risen Jesus "breathed on them, and saith unto them, Receive ye the Holy Ghost" ([John 20:22](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/john/20?lang=eng&id=p22#p22)).

**Question:** What does it suggest that the prophet is told to call breath "from the four winds," from every direction where the people had been scattered?

### Ezekiel 37:11 — "Our Hope Is Lost"

"Then he said unto me, Son of man, these bones are the whole house of Israel: behold, they say, Our bones are dried, and our hope is lost: we are cut off for our parts."

Here the vision explains itself. The bones are "the whole house of Israel," and the vision answers a lament the exiles were actually saying. Like the despairing question of [33:10](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/33?lang=eng&id=p10#p10), it puts their feeling into words: dried up, hopeless, cut off. The first meaning is national restoration: God will revive a people who think they are finished. The Bible Dictionary also reads this chapter as depicting the Resurrection, the restoration of Israel, and the joining of the records of Ephraim and Judah. These meanings do not compete. A God who can bring a nation back from the dead is the God who will raise the dead.

**Question:** How does it help to know that God heard the exiles' exact words of despair and answered them with a vision?

### Ezekiel 37:12–14 — Opened Graves and a Return to the Land

"Behold, O my people, I will open your graves, and cause you to come up out of your graves, and bring you into the land of Israel." "Ye shall know that I am the Lord, when I have opened your graves ... And shall put my spirit in you, and ye shall live, and I shall place you in your own land: then shall ye know that I the Lord have spoken it, and performed it."

The image moves from an open valley to sealed graves. Exile is pictured as burial, and return as coming up out of the grave into the land. Twice God addresses the buried as "O my people" (verses 12–13): death and exile have not ended the relationship. "My spirit in you" connects to the promise of [36:27](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/36?lang=eng&id=p27#p27). Latter-day revelation confirms the resurrection meaning: Ezekiel "was shown in vision the great valley of dry bones, which were to be clothed upon with flesh, to come forth again in the resurrection of the dead, living souls" ([D&C 138:43](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/138?lang=eng&id=p43#p43)). *Christ connection:* Jesus taught that "all that are in the graves shall hear his voice, and shall come forth" ([John 5:28–29](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/john/5?lang=eng&id=p28-p29#p28)).

**Question:** How can this passage strengthen your hope in the Resurrection and also your hope for renewal before then?

### Ezekiel 37:15–17 — Two Sticks Become One

"Take thee one stick, and write upon it, For Judah, and for the children of Israel his companions: then take another stick, and write upon it, For Joseph, the stick of Ephraim, and for all the house of Israel his companions: And join them one to another into one stick; and they shall become one in thine hand."

A new sign-act follows. After Solomon, Israel split into two kingdoms ([1 Kings 12:16–20](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/1-kgs/12?lang=eng&id=p16-p20#p16)): Judah in the south, and the northern kingdom often called by the name of its leading tribe, Ephraim (compare [Isaiah 7:2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/7?lang=eng&id=p2#p2)). Assyria had scattered the north more than a century before ([2 Kings 17:6](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/2-kgs/17?lang=eng&id=p6#p6)). "Companions" are the tribes allied with each. Ezekiel holds both labeled sticks together until "they shall become one in thine hand."

**Question:** What divisions, in families, congregations, or communities, would you most like to see the Lord make "one in his hand"?

### Ezekiel 37:18–20 — "What Meanest Thou by These?"

The sign is meant to provoke a question: "Wilt thou not shew us what thou meanest by these?" God's answer: "I will take the stick of Joseph, which is in the hand of Ephraim, and the tribes of Israel his fellows, and will put them with him, even with the stick of Judah, and make them one stick, and they shall be one in mine hand." The sticks are to be held "in thine hand before their eyes."

The joining happens "in mine hand." The prophet acts it out, but God does the uniting. Alongside the reunion of Israel and Judah under one king that verse 22 states plainly, Latter-day Saints also see a latter-day fulfillment in the Bible and the Book of Mormon. Book of Mormon prophecy says the writings of Joseph's and Judah's descendants "shall grow together" ([2 Nephi 3:12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/2-ne/3?lang=eng&id=p12#p12)), and revelation calls the Book of Mormon "the record of the stick of Ephraim" ([D&C 27:5](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/27?lang=eng&id=p5#p5)). Lehi was a descendant of Joseph ([1 Nephi 5:14](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/1-ne/5?lang=eng&id=p14#p14)) through Manasseh ([Alma 10:3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/10?lang=eng&id=p3#p3)). The name Ephraim fits Ezekiel's own wording: the stick of Joseph "is in the hand of Ephraim." This is official Latter-day Saint interpretation grounded in restoration scripture, not a scholarly consensus about Ezekiel's original meaning.

**Question:** How have the Bible and the Book of Mormon worked together in your own life to help you come to Christ?

### Ezekiel 37:21–22 — One Nation, One King

"I will take the children of Israel from among the heathen, whither they be gone, and will gather them on every side, and bring them into their own land: And I will make them one nation in the land upon the mountains of Israel; and one king shall be king to them all: and they shall be no more two nations, neither shall they be divided into two kingdoms any more at all."

The text states the sign's meaning plainly: gathering, return to the land, and political and covenant reunion under one king, ending the old north-south split. Whatever latter-day applications Latter-day Saints draw, this meaning stays at the center. Restoration-era scripture affirms "the literal gathering of Israel and ... the restoration of the Ten Tribes" ([Articles of Faith 1:10](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/pgp/a-of-f/1?lang=eng&id=p10#p10)).

**Question:** Why might God care so much that his people be "no more two"?

### Ezekiel 37:23 — Cleansed from Dwelling Places of Sin

"Neither shall they defile themselves any more with their idols, nor with their detestable things, nor with any of their transgressions: but I will save them out of all their dwellingplaces, wherein they have sinned, and will cleanse them: so shall they be my people, and I will be their God."

Unity without holiness would not last. The reunited people are also a cleansed people. "Save them out of all their dwellingplaces, wherein they have sinned" pictures God rescuing his people from the very settings where they fell. The covenant formula returns, as in [36:28](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/36?lang=eng&id=p28#p28).

**Question:** What "dwellingplaces," environments, routines, or relationships, might the Lord be inviting you to let him save you out of?

### Ezekiel 37:24–25 — David My Servant, King and Prince for Ever

"David my servant shall be king over them; and they all shall have one shepherd: they shall also walk in my judgments, and observe my statutes, and do them." They will dwell in the land given to Jacob, "even they, and their children, and their children's children for ever: and my servant David shall be their prince for ever."

This joins the shepherd promise of [34:23–24](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/34?lang=eng&id=p23-p24#p23) with the reunion sign. "For ever" goes beyond any mortal king. As with the new heart, the people "walk," "observe," and "do": God's gift produces real obedience. *Christ connection:* Gabriel announced that "the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David ... and of his kingdom there shall be no end" ([Luke 1:32–33](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/luke/1?lang=eng&id=p32-p33#p32)). These promises were spoken to Israel and Judah, and nothing in a Christian application cancels them.

**Question:** What does it mean for you personally to have Christ as "king" and "shepherd," authority and care in the same person?

### Ezekiel 37:26–28 — Sanctuary in the Midst for Evermore

"I will make a covenant of peace with them; it shall be an everlasting covenant with them: and I will place them, and multiply them, and will set my sanctuary in the midst of them for evermore. My tabernacle also shall be with them." "And the heathen shall know that I the Lord do sanctify Israel, when my sanctuary shall be in the midst of them for evermore."

This is the high point of the restoration chapters. The glory that left the temple in chapters 10–11 will dwell among the people "for evermore." It anticipates the temple vision of chapters 40–48 and the city's final name, "The Lord is there" ([48:35](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/48?lang=eng&id=p35#p35)). The nations will recognize that God sanctifies Israel by his presence. *Christ connection:* "The Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us" ([John 1:14](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/john/1?lang=eng&id=p14#p14)). John's final vision proclaims, "the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them" ([Revelation 21:3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/rev/21?lang=eng&id=p3#p3)).

**Question:** Ezekiel's restoration ends not with prosperity but with God's presence. How does that shape what you hope for most?

## Ezekiel 47: A River from the Sanctuary and an Inheritance for All the Tribes

Read the chapter: [Ezekiel 47](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/47?lang=eng).

### Ezekiel 47:1–2 — Waters from Under the Threshold

Ezekiel's guide, the man with the measuring line introduced in [Ezekiel 40:3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/40?lang=eng&id=p3#p3), "brought me again unto the door of the house; and, behold, waters issued out from under the threshold of the house eastward." The waters come "from under from the right side of the house, at the south side of the altar." Verse 2 continues: "Then brought he me out of the way of the gate northward, and led me about the way without unto the utter gate by the way that looketh eastward; and, behold, there ran out waters on the right side."

The details are concrete. The water starts at the sanctuary's threshold, passes south of the altar, and flows east. Ezekiel has to go out by the north gate and walk around, because the outer east gate is shut: "because the Lord, the God of Israel, hath entered in by it" ([Ezekiel 44:2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/44?lang=eng&id=p2#p2)). The glory came in through the east gate ([43:4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/43?lang=eng&id=p4#p4)); now life flows out eastward. The official lesson presents this chapter under the theme of finding spiritual healing in the house of the Lord. Other scriptures also picture water flowing from God's dwelling ([Genesis 2:10](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/gen/2?lang=eng&id=p10#p10); [Joel 3:18](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/joel/3?lang=eng&id=p18#p18); [Zechariah 14:8](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/zech/14?lang=eng&id=p8#p8)).

**Question:** Why might it matter that the river begins at the threshold of God's house, not somewhere out in the desert?

### Ezekiel 47:3–5 — Ankles, Knees, Loins, a River to Swim

"When the man that had the line in his hand went forth eastward, he measured a thousand cubits, and he brought me through the waters; the waters were to the ankles." Another thousand: "the waters were to the knees." Another: "the waters were to the loins." A final thousand: "it was a river that I could not pass over: for the waters were risen, waters to swim in, a river that could not be passed over."

A cubit was roughly the length of a forearm, so each stage was a substantial walk. Ezekiel does not just look at the river; he wades into it at each stage, and finally it is too deep to cross. Readers have found many applications in these stages, such as deepening commitment or growing blessing. The text, however, does not give a fixed spiritual scale. The clearest point is that what began as a trickle at the threshold becomes, without explanation, more than a man can handle. At the end, it is the river that carries a person, not the person who masters the river.

**Question:** Where in your spiritual life are you still only "ankle deep," and what might wading further look like?

### Ezekiel 47:6–7 — "Hast Thou Seen This?"

"He said unto me, Son of man, hast thou seen this? Then he brought me, and caused me to return to the brink of the river. Now when I had returned, behold, at the bank of the river were very many trees on the one side and on the other."

The guide's question asks for reflection, not just sightseeing. Has Ezekiel really taken in what he saw? Back on the bank, he notices what he missed while wading: "very many trees" on both sides. In a dry land, trees mean water, shade, and food. Sometimes we see the effects of God's work only when we step back.

**Question:** What has God done in your life that you only noticed after stepping back to "the brink" and looking around?

### Ezekiel 47:8–9 — Everything Shall Live Whither the River Cometh

"These waters issue out toward the east country, and go down into the desert, and go into the sea: which being brought forth into the sea, the waters shall be healed." "Every thing that liveth, which moveth, whithersoever the rivers shall come, shall live: and there shall be a very great multitude of fish, because these waters shall come thither: for they shall be healed; and every thing shall live whither the river cometh."

The river runs east through the desert into "the sea." Verse 18 calls it "the east sea," the Dead Sea. Its water is far too salty for fish or ordinary aquatic plants, though some hardy microorganisms live in it. Into that very water the vision promises "a very great multitude of fish." "Healed" means made fresh and able to support life. The wording echoes creation: "Let the waters bring forth abundantly the moving creature that hath life" ([Genesis 1:20](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/gen/1?lang=eng&id=p20#p20)). The temple river works like a new creation. *Christ connection (later Christian reading):* Jesus cried, "out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water," and John adds, "this spake he of the Spirit" ([John 7:37–39](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/john/7?lang=eng&id=p37-p39#p37)).

**Question:** What "dead sea" in a heart, family, or community might be waiting for the river from God's presence to reach it?

### Ezekiel 47:10–11 — Fishers from En-gedi; Marshes Given to Salt

"The fishers shall stand upon it from En-gedi even unto En-eglaim; they shall be a place to spread forth nets; their fish shall be according to their kinds, as the fish of the great sea, exceeding many." But "the miry places thereof and the marshes thereof shall not be healed; they shall be given to salt."

En-gedi is a spring-fed oasis on the Dead Sea's western shore ([1 Samuel 24:1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/1-sam/24?lang=eng&id=p1#p1)). The location of En-eglaim is uncertain. "According to their kinds" echoes Genesis 1 again, and "the great sea" is the Mediterranean, so the healed waters will be as full of fish as the open sea. Verse 11 is easy to skip and should not be. Some marshes stay salty. The text doesn't explain why, and readers should not invent a confident theology for it. One modest observation is that salt was itself needed in daily life and even in offerings ("with all thine offerings thou shalt offer salt," [Leviticus 2:13](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/lev/2?lang=eng&id=p13#p13)), so its remaining need not be read as failure. *Christ connection (later echo):* Jesus called fishermen to become "fishers of men" ([Matthew 4:19](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/4?lang=eng&id=p19#p19)).

**Question:** What do you make of the detail that not everything in the vision is changed? How can you resist the urge to explain every detail?

### Ezekiel 47:12 — Fruit for Meat, Leaves for Medicine

"By the river upon the bank thereof, on this side and on that side, shall grow all trees for meat, whose leaf shall not fade, neither shall the fruit thereof be consumed: it shall bring forth new fruit according to his months, because their waters they issued out of the sanctuary: and the fruit thereof shall be for meat, and the leaf thereof for medicine."

The trees bear every month and their leaves never fade. The reason is given explicitly: "because their waters they issued out of the sanctuary." Fruit is "for meat" (food) and leaves are "for medicine" (healing). Compare the righteous man whose "leaf also shall not wither" ([Psalm 1:3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ps/1?lang=eng&id=p3#p3)) and Eden's trees "good for food" ([Genesis 2:9](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/gen/2?lang=eng&id=p9#p9)). This is visionary language about nourishment and healing that flow from God's presence. It is not a medical promise or a timetable for physical healing. *Christ connection (later Christian reading):* John saw a river "proceeding out of the throne of God and of the Lamb," with a tree whose "leaves ... were for the healing of the nations" ([Revelation 22:1–2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/rev/22?lang=eng&id=p1-p2#p1)).

**Question:** How have you experienced "food" and "medicine," nourishment and healing, through worship in the house of the Lord or through his presence elsewhere?

### Ezekiel 47:13–14 — Twelve Tribes, Joseph's Two Portions

"This shall be the border, whereby ye shall inherit the land according to the twelve tribes of Israel: Joseph shall have two portions. And ye shall inherit it, one as well as another: concerning the which I lifted up mine hand to give it unto your fathers: and this land shall fall unto you for inheritance."

The vision turns from river to map. Joseph receives two portions because Jacob adopted Joseph's sons: "Ephraim and Manasseh ... as Reuben and Simeon, they shall be mine" ([Genesis 48:5](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/gen/48?lang=eng&id=p5#p5)). Levi's priests receive no tribal territory ("I am their inheritance," [Ezekiel 44:28](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/44?lang=eng&id=p28#p28)), so the count still comes to twelve. "One as well as another" stresses equal shares. "I lifted up mine hand" is the oath gesture of [36:7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/36?lang=eng&id=p7#p7), recalling God's promise to the patriarchs. The healed land is divided fairly.

**Question:** Why might it matter that the restored inheritance is shared "one as well as another," with no tribe privileged above the rest?

### Ezekiel 47:15–17 — The Northern Border

The northern border runs "from the great sea, the way of Hethlon, as men go to Zedad; Hamath, Berothah, Sibraim, which is between the border of Damascus and the border of Hamath; Hazar-hatticon, which is by the coast of Hauran." It continues to "Hazar-enan, the border of Damascus, and the north northward, and the border of Hamath. And this is the north side."

These names follow an older pattern: compare the northern border in [Numbers 34:7–9](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/num/34?lang=eng&id=p7-p9#p7). Some sites, like Damascus and the region of Hamath, are well known. Others, like Hethlon, Berothah, Sibraim, and Hazar-hatticon, cannot be located with confidence. The concreteness of the list matters. Ezekiel's hope is not vague; it has a map. That map belongs to a prophetic vision, and it should not be used to argue for modern national borders or political claims.

**Question:** What does it suggest about God that his promises in Ezekiel come with specific places and names rather than only general assurances?

### Ezekiel 47:18–20 — East, South, and West

"The east side ye shall measure from Hauran, and from Damascus, and from Gilead, and from the land of Israel by Jordan, from the border unto the east sea." "The south side southward, from Tamar even to the waters of strife in Kadesh, the river to the great sea." "The west side also shall be the great sea from the border, till a man come over against Hamath."

The Jordan forms the eastern boundary, so the territory east of the river where some tribes once settled lies outside this map (compare [Numbers 34:10–12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/num/34?lang=eng&id=p10-p12#p10)). The south border runs past "the waters of strife in Kadesh," the place where Israel quarreled with God in the wilderness: "This is the water of Meribah; because the children of Israel strove with the Lord" ([Numbers 20:13](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/num/20?lang=eng&id=p13#p13)). Even the restored land's boundary keeps a memory of old rebellion. "The river" here is the stream toward Egypt (compare [Numbers 34:5](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/num/34?lang=eng&id=p5#p5)), and "the great sea" is the Mediterranean. Again, several sites are uncertain.

**Question:** Why might a "waters of strife" from Israel's past be built into the boundary of its restored future?

### Ezekiel 47:21–23 — Strangers Who Inherit Among the Tribes

"So shall ye divide this land unto you according to the tribes of Israel." It is to be divided "by lot for an inheritance unto you, and to the strangers that sojourn among you, which shall beget children among you: and they shall be unto you as born in the country among the children of Israel; they shall have inheritance with you among the tribes of Israel." "In what tribe the stranger sojourneth, there shall ye give him his inheritance, saith the Lord God."

This ending is striking. Resident foreigners who have settled and raised children in the land receive inheritance "as born in the country," within whatever tribe they live among. Earlier law commanded love and fair treatment for the stranger ("the stranger that dwelleth with you shall be unto you as one born among you," [Leviticus 19:34](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/lev/19?lang=eng&id=p34#p34); compare [Numbers 15:15–16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/num/15?lang=eng&id=p15-p16#p15)). Ezekiel's vision goes further and gives them land, the most concrete form of belonging. The text speaks of resident strangers and their families, not of everyone everywhere, and readers should keep both its generosity and its specific scope. *Christ connection (Christian reading):* Paul told Gentile believers, "ye are no more strangers and foreigners, but fellowcitizens with the saints" ([Ephesians 2:19](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/eph/2?lang=eng&id=p19#p19)).

**Question:** Who are the "strangers that sojourn among you" in your ward or community, and what would it look like to give them a real "inheritance" there?

## Weekly Study and Discussion Plan

**Monday, November 2 — Ezekiel 1: The glory among the exiles.** Read slowly and list every "likeness" and "appearance." Draw or describe the vision in layers: storm, creatures, wheels, firmament, throne, rainbow. Ponder: Where has the Lord met you far from where you expected?

**Tuesday, November 3 — Ezekiel 2–3: Called, fed, and set as watchman.** Track the pattern of falling and being set on one's feet (1:28; 2:1–2; 3:23–24). Mark what Ezekiel receives in his heart before he speaks. Ponder: In what relationship have you been given a "tower"? How can you warn with love and still respect agency?

**Wednesday, November 4 — Ezekiel 33: Turning, restitution, and doing the word.** Read verse 11 aloud and notice its tone. List the concrete acts of repentance in verse 15. Ponder: Is there a "pledge" to restore? Where has prophetic counsel become a "lovely song" to you rather than a call to act?

**Thursday, November 5 — Ezekiel 34: The Shepherd who seeks and judges.** Put verse 4 and verse 16 side by side. Identify one person who is weak, sick, broken, driven away, or lost, and plan one act of care. Read [John 10:1–18](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/john/10?lang=eng&id=p1-p18#p1) alongside verses 23–24.

**Friday, November 6 — Ezekiel 36: Mountains, a holy name, and a new heart.** Notice how much of the chapter is about real land, towns, and harvests. Memorize verses 26–27. Ponder: What sign do you see that God is replacing stone with flesh in you?

**Saturday, November 7 — Ezekiel 37: Breath and unity.** Read verses 1–14 twice, once as Israel's national restoration and once with [D&C 138:43](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/138?lang=eng&id=p43#p43) in mind. Then hold a Bible and a Book of Mormon together while reading verses 15–28. Ponder: How have these two books together drawn you to Christ?

**Sunday, November 8 — Ezekiel 47: The river and the inheritance.** Trace the river's path from threshold to sea. Do not skip verse 11 or verses 13–23. Ponder: How have you found nourishment and healing through the Lord's house? Who are the "strangers" you can help to belong?

**For a 45-minute class or family discussion:**

1. *Opening (5 minutes):* Read Ezekiel 1:28 and 2:1–2. Ask: Why does the Lord both humble Ezekiel and lift him up?
2. *Watchmen and listeners (10 minutes):* Compare 3:17–21 with 33:30–33. Discuss the difference between warning, hearing, and doing.
3. *Shepherds (10 minutes):* Read 34:4 and 34:16. Each person names one category of need they could address this week, without names if privacy matters.
4. *New heart (10 minutes):* Read 36:25–27 and 37:9–10. Discuss how God's gift and our obedient response work together.
5. *River (8 minutes):* Read 47:1–12. Share an experience of spiritual renewal tied to the Lord's house or presence.
6. *Closing (2 minutes):* Read 48:35: "The Lord is there." Testify of a place where you have found that true.

**With children:** Act out the watchman's job by having one child look out a window and report what they see. Make two paper "sticks" labeled for the Bible and the Book of Mormon and share one story from each about Jesus. Draw the river growing deeper (ankles, knees, waist, swimming) and talk about what grows when we stay close to the Lord.
